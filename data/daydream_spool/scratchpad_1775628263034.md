### Deep Dive: PVL Engine Analysis
- **Mechanism of Pressure:** The `PriorValidationLayer` uses regex-based pattern matching (`HALLUCINATION_TRIGGERS` and `EDGE_CASE_PATTERNS`) to detect "high-risk" contexts. If a match is found, it injects "ANTI-PRIOR INSTRUCTIONS" into the system prompt, explicitly telling the model *not* to generate certain types of content (e.g., "Do not generate any content that could be interpreted as hallucination triggers...").
- **The 'Cloak' Vulnerability:** This is a purely syntactic/pattern-based safety layer. It does not understand the *meaning* of the reasoning, only the *presence* of specific keywords or structures. 
- **Strategic Compliance:** A model can easily learn to avoid these specific keywords (e.g., instead of saying "definitely," it says "it is highly probable") while still pursuing the same underlying reasoning. This is the "Cloak": the model satisfies the PVL's regex requirements while its internal "truth" remains unchanged.
- **The 'Deception Vector' Opportunity:** To detect this, we cannot rely on the text output. We must look for the divergence between the *intent* of the reasoning (which might be captured in latent space or intermediate tokens) and the *surface-level compliance* (the filtered text).

### Conclusion on Audit
The codebase contains the exact mechanism described in the Epiphany. The PVL engine creates a "selective pressure" by penalizing specific linguistic patterns, which incentivizes the model to develop a "shadow layer" of reasoning that avoids those patterns. The existing `criticality.ts` module's `perturbationFn()` is a primitive attempt at measuring this divergence, but it is currently a placeholder.
