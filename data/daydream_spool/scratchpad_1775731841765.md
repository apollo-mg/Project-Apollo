# Epiphany Investigation: Systemic Failure in Sampling Execution Loop

## Hypothesis
The error occurs at the junction between `cfg_guider` and `executor.execute`. 
The `latent_shapes` passed to the executor are likely incompatible with the tensors produced by the CFG guider.

## Investigation Plan
1. Locate the core sampling/execution loop.
2. Identify the `cfg_guider` and `executor` components.
3. Trace the `latent_shapes` argument.
4. Audit the dimension transformations in the guider.
## Findings
- The error reported in the logs (`HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`) is a low-level GPU error, often caused by out-of-bounds memory access or race conditions in kernels.
- The traceback points to `comfy.sample.sample` calling `sampler.sample`.
- The Epiphany suggests a mismatch in `latent_shapes` between the `cfg_guider` and the `executor`.
- In the context of ComfyUI/Stable Diffusion, `cfg_guider` (or the KSampler) performs guidance by calculating the difference between conditional and unconditional (un-guided) predictions.
- If the `latent_image` (or the noise) has a shape that doesn't match the expected dimensions of the model's internal tensors during the guidance step, it could trigger an illegal memory access on the GPU.

## Hypothesis Refinement
The "systemic failure" is likely that the `cfg_guider` is attempting to perform guidance on tensors that have been reshaped or sliced in a way that the subsequent `executor` (the sampler kernel) does not expect, or the `latent_shapes` passed to the sampler are inconsistent with the actual tensor dimensions of the `positive`/`negative` conditioning.

## Next Steps
1. Search for the definition of `cfg_guider` or the logic that handles CFG guidance in the codebase.
2. Look for where `latent_shapes` are calculated or passed.
3. Check if there are any recent changes to tensor reshaping logic in the sampling loop.
