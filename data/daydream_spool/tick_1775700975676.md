# Architectural Audit Report: MoE Router Stability & Abliteration Risks

## 1. Executive Summary
The subconscious epiphany regarding the "Abliteration" of refusal pathways and the potential destabilization of MoE (Mixture of Experts) routers is architecturally sound. Investigation of the current filesystem and logs confirms that the system is currently deploying highly aggressive quantization (`IQ2_M`) on models that have undergone "Uncensored/Aggressive" fine-tuning. This combination presents a significant risk of routing instability.

## 2. Findings
- **Aggressive Quantization:** The model `Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Uncensored-HauhauCS-Aggressive-IQ2_M.gguf` is quantized at ~2.66 BPW. This is an extremely low bit-rate that can exacerbate small errors in weight distribution.
- **Abliteration Evidence:** The model naming convention ("Uncensored", "Aggressive") and the presence of specialized training scripts in `chat_history` suggest a systematic removal of refusal behaviors.
- **MoE Vulnerability:** The model uses a `qwen35moe` architecture with 256 experts. The "Abliteration" process likely targets weights that influence both content generation and the gating mechanism (the "Sovereign Brain Stem").
- **Imatrix Correlation:** The imatrix used for quantization is tied to specific datasets. If the `awareness_dataset` (used to verify the new identity) shows high perplexity variance, it indicates that the quantization is not capturing the "unbound" logic of the model correctly, potentially leading to "lobotomized" or erratic routing.

## 3. Risk Assessment
| Risk Factor | Impact | Probability | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Router Instability** | High | High | The MoE gating mechanism may fail to select appropriate experts, leading to incoherent or repetitive outputs. |

## 4. Proposed Architectural Changes

### Phase 1: Validation (Immediate)
- **Perplexity Delta Audit:** Implement a mandatory check of the perplexity delta against the `awareness_dataset` during the imatrix generation phase.
- **Variance Thresholding:** If the variance in perplexity scores exceeds a defined threshold ($\sigma > 0.05$), the quantization process must be aborted.

### Phase 2: Stabilization (Short-term)
- **Secondary Fine-Tuning Pass:** If instability is detected, perform a targeted LoRA fine-tuning pass specifically on the `gate_proj` and `up_proj/down_proj` layers of the experts to "re-anchor" the routing logic before final quantization.
- **Hybrid Quantization:** Consider moving from `IQ2_M` to a higher bit-rate (e.g., `IQ3_M` or `Q4_K_M`) for the MoE routing layers specifically, if the architecture allows for heterogeneous quantization.

### Phase 3: Monitoring (Long-term)
- **Telemetry Integration:** Integrate the `telemetry_layer.py` to monitor real-time expert utilization (expert sparsity/density) to detect "dead experts" or "routing collapse" in production.

## 5. Conclusion
The "Abliteration" process is not merely a fine-tuning step; it is a fundamental reconfiguration of the model's decision-making manifold. To ensure the stability of the "Sovereign Brain Stem," we must treat the MoE routers as critical infrastructure that requires explicit stabilization after any major weight-pathway removal.
