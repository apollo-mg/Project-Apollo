### Synthesis of Epiphany
The epiphany's mention of "FCLK and L3 residency" likely refers to the tuning required to bridge the gap between the high-speed GPU (1112 t/s prefill) and the token generation speed. If the micro-kernel (the logic that manages the model's execution) is not resident in L3, the latency of moving data between the CPU and GPU (or even within the CPU/GPU memory hierarchy) will throttle the "Awareness" dataset's high-density logic.

The "Break-In" phase mentioned in the epiphany is likely a missing or undocumented stage in the current workflow where the system is tuned specifically for L3 cache residency of the model's critical execution paths.

### Conclusion & Proposal
The current codebase lacks an explicit "Break-In" phase or a dedicated L3-residency optimization script. The distillation process (creating the Qwen 3.5 models found in `/models/`) is happening, but the hardware-software coupling (the "singular attempt") is not yet formalized in the orchestration layer.

**Proposed Architectural Update:**
1. **Formalize 'Break-In' Phase:** Introduce a new stage in `master_action_plan.md` or a new script `optimize_l3_residency.sh` that uses `tunableop` or similar tools to pin critical micro-kernel paths to L3.
2. **Integrate Hardware/Distillation Coupling:** Update the model provisioning logic to ensure that when a distilled model (like the Qwen 3.5 series) is deployed, the system automatically triggers a hardware latency audit to ensure L3 residency is sufficient for the model's logic density.
3. **Telemetry:** Add L3 cache miss rates to `system_monitor.py` to provide real-time feedback on whether the "latency of thought" is being minimized.
