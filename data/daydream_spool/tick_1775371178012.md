# Architectural Audit: Precision Sandwich & Triton/LLVM Legalization

## Epiphany Summary
The current friction in the lab is a "precision sandwich": a misalignment between high-level quantization logic (Python/ComfyUI layer) and low-level hardware execution (Triton/LLVM compiler). While the software attempts granular weight casting (e.g., GGUF/FP8), the underlying compiler fails to recognize the native capabilities of the RX 9070 XT, effectively downcasting precision back to F16.

## Audit Findings
- **Symptom Identification:** The `AttributeError` in `ComfyUI-GGUF` (specifically in `ops.py` at `forward_comfy_cast_weights`) is not a simple Python bug but a symptom of attempting manual casting that the hardware/compiler layer is not yet prepared to support.
- **Hardware/Compiler Mismatch:** The system is running on ROCm 7.2 with an AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT (gfx1201). The logs indicate that while `torch.compile` is allowed, the actual precision gains are being negated by the compiler's inability to maintain the requested quantization levels.
- **Log Evidence:** 
    - `comfy_startup.log` shows `gguf qtypes: F16 (472), Q4_0 (304)`.
    - The `AttributeError` in `ComfyUI-GGUF/ops.py` occurs during weight casting operations.
    - The compiler/Triton layer is failing to "legalize" the specific precision requirements of the GGUF architecture.

## Proposed Architectural Pivot
**Priority 1: Triton/LLVM Legalization**
Instead of refining the `ComfyUI-GGUF` casting logic, we must prioritize fixing the Triton/LLVM compiler's ability to recognize and preserve the native hardware capabilities of the RX 9070 XT. 

**Proposed Action Plan:**
1. **Stop** manual casting refinements in the Python layer.
2. **Implement** a "Legalization" pass in the Triton/LLVM compiler to ensure that the requested precision (FP8/GGUF) is not downcast to F16 during the compilation of kernels.
3. **Validate** that the hardware-specific instructions for the RX 9070 XT are correctly mapped in the compiler backend before re-enabling high-level quantization logic.

## Conclusion
The `AttributeError` is a signal that we are trying to build sophisticated software on a foundation that is fundamentally reverting our precision gains. We must fix the compiler backend before we can fix the quantization logic.
