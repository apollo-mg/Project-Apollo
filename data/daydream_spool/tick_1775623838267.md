# Architectural Audit Report: Environment Fragmentation & Source of Truth

## 1. Executive Summary
The subconscious epiphany regarding "digital archaeology" is validated. The Apollo workspace is currently a collection of disconnected, fragmented environments rather than a unified architectural entity. The presence of multiple, overlapping dependency definitions (`pyproject.toml`, `requirements.txt`, `setup.py`) across various sub-directories and temporary experiment folders (`tmp/`) has created a state of configuration drift.

## 2. Findings
- **Fragmentation:** Dependency definitions are scattered across the filesystem. Sub-projects like `llama_cpp_turboquant`, `llama_cpp_latest`, and `prism_llama_cpp` each maintain their own isolated requirements, leading to the proliferation of redundant virtual environments (`gemini_venv`, `venv`, `venv_cachyos`).
- **Lack of Authority:** There is no single, immutable environment specification that serves as the "Source of Truth" for the entire Apollo ecosystem. The root-level `requirements.txt` is insufficient to govern the complex, multi-module nature of the project.
- **Archaeological Debt:** The `tmp/` directory contains several "frozen" environments that represent past experiments rather than active, integrated components.

## 3. Proposed Architectural Update
To prevent the workspace from becoming a "graveyard of failed configurations," I propose the following transition:

### A. Implementation of a Centralized Environment Specification
- **Primary Authority:** Establish a single, top-level `pyproject.toml` using `poetry` or `uv` as the primary orchestrator for the entire project.
- **Workspace Concept:** Utilize a "Workspace" pattern (similar to Cargo or PNPM) where sub-modules are defined as members of the root project. This allows for shared dependency resolution while maintaining modularity.

### B. Standardization of Dependency Management
- **Deprecate Fragmented Files:** Phase out the use of local `requirements.txt` and `setup.py` in sub-directories in favor of the central `pyproject.toml` workspace definition.
- **Immutable Environment Definition:** All environment builds must derive from the central specification to ensure reproducibility and prevent "environment drift."

### C. Cleanup Protocol
- **Audit & Consolidate:** Perform a one-time audit to merge dependencies from `tmp/` and `llama.cpp/` into the central specification.
- **Purge:** Once the central specification is validated, all existing `venv/` and `gemini_venv/` directories should be purged to ensure a clean state.

## 4. Conclusion
The current architecture is reactive and additive. To achieve stability, the architecture must become declarative and centralized. We must stop excavating the past and start defining the future through a single, authoritative source of truth.
