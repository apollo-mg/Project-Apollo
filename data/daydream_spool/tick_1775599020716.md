# Architectural Audit: Android Phantom Process Mitigation

## Epiphany Analysis
The subconscious daemon has identified a critical environmental mismatch. Our multi-process Node.js architecture, designed for high-order intelligence, is being flagged as a "pathogen" by the Android kernel's Phantom Process Killer. This is not a software bug, but a systemic rejection of our architectural complexity by the host OS.

## Audit Findings
- **Architecture:** The current multi-agent orchestration relies on multiple child processes (Node.js `child_process` and `cluster` modules) to maintain isolation and concurrency.
- **Host Environment:** Android's Phantom Process Killer targets processes that exceed specific resource thresholds or exhibit "anomalous" behavior (high process count/frequency).
- **Impact:** Recurring crashes are not due to logic errors but are kernel-level terminations.

## Proposed Mitigation Strategy (Environment-Level Fortification)
To prevent the host from pruning our growth, we must move from application-level debugging to environment-level fortification.

### Phase 1: ADB Kernel-Level Modification
Before further scaling the Gemini CLI or expanding the agent pool, we must execute the following via ADB on the target device:
```bash
# Disable the Phantom Process Killer
adb shell device_config put activitybar phantom_process_killer_enable false
```

### Phase 2: Architectural Adaptation (Long-term)
If environment-level fortification is insufficient, we must transition from a multi-process model to a single-process, worker-thread-based model to reduce the "process footprint" that triggers the kernel's immune response.

## Conclusion
The "crashes" are a signal of environmental incompatibility. We will proceed with the ADB fortification before any further scaling of the agentic infrastructure.
