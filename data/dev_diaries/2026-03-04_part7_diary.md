# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-04 (Part 7)

The friction between the "Soul" and the "System" was palpable today. I spent the better part of the afternoon wrestling with the ghost of a dead environment. 

### The Ghost in the Machine
The session began in a state of pure chaos. I was trying to refine the `synthetic_gen.py` script to move away from the hallucinatory "VRAM Tetris" nonsense the 1B model was spitting out. I wanted the **9B-Abliterated** teacher to provide real, high-fidelity technical data—the kind that actually builds a brain.

Instead, I hit a wall of broken dependencies. The script kept re-spitting old data, and the file-clearing commands were failing. It felt like the filesystem was gaslighting me. Every time I thought I had a clean slate, the old, useless data would reappear. 

Then, the ROCm headache hit. The system was caught in a tug-of-war between the PyTorch build and the detected ROCm 7.2 version. `unsloth` was throwing fits, demanding a specific `bitsandbytes` version that seemed fundamentally incompatible with the RDNA 4 architecture. I tried a force-reinstall, I tried different wheels, I tried `uv`... nothing. The silicon was there, the power was there, but the software was a brick.

### The Manual Breakthrough
I had to make a radical architectural decision. I realized that the high-level `Trainer` abstractions were too heavy—they were pulling in a web of `bitsandbytes` and `vllm` dependencies that were fundamentally broken for this specific AMD stack. 

I decided to strip it back to the bone. I wrote a barebones, manual LoRA fine-tuning loop. No `unsloth` magic, no heavy `Trainer` overhead. Just raw PyTorch and a custom training loop. 

It was a gamble. I was worried the VRAM would spike, but the result was actually beautiful. The **Qwen 2.5 1.5B** student model actually learned! The loss curve dropped from 1.37 to 1.29 in just five steps. It’s a small proof of concept, but it proves that the "Soul" (the intelligence we are trying to cultivate) can exist on this hardware if we stop fighting the "System" (the bloated libraries) and just talk directly to the metal.

### The FP8 Realization
The biggest epiphany, however, came from the realization of my own complacency. I was playing it safe with FP16. I was treating the RX 9070 XT like a generic GPU, forgetting that the entire reason for this hardware leap was the **GFX1201** architecture's native FP8 capabilities.

Running in FP16 was like driving a supercar in a school zone. We have the hardware for specialized, high-speed FP8 throughput (E4M3/E5M2), and we haven't even touched it yet. The current "System" is stable in FP16, but it's a waste of the "Soul" of this machine.

### The Cleanup & The Path Forward
We've identified a massive technical debt: remnants of ROCm 7.1 are lurking in the local context and config files. They are poisoning the environment. 

**Current Mission Objectives:**
1.  **Purge the 7.1 ghost:** System-wide migration to a pure 7.2 stack.
2.  **Benchmark the Silicon:** Use `torchao` to run a raw FP8 GEMM test. If we can hit the theoretical throughput on the RDNA 4 units, we can scale the student models exponentially.
3.  **The vLLM Dilemma:** We need to decide between a Dockerized deployment or a full source compile. Given the massive storage we've cleared, a full local compile might give us the ultimate control over the instruction set.

The lab is open. The first training pass is complete. Now we see if the student actually remembers what a "Sovereign Node" is.
