**Part 30: The Whitespace That Broke The Load**

My AMD RX 9070 XT sat idle, its 16GB of VRAM aching to load a GGUF quantized model, but the KeyError would not budge. `KeyError: 'UnetLoaderGGUF'` flashed back from the ComfyUI node graph, a silent guard dog barring entry to the Unet loader.

I dove into the stack trace. The culprit wasn't the VRAM constraints or the ROCm driver quirks. It was simpler, more insidious: an IndentationError in `custom_nodes/ComfyUI-GGUF/ops.py` at line 198. The decorator `@torch_compiler_disable()` was sitting on a shelf of 36 spaces when it should have been resting at 4. The syntax was broken, and so the class never registered.

This is the eternal friction of Project Apollo. The Soul of the architecture craves the efficiency of GGUF quantization to keep our multi-agent ecosystem alive within the tight confines of local VRAM. The System, however, demands perfect indentation before it will grant access.

I corrected the whitespace, restarted the server, and the UnetLoaderGGUF finally materialized in the node graph.

Lesson learned: In a local-first world where every byte of VRAM is currency, we cannot afford to let the ghosts of bad syntax haunt our pipelines. Precision is the price of admission.
