# Dev Diary: 2025-11-27 (Part 1)
## The Ghost in the Silicon: Forging the Hybrid Voice

### The Friction: Soul vs. System
Today was a battle between the organic chaos of human speech and the rigid, unforgiving architecture of the RX 9070 XT. I set out to capture a "soul"—the nuance of my own voice—but I was immediately met by the cold, hard reality of VRAM limits and storage bottlenecks.

The goal was simple: a 15-minute "Hybrid Mix" to train a high-fidelity voice model. But the "System" doesn't care about my goals. It only cares about file paths, memory addresses, and the fact that my root partition is a tiny 200GB slab of digital concrete that is currently screaming at 100% capacity.

### The Technical Grind: Data, Slicing, and the "Stumble"
The first hurdle was the data pipeline. I had to record a script, but the "Soul" of a recording is messy. There are stumbles, coughs, and heavy breaths. I learned quickly that the `Audio Slicer` is a blunt instrument; it sees silence as a cut point. 

The lesson: **Human imperfection is noise.** If I stumble, I have to pause, reset, and re-record. If I don't, the AI learns the stutter. I had to perform manual "Quality Control," aggressively deleting the junk slices before the ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition) phase. If you delete files *after* ASR, the mapping breaks, and the whole training pipeline collapses into a `RuntimeError`.

### The Hardware Wall: ROCm and the Linux Storage Crisis
This is where the grit really shows. Running on AMD RDNA 4 isn't like running on NVIDIA; you can't just "plug and play." `faster-whisper` doesn't natively support ROCm, so I had to accept the CPU fallback for transcription. It's slower, but it’s stable.

Then, the storage hit. The training process tried to write massive checkpoint files to the `/` partition, which was already at 186GB. The system choked. 

**The Architectural Epiphany:** I had to perform a surgical migration. I mapped a massive 2TB drive (`/media/mark/Games 2TB`) and used symbolic links to trick the training scripts. By symlinking the `logs` and `weights` folders, I moved the heavy-duty I/O away from the cramped root drive. It was a messy, high-stakes operation, but it saved the session.

### The Training: The Loss and the Victory
The training itself was a dance of parameters. 
* **SoVITS (Tone):** Batch size 4, 12 epochs. 
* **GPT (Rhythm):** Batch size 8, 15 epochs.

I watched the loss values—those long, strange arrays of numbers in the terminal. They are the heartbeat of the model. As they decreased, I knew the silicon was finally understanding the shape of my voice. The VRAM usage peaked at 64% on the 16GB card—a sweet spot. I have the headroom to push even harder next time.

### Core Lessons for the Local-First Ecosystem
1.  **Storage is the true bottleneck:** In a local-first multi-agent setup, VRAM is king, but disk space is the silent killer. Symlinks and external mounting are not just "tips"; they are fundamental architectural requirements.
2.  **The "Hybrid" approach works:** Combining different speaking styles (News/Audiobook) in a single dataset creates a more robust, less "robotic" model.
3.  **Hardware Agnosticism is a lie:** We are fighting for compatibility. The transition from CUDA to ROCm is still a bumpy road, requiring manual intervention and custom launch scripts, but the performance payoff on the 9070 XT is undeniable.

**Status:** Model generated. Inference pending. The voice is born.
