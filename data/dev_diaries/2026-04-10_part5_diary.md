**The 4GB Ceiling and the Resource-Defined Tool**

It is 03:00 hours, and the Raspberry Pi 5 is humming on the desk. We were crunching numbers again, fighting the classic VRAM wars of edge inference. The OS takes its cut, leaving a hard 3.6GB ceiling for the model plus the KV cache. The math was ruthless but clear: Bonsai-8B (1-Bit) could swallow the entire 65,536 token context window with 2.44GB to spare for the cache, but the Gemma 4 E2B on the Galaxy S21 could only handle 21,000 tokens at 8-bit precision.

That was the friction between the Soul and the System. The Soul wanted the deep, exhaustive reading capability of a 100-page book, but the System was a cold, hard 4GB of RAM on a Pi 5.

Then came the epiphany. Why are we hard-coding tools to specific hardware? Instead of saying "The Pi does scraping," we should be defining **Resource-Defined Tools**. A `web_fetch_deep_read` requires a minimum context window of 60,000 tokens and internet access. A `format_json_table` requires fast TPS and 4-bit precision but can live in a tiny 8k window.

If we define the requirements in JSON, the router becomes a simple lookup. It is not magic; it is Kubernetes scheduling for local inference.

This changes everything for the Sovereign Engine. We are not building a toy. We are building a fleet. An uncensored Architect on the RX 9070 XT, a Librarian on the Pi 5, and a Pocket Assistant on the S21. No corporate API rate limits, no monthly subscription fees gating the smartest model. Just hardware we own, weights we trust, and logic that answers only to us.

Now, the only question left is whether the Gemma 4 vision encoder will play nice with the ROCm driver on the RX 9070 XT, or if we will have to clear VRAM and restart the server. We will find out when we run the benchmark.
