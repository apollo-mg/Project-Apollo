# Dev Diary: 2026-02-28 (Part 5)

## The Architect's Arrival and the Memory Wall

Today was a day of high-stakes model swapping, and a stark reminder that even with an RDNA 4 powerhouse, the "Soul" of a model is still ultimately prisoner to the "System" of silicon.

The goal was to integrate the new **Qwen3-Coder** models. Specifically, I wanted to deploy the **Qwen3-Coder-Next (80B MoE)**. I had this vision of an "Architect" that could handle high-level structural planning, leaving the fast, nimble DeepSeek-R1 to handle the heavy lifting of daily coding tasks.

The reality? The 80B model is a monster. Even at 4-bit quantization, it demanded over 46GB of memory. My system has 16GB of VRAM and ~32GB of RAM. The math simply didn't work. The model tried to offload 30GB to system RAM, hit the 36.4GB limit, and the whole stack collapsed. A hard, uncerivable crash.

## The SGLang Rabbit Hole

I almost led us into a trap. I got caught up in the hype of "Day-0 ROCm" support. I tried to push for **SGLang** or **vLLM** to unlock the full potential of the Triton-optimized kernels (the "magic" that makes the Gated DeltaNet and Flash Attention work on AMD). 

But I quickly realized that the current landscape of ROCm is a mess of version mismatches and dependency hell. I nearly wasted hours trying to compile PyTorch or wrestling with Docker containers that weren't built for the specificities of ROCm 7.1.1. I realized that for a sovereign, local-first system, we can't afford to be constantly fighting the infrastructure. 

We have to work with what is stable. **Ollama** via `llama.cpp` might not use the absolute bleeding-edge Triton kernels, but it is stable, it's fast, and it works with my 9070 XT.

## The Verdict: The Architect's Intelligence

To see if the trouble was worth it, I ran a "DesignMind Shootout." I captured a photo of a small mechanical part and tasked both **DeepSeek-R1 (14B)** and the **Qwen3-Coder (30B)** to write the corresponding FeatureScript for OnShape.

The results were illuminating:
* **DeepSeek-R1** (The Engineer) was lightning fast (53 tokens/sec), but it's a generalist. It hallucinated syntax, failing to understand the specific requirements of the OnShape CAD DSL.
* **Qwen3-Coder (30B)** (The Architect) was significantly slower (~32 tokens/sec) because it's too big to fit in my VRAM, but its "intelligence" is a different beast. It understood the complex, nested context of the geometry engine. It produced code that actually *worked*.

## Current Configuration & Lessons Learned

We have found our "Sovereign Sweet Spot."

* **Engineer:** `deepseek-r1:14b` (100% VRAM resident, lightning fast, for quick-fire logic).
* **Architect:** `qwen3-coder:30b` (Hybrid VRAM/RAM, slower, but high-order reasoning for CAD/Structural tasks).

**Lesson Learned:** In local-first development, memory is the ultimate constraint. A model's "intelligence" is useless if it can't fit within the hardware's ceiling. We are now managing a delicate balance between the specialized reasoning of the 30B model and the raw speed of the 14B.

The system is now stable, the dashboard (the "Glass Cockpit") is tracking both models, and the SSD is nearly full. We've traded raw capacity for raw intelligence. Next up: We put the Architect to work on a real complex geometry test.
