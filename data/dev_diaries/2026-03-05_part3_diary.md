# Dev Diary: 2026-03-05 (Part 3)

## The Silicon Struggle: Breaking the GGUF Bottleneck

Today was a battlefield of architectural friction. We are deep in the transition from "standard" local LLMs to a true Sovereign Engine, but the silicon is fighting us every step of the way. 

The goal was clear: Implement a high-priority forensic toolset—a "Liar-Trap"—to stop the Gemini CLI from choking our VRAM with massive log files. I’ve successfully formalized the **Hybrid Sovereign Architecture**. By implementing `log_forensic_analysis` and `verify_hallucination` (powered by DeepSeek-R1), we’ve offloaded the heavy lifting to the local RX 9070 XT. The "Soul" (the reasoning) is now separated from the "System" (the raw log data).

### The RDNA 4 Reality Check
The testing phase was eye-opening. We pitted the `qwen3.5-9b-heretic` against the `4b-aggressive` variant. The results were a wake-up call. While the 9B model is stable, the 4B model—despite its smaller footprint—was a total "Backend Crater." 

The 4B GGUF was hitting a massive performance wall, crawling at a pathetic 4-5 TPS compared to the 9B's 20 TPS. It’s clear the GGUF quantization isn't playing nice with the new hybrid Linear Attention architecture on the RDNA 4 (GFX1201) backend. This isn't just a software quirk; it’s a fundamental mismatch between the quantization-heavy GGUF format and the high-speed throughput the 9070 XT expects.

### The "Final Boss" of Compilation
The real headache, however, was the PyTorch/ROCm 7.2 integration. My other agent has been trapped in a 24-hour compilation loop, trying to build PyTorch from source to unlock proper FP8 support and the full glory of the RDNA 4 architecture. 

The logs from that build were a nightmare of "Tribal Knowledge" fixes. We encountered:
- **Triton `stdbool.h` errors**: The Triton submodule was failing because it couldn't recognize `bool` types in the HIP backend.
- **Pybind11 Literal Operator errors**: Strict C++ enforcement was killing the build over minor whitespace issues in the literal operators.
- **CMake Minimum Version Mismatch**: ROCm 7.2 demands modern CMake, requiring us to manually patch the versioning across the entire source tree.

I’ve had to perform "surgical" patches on the source code—injecting headers, fixing whitespace, and clearing out the Ninja "dirty manifest" loops. It’s messy, it’s manual, and it’s exactly what it takes to build real-world, local-first AI.

### Current Status: The Engine is Primed
The build is now in its final, high-stakes phase. I've scripted the final `setup.py` with the exact `CXXFLAGS` and `HSA_OVERRIDE` required to force the compiler to respect the RDNA 4 path. 

Once this wheel finishes—and it will, provided the `MAX_JOBS=8` doesn't choke the 32GB RAM—we will finally have the ability to run the **Qwen 3.5 4B Aggressive** via vLLM using FP8 Safetensors. This will turn the "crawler" into a "sprinter," giving us a local, high-speed forensic engine that doesn't rely on the slow GGUF-to-Vulkan fallback.
