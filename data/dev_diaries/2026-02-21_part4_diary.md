# Dev Diary: The RDNA 4 Wall

**Date:** 2026-02-21
**Project:** Apollo
**Status:** Architecture Validated / Hardware/Driver Deadlock

Today was a brutal lesson in the difference between theoretical architectural beauty and the messy reality of early-adoption silicon. We've successfully validated the "System 2" logic—the Resident Receptionist (Llama 3.2 1B) can indeed sit in VRAM and triage queries instantly—but the "System 1" (the heavy-lifting Engineer) is currently trapped behind a wall of driver instability.

### The V1 Engine Crash
The first half of the day was a battle against the `rocm/vllm-dev:nightly` image. I tried everything to force a stable V0 engine path to bypass the GFX1201 mapping bug, but the engine is stubborn. It’s hardcoded to V1, and V1 is fundamentally incompatible with the RDNA 4 kernels on the RX 9070 XT. Every time it tries to compile a custom kernel, the whole thing throws an `HSA_STATUS_ERROR_EXCEPTION: 0x1016`. It's not just a software bug; it's a deep-seated driver/hardware handshake failure.

### The Resident Receptionist & The VRAM Ghost
I pivoted to an Ollama-based approach to see if we could at least get some performance data on the new Resident architecture. The logic holds: the 1B model is "hot," and the triage latency is nearly zero. 

However, the 14B DeepSeek-R1 model is a different story. Even with a reduced context window, the load times were staggering—120 seconds, then 100, then 105. It's not a simple OOM (Out of Memory) issue, as `rocm-smi` shows VRAM usage staying flat around 1.7GB. The system is stuck in a "ghost state." Ollama thinks it needs to offload the model to the CPU because of a VRAM reservation conflict, or it's failing to detect the GPU entirely due to a `rocminfo` timeout during discovery. 

It's a classic "System vs. Soul" conflict. The architecture's soul—the multi-agent, tiered reasoning—is ready, but the silicon is refusing to play ball.

### The Diagnosis
The root cause is a failure in GPU discovery. Ollama's `llama.cpp` runner is seeing the RDNA 4 card and effectively deciding it doesn't know how to talk to it, even when I attempt to spoof it as an RDNA 3 (GFX1100) device. The binary is fundamentally broken for this hardware generation. 

### Moving Forward
We have a choice:
1.  **The Hard Path:** Build vLLM from source. If we can compile the kernels specifically for the 9070 XT, we might bypass the nightly image's failures.
2.  **The Pragmatic Path:** Wait for the ROCm/Ollama ecosystem to mature.

The "Resident Receptionist" is a success in principle, but it's currently running on the CPU, making it a lightweight shell of what it should be. We have the intelligence; we just don't have the speed.

**Next Session:** I'm going to attempt a source build of vLLM. If I can't get the hardware to cooperate with the existing binaries, I have to control the compilation myself. 

**Current State:** 
- **Architecture:** Solid.
- **Logic:** Proven.
- **Hardware Interface:** Broken.
