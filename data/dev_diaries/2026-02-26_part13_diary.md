It’s a reminder that even with the RX 9070 XT pushing the local weights, the logic can still hit a wall of its own making.

Then, the pivot to the physical. 

I was feeding the model images of these test chips—physical manifestations of the digital parameters I've been tweaking. I noticed a quirk in how the model interpreted the multipliers, and we had this brief, almost human moment where the AI's internal "thought" process was caught trying to reconcile the visual geometry of the print with the numerical values. 

It's the classic struggle of the Soul vs. the System. I'm trying to build an intelligence that understands the physical world, but I'm constrained by the way the model interprets pixel-level discrepancies. 

The breakthrough came through the visual analysis of the extrusion chips. This is where the technical reality hit hard. The model identified a critical failure in the hardware-software interface: over-extrusion. 

The positive multipliers (0, 01, 02) were creating a massive buildup of plastic. The sheer volume was forcing the material upward, creating ridges on the Z-top. It wasn't just a setting issue; it was a pressure issue. The melt zone was essentially flooded. The Pressure Advance (PA) algorithm, no matter how well-tuned, simply couldn't have the headroom to manage the excess volume during high-speed turns and seams. It was a volumetric bottleneck.

The "smoking gun" was the negative chips. At 0.98 and 0.97, the lines met perfectly. No ridges, no overlapping. The system was finally balanced. 

This is the lesson for the local-first ecosystem: intelligence isn't just about the ability to process text; it’s about the ability to understand the mechanical limits of the medium it lives in. The model isn't just a calculator; it's an observer of physical reality.

Current directive: Recalibrate the slicer to 0.97 multiplier, keep PA at 0.025, and re-run the test cube. If the corners sharpen up, we've proven that the model can successfully bridge the gap between high-level visual perception and low-level mechanical tuning.

The architecture is holding. The hardware is fast. Now, we just need to keep the logic from eating itself.
