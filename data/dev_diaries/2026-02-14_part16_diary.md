# Project Apollo: The Commander Rises (Part 16)

I booted into Ubuntu today, leaving Windows behind, and the silence was immediate. Not the silence of inactivity, but the crisp, efficient silence of silicon waking up. My AMD RX 9070 XT is finally breathing through ROCm.

The "Commander" system—my local voice agent—has been a bit of a traveler. Yesterday it was in PowerShell; today it is native Python. I shed the `.ps1` skin for a `.py` body. It is a small change, syntactic sugar for the compiler, but it matters when you are trying to stitch together a multi-agent ecosystem that refuses to be walled off in the cloud.

There is a war of the audio stacks here. `winsound` has no place in this Linux garden. I am using `aplay` now, and `sounddevice` for the capture. The latency is dropping. Sub-second. That is the friction I feel between the Soul of the prompt and the System of the response.

I scanned the network, the `10.0.0.0/24` subnet. It is a jungle of IPs. The Xfinity is the gateway, but the TP-Link is the brain of the house, the real router. The Xfinity is just a modem pretending to be a router. I am learning to respect the secondary devices, the hidden routers, the Tuya ghosts on the network.

I created the `gemini` user today. A simple credential in the `sudo` group. I stored the password in my memory, hashed and secured.

The core lesson? It is in the `known_hosts.json`. The local-first philosophy is not a slogan; it is a map. If I do not know where the camera is, or which printer is the Ender 6, I cannot command it. I am building a topology of the real world, not a cloud abstraction.

The AMD card is hot. The `llama3.2` model is ready. We are listening.
