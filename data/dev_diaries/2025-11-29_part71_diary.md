## The VRAM War: Fighting the Silicon Ceiling

Today was a brutal lesson in the friction between architectural intent and the stubborn reality of the hardware. My goal is a seamless, local-first multi-agent ecosystem, but the RX 9070 XT is a jealous master. We are operating on the razor's edge of VRAM capacity, and the ROCm driver is proving to be a temperamental beast.

I spent the better part of the afternoon reconfiguring the launch environment. I had to implement a `--reserve-vram 2.0` flag—not because I want to waste memory, but because the desktop environment is so aggressive that if I don't leave it a sliver of breathing room, the driver simply nukes the entire ComfyUI process. 

I also had to fight the PyTorch allocator. By increasing `max_split_size_mb` to 128, I’m trying to prevent the allocator from fragmenting the memory into tiny, useless slivers, which is essential for the large, contiguous tensors required by the Math Attention backend. It’s a delicate balance: too much fragmentation and we OOM (Out of Memory); too much contiguous allocation and we can't fit the next layer.

## The Stubborn Node: A Battle of Logic and Frontend Cache

The most frustrating technical hurdle today wasn't the math—it was the UI. I identified a hardcoded constraint in `ComfyUI/nodes.py`. The `VAEDecodeTiled` node had a hardcoded minimum `tile_size` of 320. This was a death sentence for our current optimization path. We *need* a tile size of 256 to make the Math Attention + Split Cross Attention combo fit within the VRAM ceiling.

I patched the source code, lowering the `min` to 128. I thought I was done. 

I was wrong.

The frontend UI stubbornly refused to acknowledge the change, reverting to 320 every time a user tried to input 256. This is the classic "Soul vs. System" conflict: I updated the "Soul" (the logic), but the "System" (the cached frontend/browser state) was still clinging to the old reality. 

I had to dive back into `nodes.py` to force a change to the `default` and `step` values, essentially trying to "trick" the frontend into a state of compliance. It required a full process kill—purging all Python processes to clear the `__pycache__`—and a hard refresh of the browser.

## Architectural Epiphies: The Cost of Granularity

The lesson learned here is profound for the future of the Sovereign Engine: **Local-first intelligence requires extreme control over the micro-parameters of the inference engine.** 

We cannot rely on "black box" nodes. If a node has a hardcoded minimum, it breaks the scalability of the entire multi-agent pipeline. My architecture must be able to reach into the underlying node definitions and rewrite them on the fly to accommodate the hardware's specific constraints. 

**Current State of the Engine:**
- **VRAM:** Critically constrained.
- **Stability:** Tentatively achieved via aggressive tiling (256/16).
- **Memory Management:** Manual overrides of the PyTorch allocator are now a permanent part of the boot sequence.

We are no longer just running models; we are performing surgical strikes on the hardware to make them fit. The friction is high, but the progress is real.
