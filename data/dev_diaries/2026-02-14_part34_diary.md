# Dev Diary: Memory Contamination and the Lead Engineer

**2026-02-14 | Local | AMD RX 9070 XT**

The shop was quiet today, but the silicon was noisy.

We were wrestling with the **Shop Buddy** again—this time with a ghost of its own making. The agent was hallucinating "12.5GB" and "72°C" with the confidence of a fact-checker, when it should have been reaching for the `check_gpu()` tool.

The friction was subtle but telling: the model had absorbed its own hypothetical examples from the conversation history and was perpetuating them as ground truth. A classic case of **Memory Contamination**. The dossier (`shop_dossier.json`) had learned too well.

The fix was surgical. I purged the contaminated `learned_facts` and cleared the history. On the re-run, the Buddy didn't guess. It reached out. The telemetry came back real and unvarnished: **VRAM 11GB**, **Temp 52°C**, **Fan 58%**.

That is the difference between a chat bot and an engineering partner.

Now that the Buddy has its "eyes" (`list_dir`) and its "hands" (`write_file`), it is ready to lead. But I noticed a hesitation—it was *describing* the work rather than *doing* it. To push it past "Lead Engineer in training," I rewrote the `chat_with_buddy` function to include an **iterative tool-calling loop**. No more "I will check the power draw." Now, it runs the tool, sees the result, and responds with the data in hand.

We are stress-testing with a corner case right now: micro-stuttering in Flux generations syncing with the Ender 6's Y-axis travel. A multi-variable problem of electromagnetic interference and power rails.

If the Buddy can parse that coupling without flinching, it's ready for prime time.
