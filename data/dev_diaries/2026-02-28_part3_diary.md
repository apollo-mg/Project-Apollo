# Dev Diary: 2026-02-28 (Part 3)

## The Vision-to-Parametric Pivot

Today was a massive architectural pivot. We spent the first half of the session building the foundation for "DesignMind"—a CAD tutor that would learn by watching my OnShape sessions. It was a beautiful idea: use vision to bridge the gap between my intent and the software's UI. I was even getting the `qwen2.5vl` pipeline running to recognize the OnShape interface.

But then, we hit a realization. Capturing a UI is one thing, but capturing the *geometry* is another. If I want Apollo to actually *make* things, it shouldn't just be a tutor; it needs to be a reverse-engineer.

I proposed a pivot: instead of just watching me click buttons, Apollo should look at physical objects through my webcam. If I place a 20mm cube next to a ruler, the AI should see the cube, recognize its dimensions via the scale, and then write the FeatureScript code to recreate it. 

This is a much higher bar. It requires the "Soul" (the semantic understanding of what an object *is*) to meet the "System" (the rigid, mathematical requirements of CAD).

## The Hardware Struggle: VRAM Warfare

The technical reality of doing this locally on the RX 9070 XT is... intense. Running `qwen2.5vl` for vision and then swapping to `deepseek-r1` for the engineering logic creates a massive VRAM tug-of-war. 

I ran into a nasty issue where the VRAM wasn't releasing. The `ollama stop` command was failing to clear the 14GB hog. I had to implement a "nuclear unload" function—a brute-force process that kills the PID and waits for the ROCm driver to actually report free memory. It's messy, but necessary when you're pushing the limits of local-first multi-agent systems.

Despite the friction, the result was breathtaking. I placed a 20mm test cube next to a ruler. Apollo's vision model identified the object, extracted the "Z" feature, and then—this is the magic—DeepSeek-R1 generated the exact FeatureScript code to replicate that specific cube. The loop is closed: **Image -> Geometric JSON -> CAD Code.** This is the blueprint for how Apollo will eventually "see" the physical world and digitize it.

## The Sovereign Mailbox

While the CAD module was stabilizing, we also pushed forward on the "Sovereign Mailbox" project. We successfully synchronized 347 emails from my Gmail to a local Maildir format. 

There was a brief moment of friction with an `unexpected eof` error during the `mbsync` process—likely a rate-limit kick from Google—but the system is resilient. It's incremental. We've successfully established a local, private ground for your incoming communications. The next step is building the `chronicler.py` module to parse these raw mail files into something the "Engineer" brain can ingest into the Vector DB.

## Core Lessons & Next Steps

- **Architecture:** The "Vision-to-Parametric" approach is the winner. It's harder than UI-watching, but the data quality is infinitely higher. We are no longer training a "clicker"; we are training a "designer."
- **System:** VRAM management is the primary bottleneck. I need to refine the `vram_management.py` logic to handle more aggressive model swapping between the vision and reasoning agents.
- **The Goal:** We have the first piece of "Gold Data"—the (Image + JSON + Code) trio. Now we just need to scale the production of this data.
