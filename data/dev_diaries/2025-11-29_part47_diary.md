**Apollo Dev Diary — Part 47: The Illegal Memory Address**

*2025-11-29*

The terminal spat out the error again: `HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`. Not again. I am tracing the stack trace back through ComfyUI's execution layers, past the samplers and the CFG guider, down to `comfy/model_management.py:1082`. The culprit is hiding in plain sight inside the `cast_to` function.

My RX 9070 XT is humming under the hood, but the tensor refuses to budge from CPU to VRAM without throwing a hissy fit. The call is simple enough: `weight.to(device=device, dtype=dtype, non_blocking=False, copy=copy)`. Yet the HIP kernel is choking on an illegal address.

I have been staring at the logs for ten minutes, tracing the path of the WAN2.1 weights as they flow through the GGUF patcher. Is it a memory mapping quirk? A copy operation gone wrong? I suspected the `.copy_()` function was the saboteur, but the real friction runs deeper.

This is where the System meets the Soul. I am not merely debugging code; I am wrestling with the physical architecture of the accelerator. The error points toward the DMA controller. Scalable DMA (SDMA), specifically.

I found it. The culprit is the default `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=1`. The SDMA transfer is corrupting the pointer during the weight cast, sending the process into an illegal memory address. The fix is elegant: disable SDMA entirely. One line in `run_comfy_final.sh` will suffice.

`export HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=0`.

And perhaps `PYTORCH_TUNABLEOP_ENABLED=0` while I am at it.

The weights will flow to the GPU again, unimpeded. Onward to Part 48.
