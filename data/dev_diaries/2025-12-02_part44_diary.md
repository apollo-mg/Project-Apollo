# Precision Mismatch: The Half-Precision Ghost in the Machine

The dream of local-first AI is a constant battle against the silicon-level idiosyncrasies of the hardware we’re actually running on. Today’s session was a brutal reminder of that. I’m sitting here, staring at the RX 9070 XT, watching the fans kick up as it tries to process the sheer weight of the WanVideo model. We’re pushing for high-fidelity video generation, moving away from the generic cloud-based APIs and toward something that actually feels like it has a *soul*.

But the machine? The machine is pedantic. It’s obsessed with types.

### The Error: A Dtype Schism

The terminal spat out a `RuntimeError`. Not a memory leak, not a VRAM fragmentation issue (though those are always lurking), but a fundamental breakdown in data types.

`Expected query, key, and value to have the same dtype, but got query.dtype: c10::Half key.dtype: float and value.dtype:t float instead.`

The math doesn't add up because the data types don't align. In the middle of a complex attention mechanism—specifically within the `scaled_dot_product_attention` call—the system was trying to perform a most-important-calculation with a mismatch. The Query was `Half` (FP16), but the Key and Value were `Float` (FP32). 

This is the classic friction of the "System." We are trying to run these massive, quantized models to fit within the 16GB VRAM envelope of the 9070 XT, but somewhere in the pipeline, a layer is casting things back to FP32, or a weight is being loaded in a way that breaks the chain of precision.

### The Technical Friction

Looking at the stack trace, the culprit is deep in the `WanVideo` implementation. We’re using `WanTEModel`, which is clearly part of this new frontier of video generation. It’s a massive, beautiful, terrifying architecture. 

The trace shows `Flash Attention` attempting to kick in. We’re using `--use-flash-attention` to keep things fast and lean, but when that failed, it fell back to `SDPA` (Scaled Dot Product Attention). And that’s where the mismatch hit the wall. 

It’s a direct clash between the "Soul" of the model—the intricate, non-linear relationships captured in the weights—and the "System"—the rigid requirement for every single tensor to play by the same rules of precision. If the Query is half-precision to save memory, but the Key is full-precision, the GPU driver (ROCm 7.1.1) just gives up. It won't guess. It won't "fix it" for you. It just crashes.

### The Multi-GPU Layer

What’s interesting is how the `MultiGPU` patching is handling this. We have all these custom nodes—`UnetLoaderMultiGPU`, `WanVideoSamplerMultiGPU`, `LoadWanVideoT5TextEncoderMultiGPU`—designed to manage the memory across the device and CPU. We are trying to implement a complex, heterogeneous memory management system (VRAM + CPU + Store Pruning) to keep the system from choking on its own complexity.

But even with `DisTorch` and `MultiGPU` patching attempting to manage the offloading, the fundamental atomicity of the tensor operation is still the final arb way. You can have the most advanced memory management in the world, but if your tensors aren't speaking the same language, the architecture collapses.

### Lessons for the Local-First Ecosystem

This error isn't just a bug; it's a roadmap. 

1.  **Precision Management is King:** As we move toward local multi-agent video generation, we can't just rely on "load it and see." We need more granular control over dtype casting in the wrapper nodes. The `WanVideoWrapper` needs to be smarter about enforcing a unified dtype across the entire attention block.
2.  **The ROCm Frontier:** Running on AMD means we are constantly on the bleeding edge. The `gfx1201` architecture is powerful, but the drivers and the PyTorch implementation (2.9.1+rocm) are still being battle-tested for these specific high-precision attention kernels.
3.  **Quantization vs. Stability:** We are pushing GGUF and NF4 quantization to the limit to fit these models. This is great for the "Soul"—it allows us to run massive models locally—but it creates a minefield of dtype mismatches. Every time we quantize, we are essentially introducing a new way for the system to break.
