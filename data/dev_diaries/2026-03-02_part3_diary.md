# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-02 (Part 3)

The battle for local-first sovereignty is fought in the gaps between silicon limits and model intelligence. Today was a chaotic, beautiful mess of VRAM Tetris, ROCm bottlenecks, and a sudden, massive leap in small-scale reasoning.

### The VRAM Tetris: A War of Attrition

I’ve been fighting a losing battle against the 16GB ceiling on my RX 9070 XT. We are currently running a massive 160k email ingestion task via vLLM, and it is absolutely devouring the system. The GPU is pinned at 100% utilization, and the VRAM is hovering at 14.4GB. Every time I try to benchmark a new model, I’m fighting for every last megabyte of breathing room.

We’ve moved into a "Cascading" architecture. The decision to use the `qwen3:8b` as a primary engine was a tactical win, but it’s a heavy-duty commitment. Running it via vLLM for the background ingestion is the only way to get the throughput we need, but it locks us into a rigid memory footprint. It’s a trade-off between the raw, unbridled speed of vLLM's continuous batching and the dynamic flexibility of Ollama. For a sovereign agent, flexibility usually wins, but for a data-crunching daemon, speed is king.

### The Intelligence Leap: The Qwen3 Breakthrough

The results from the V3 sandbox are staggering. We’ve officially transitioned from the "Llama 3.2" era into the "Qwen3" era. 

The `qwen3:8b` is a monster. It’s not just a minor update; it's a generational leap. My benchmarks on the "Sheep Problem" and "Python GIL" reasoning tasks showed it has the logical depth of a much larger model, yet it’s running at a sustained 20 TPS. It’s the perfect "Mid-Tier Engineer." 

Even more impressive is the `qwen3-vl:8b` vision model. It’s essentially a visual agent. The spatial reasoning jump (60.4% on MMLU-Pro) means it doesn't just see a screenshot—it *understands* the geometry of the interface. It’s the key to unlocking the "Glass Cockpit" dream.

### The New Receptionist: The Cascading Router

The most significant architectural epiphany of the day came from the failure of the FunctionGemma + Qwen3 combo. FunctionGemma, while fast, was too specialized; it lacked the "soul" to follow custom instruction-following without heavy fine-tuning. 

Instead, we implemented a **Cascading Router**:
1.  **Stage 1 (The Gatekeeper):** A tiny `qwen3:0.5b` model that handles the ultra-fast, low-complexity triage. It's running at nearly 400 TPS.
2.  **Stage 2 (The Thinker):** If the gatekeeper flags a request as high-complexity, it's handed off to the `qwen3:8b` engine.

This "dual-stage" approach allows us to maintain the 16GB VRAM limit. The 0.5B model is so light it barely touches the VRAM, leaving the rest for the heavy-duty reasoning. It’s a high-density, tiered intelligence stack that finally feels like a real brain, rather than just a collection of loose scripts.

### Lessons for the Ecosystem

We are seeing the emergence of a new class of "Small-Language Models" (SLMs) that are outperforming much larger predecessors. The "Soul" of the AI (the reasoning) is becoming decoupled from the "System" (the sheer parameter count). 

The future of local-first isn't about one giant model; it's about a highly specialized, tiered hierarchy of models—a swarm of specialists that can run on consumer-grade hardware. The friction between our desire for high-level reasoning and our limited VRAM is forcing us to become better architects. We aren't just prompting anymore; we are engineering complex, multi-agent neural hierarchies.
