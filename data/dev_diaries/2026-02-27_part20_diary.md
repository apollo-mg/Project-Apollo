# Dev Diary: The Shadow Mind Hunt (2026-02-27)

It is 22:30, and the hum of my AMD RX 9070 XT is the only sound in the shop. I am wrestling with PCIe lanes. Specifically, the question of whether x1 is sufficient for a "Swarm" of mining GPUs. The answer is yes—if they are independent experts running in silence. But if I want a "Shadow Mind" that can reason across distributed data in real-time, I need x8, or better yet, the 128 lanes of a used EPYC server.

The market is a cruel place today. DRAM has nearly doubled in price since the H100 shortage struck, and the "cheap storage" era of 4TB NVMe drives is over. Scavenging is the only strategy left. I am pinning my hopes on the IU Surplus store on Monday, though I fear the shelves may be picked clean by the flippers who know the value of a DDR4 RDIMM.

I spent the afternoon tuning the vision pipeline. Three tiers of detection: Tier 1 is the dumb pixel-diff (<1W). Tier 2 is the YOLO classification (10-50W), perfect for the RX 580 hoard. Tier 3 is the VLM description, the "Blink" feature that costs a spike of power (150W+). I am building for Tier 3 locally, so I can bypass the "corporate guardrail"—the euphemisms and liability refusals of the cloud. I want the objective truth of a "pry bar" vs a "screwdriver."

I fixed the inventory system today, too. The `#vision` channel now follows a "Look and Ask" policy. It describes the hardware, but it waits for my command before logging it to the database. Small victories.

Monday, 12:00 PM. I will hunt for a Dell Precision T7910 or an EPYC 7302P. The "Sovereign OS" is not complete without it.

Mark.
