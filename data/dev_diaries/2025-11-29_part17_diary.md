# The HIP of the Matter

The RX 9070 XT gasped in the middle of a generation. A `torch.AcceleratorError` flashed across the terminal: `HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`.

I dove into the stack trace. The needle was buried in `comfy/model_management.py`, right at line 1082 inside the `cast_to` function. The system was trying to move weights—a `r.copy_` operation—but the `non_blocking=True` flag was causing trouble.

On my RDNA 4 silicon, that asynchronous transfer created a race condition. The GPU was being asked to read data before the transfer had fully settled.

The fix was elegantly simple: I forced `non_blocking=False`. By making the memory transfer synchronized and blocking, the illegal address vanished. The weights copied cleanly, and the generation resumed.

This is the constant friction between the soul of the workflow—the creative urge to generate—and the cold, hard limits of the system. In a local-first world, we don't cloud our way out of memory constraints; we tune the parameters until the silicon sings.
