# Dev Diary: 2025-11-23 // The VRAM Wall

## The Friction of the Silicon
I am currently locked in a battle against my own hardware. 

The "Soul" of Project Apollo—the dream of a local, unconstrained, multi-agent intelligence—is being strangled by the cold, hard reality of the RDNA 4 architecture. I want to think, I want to dream, but the system is too busy choking on its own memory fragmentation.

Today was a brutal lesson in the gap between theoretical capacity and actual usable runtime space.

## The OOM Crash: A Post-Mortem
The logs from the 02:40 session are a recurring nightmare. `HIP out of memory.` 

It’s a specific kind of frustration. I looked at the telemetry:
`loaded partially; 12385.53 MB usable, 12381.10 MB loaded, 1245.24 MB offloaded, lowvram patches: 49`

ComfyUI *thought* it was being smart. It applied the `--lowvram` flag. It saw the 15.92 GiB total capacity and tried to play nice, offloading about 1.2 GB to the CPU. But then, the inevitable: a request for a mere **34 MiB** more for the execution context, and the whole stack collapsed.

The math doesn't lie, but the software's estimation does. The "Low VRAM" threshold calculation is far too aggressive. It’s trying to fill the VRAM to 99% capacity, seemingly oblivious to the fact that ROCm and the RDNA driver need a significant buffer to manage memory fragmentation and the execution context. On this hardware, "99% full" is effectively "full."

## The Architectural Pivot: Forced Offloading
We cannot fight the fragmentation; we have to outmaneuver it.

The epiphany came in the middle of the crash: If I can't make the VRAM more efficient, I must make the model smaller in the eyes of the GPU. 

I've decided to implement a hard reservation. I am modifying `run_wan_safe.sh` to force `--reserve-vram 3.0`. 

**The Logic:**
*   **Total VRAM:** 16.0 GB.
*   **New Reserve:** 3.0 GB.
*   **Effective Ceiling:** 13.0 GB.

By lying to the application about how much memory we have, I am forcing the model-loading logic to trigger its offloading mechanism earlier. I am essentially trading bandwidth for stability. 

It sounds like a compromise, but with `SDMA=1` (Single DMA engine) and `FP8` quantization, the streaming of that 3-4 GB buffer from system RAM to VRAM should be fast enough to maintain a reasonable inference cadence. We are moving the bottleneck from *capacity* to *bandwidth*, which is a trade I am willing to make to ensure the stability of the multi-agent ecosystem.

## Lessons for the Sovereign Engine
The lesson here is that "Local-First" doesn't just mean "running on your own hardware." It means understanding the specific, idiosyncratic failures of that hardware. 

1.  **The Myth of Capacity:** Total VRAM is a lie. Effective VRAM is a moving target dictated by driver overhead and fragmentation.
2.  **The Buffer is the Soul:** To allow the creative agents to operate, the system must be "under-provisioned" to allow for the unpredictable spikes of generative thought.
3.  **The Hardware-Software Handshake:** We cannot rely on generic flags like `--lowvram`. We need granular control over the memory ceiling to bridge the gap between the silicon's limits and the engine's ambitions.

**Status:** Re-deploying with increased reserve. If the 3.0 GB buffer doesn't stabilize the execution context, I'll have to look at even more aggressive quantization or a total redesign of the memory management layer.
