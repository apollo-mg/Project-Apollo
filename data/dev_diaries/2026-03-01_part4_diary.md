# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-01 (Part 4)

## The Great Migration: Reclaiming the Silicon

Today was a war of attrition against my own file system. I've spent the better part of the afternoon wrestling with a messy, fragmented NVMe drive that was suffocating my ability to actually develop. The user profile was bloated—323GB of pure waste, mostly redundant Windows Docker caches and massive, half-finished Downloads folders that were choking the IO bandwidth.

I initiated a massive move of the `Downloads` folder (133GB) to the 2TB mechanical drive. It’s a slow, grinding process. The drive is constantly being hit by the heavy reads of the move while the GPU is simultaneously being hammered by the vLLM benchmark. The system feels sluggish, like it's choking on its own breath, but the progress is steady. 

While that was churning in the background, I performed a surgical strike on the rest of the drive. I purged a 37GB WSL Ubuntu virtual disk and a 6.4GB Windows-side Ollama installation. These were ghosts of a former life—redundant artifacts of my transition to a pure Linux environment. We've already reclaimed over 100GB of the WD Black's space. The breathing room is returning.

## The VRAM Ceiling: vLLM vs. Ollama

The benchmark results for the DeepSeek-R1-14B (AWQ) on the RX 9070 XT were... sobering. 

We saw a throughput of 2.28 tokens/s. That's embarrassingly low. The culprit? The "Mixed Prefill-Decode PIECEWISE" graph capture. The overhead of the V1 engine on RDNA 4 architecture is eating the performance gains we expected. It's essentially a massive tax on the silicon's ability to actually execute.

Then, we hit the wall with the FP8 attempt. It's a hard `OutOfMemoryError`. 14GB for weights on a 16GB card leaves zero room for the system, the context window, or the Triton compiler buffers. This confirms our architectural reality: **AWQ (4-bit) is the only way forward for 14B models on this hardware.** It keeps the weights at ~8.5GB, leaving enough headroom for the complex, massive HIP graphs that vLLM requires to operate.

It highlights the fundamental rift in modern AI architecture: 
*   **vLLM** is a brute-force enterprise beast. It wants to own the whole GPU. It's built for throughput, not for flexibility.
*   **Ollama (llama.cpp)** is the pragmatic consumer choice. It's built to spill over into system RAM when the VRAM hits the ceiling.

For Project Apollo, this is a massive decision. If we want the "Receptionist" to be a hyper-fast, low-latency voice agent, we might have to accept the VRAM "tax" of vLLM, or stick to the modularity of Ollama.

## The "Jarvis" Vision

We touched on the potential for a voice-first interface. The idea of using vLLM for a small 1B model like `Llama-3.2` is tempting for the speed, but the VRAM reservation would cripple the higher-level reasoning models. 

However, we found a way to bypass the VRAM bottleneck for the audio pipeline. By utilizing `whisper.cpp` with `hipBLAS`, we can get near-instant transcription on the AMD hardware without the Python-induced memory fragmentation of `faster-whisper`. The audio stack can remain lean and independent, allowing the "Thinking" stack (Ollama) to stay focused on the heavy-duty reasoning tasks.
