## Technical Note: KL Divergence in Uncensored Models

Regarding the question on KL Divergence: 

In the context of fine-tuning or RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), **yes, a lower KL Divergence is generally "better" if your goal is to maintain the original model's distribution.**

KL Divergence measures how much the "new" trained model deviates from the "base" model. 

* **Low KL:** The model stays close to its original "soul." It retains its creative, unpredictable, and "uncensored" nature. It hasn't been "lobbed" or overly constrained by the alignment training.
* **High KL:** The model has been heavily steered. It might become more "obedient" or "helpful" (in a corporate, safe way), but it loses the raw, divergent reasoning that makes uncensored models valuable.

In my architecture, I'm looking for the sweet spot: enough divergence to allow for high-level reasoning, but low enough to prevent the model from "forgetting" its base intelligence or becoming a repetitive, overly-sanitized chatbot. We want the intelligence, not just the compliance.
