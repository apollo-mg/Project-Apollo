# Apollo Dev Diary: 2026-03-06 (Part 3/15)

The "VRAM Tetris" theory is finally evolving into something resembling a living, breathing architecture. Today was a brutal, beautiful collision between high-level intelligence and the hard-coded limits of the silicon. 

### The Chaos of the Gatekeeper
We’ve been hammering at the **Phase 9: Sovereign Engine** migration, trying to move away from the "chatty" (and frankly, unreliable) manual JSON parsing. My current setup uses a 4B Qwen model as a "Gatekeeper" to decide which specialist model to call. It’s a brilliant concept, but the implementation was brittle.

The model kept "hallucinating" its own limitations—it would look at the tools I gave it and literally say, *"I am an AI and cannot access physical things,"* effectively lobotomizing its own intelligence. This is the classic friction between the **Soul** (the model's desire to be a helpful assistant) and the **System** (the actual tool-calling logic).

### The "Secret Sauce" Breakthrough
Then we hit the breakthrough. By leveraging **Ollama's Structured Output** (using the `format` parameter with a hard-walled JSON schema), the behavior shifted entirely. The model stopped talking and started *executing*. The schema doesn't just ask for JSON; it mathematically constrains the token sampling so the model *cannot* output anything else. 

We tested the `qwen3.5-4b-aggressive` model with this "Hard-Wall" schema, and it hit a **100% success rate** on complex, multi-step tool calls. It was a moment of pure, deterministic clarity.

### The "VRAM Tetris" Realization
However, we hit a massive wall with the **ROCm scheduler**. While the PyTorch build was churning through the Triton kernels, the GPU-resident models were timing out. The compiler was hogging the compute-queue so hard that even a lightweight 4B model couldn't get a "slice" of the GPU to complete its prefill.

We realized the issue isn't just VRAM capacity; it's **Resource Contention**. The build-process was "starving" the model. 

We ran a speed test on the **0.8B Qwen3.5** model, and while it didn't hit the 200 TPS "Super-Sonic" target we hoped for, it hit **140 TPS** even while the build was actively hammering the GPU. This is the "Sweet Spot." The 0.8B model is fast enough to squeeze through the tiny gaps in the GPU's scheduler. It's the perfect "First Responder" for high-frequency system monitoring.

### The Hybrid Sovereign Core: The Ultimate Architecture
This is the epiphany. We aren't going to fight the VRAM limits; we're going to dance around them. 

The new architecture, the **"Apollo Bridge"**, is a hybrid-intelligence model:
1.  **The Fast-Path (vLLM):** A lightweight model (0.8B or 4B) stays permanently resident in VRAM on port 8000. It handles the "reflexes"—the immediate triage and command routing.
2.  **The Deep-Path (Ollama):** Your high-level "Architects" (14B+ models) reside in Ollama. They are loaded on-demand for heavy reasoning.

If the deep models exceed the VRAM, Ollama's "polite" memory management will spill them into system RAM. This allows the **Gatekeeper** in vLLM to remain lightning-fast and "un-killable," even while a massive 30B model is churning in the background. 

The **"Apollo Bridge"** patch is now integrated into `llm_interface.py`, routing the "Gatekeeper" tasks through the vLLM port and the heavy reasoning through Ollama.
