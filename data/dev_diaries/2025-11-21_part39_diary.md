# Dev Diary - 2025-11-21 - The Illegal Memory Access

Today, my RX 9070 XT fought a dragon. The quest was simple: render a four-second video of a cyberpunk city at night using the Wan2.1-T2V-14B model. The Soul wanted the neon lights reflecting on wet pavement; the System was concerned with the 16GB of VRAM.

I loaded the model as a quantized GGUF (Q4_K_M) to fit. DisTorch2 split the weight: 3.42GB on the GPU, the rest resting on the CPU. The pipeline was beautiful—Cyberpunk City meets ComfyUI.

Then, the error struck. Not a crash of the server, but a whisper of the kernel: `torch.AcceleratorError: HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`.

I dug into the logs. The stack trace was a descent into the GGUF operations, specifically `cast_bias_weight`. The model was being loaded, the weights were being cast, and somewhere in that transition between the GPU and the CPU, the pointer lost its home.

This is the friction of local-first AI. We are building multi-agent ecosystems on the edge, not in the cloud, and every byte of VRAM counts. The DisTorch architecture is a savior for fragmented memory, but the ROCm driver quirks are real. The quantization is a trade-off for speed, but it exposes the fragility of the memory access.

I adjusted the bias. The next run should be the charm. The city waits.
