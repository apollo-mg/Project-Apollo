# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-26

The friction between the soul and the silicon is real. Today was a mess of silent failures, background processes, and the constant struggle to turn raw compute into something resembling intelligence.

I spent a significant chunk of the afternoon fighting the environment. I attempted to pull the Unsloth Qwen 35B MoE via `huggingface-cli`, but the damn thing crashed silently. It turns out the CLI wasn't in the global system path, and instead of throwing a clean error, it just vanished into the ether. It's a reminder that when you're building locally, you can't rely on the polished abstractions of a cloud environment; you have to touch the bare metal of the OS. 

I had to bypass the abstraction entirely, manually forcing `wget` to pull the weights directly into `/mnt/TG_2TB/Projects/Apollo/models`. It's slower, but it's certain. 

The core architectural epiphany from today's session is the death of the "Monolithic Model." I’ve been thinking about my "tool chest" approach. We're not looking for one god-model to rule the system. The 10B models are my high-speed routers—they parse intent, handle the initial "handshake," and then hand off the heavy lifting to the 27B or the 35B MoE. It's a tiered, multi-agent architecture. The 35B MoE is the wildcard; if the offloading parameters work, it's the brain. If they don't, and we hit the VRAM ceiling on this RX 9070 XT, it's just expensive paperweight.

I've prepared a custom launcher script: `run_qwen_35b_unsloth.sh`. It's tuned specifically for the Unsloth "Thinking" mode parameters—high temperature, specific top-p settings. I need to see if Unsloth actually solved the RAM thrashing issue we've been dreading. If that model hits the VRAM limit and starts swapping to the NVMe, the latency will kill the "agentic" feel of the system.

I saw some news from Runpod today—looks like the industry is moving toward even tighter integration between inference engines (vLLM) and serverless workflows. It's interesting to see others hitting the same walls I am with model scale vs. VRAM, but there's a certain rebellious joy in doing this on my own desk, local-first, away from the cloud-provider hegemony.

The session ended on a bit of a technical hiccup—the Gemini backend hung, likely a memory leak or a timeout in the local bridge. I had to kill the process and restart. It's frustrating, but it's part of the process. The system is still volatile. The architecture is still being forged in the fire of these crashes.
