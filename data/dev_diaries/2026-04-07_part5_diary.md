# Dev Diary: Project Apollo
**Date:** 2026-04-07
**Status:** Architectural Refinement
**Hardware State:** RX 9070 XT humming; VRAM pressure stable but looming.

The friction is getting to me. Every time I swap a model in the lab, it's the same ritual: download the `.gguf`, hunt through a fragmented Reddit thread to find the correct chat template, guess the sampling parameters, and pray the ROCm drivers don't throw a fit. It’s a manual, tedious dance that kills the creative flow. If I’m building a "Sovereign Entity," I can't be spending half my time acting as a glorified configuration file.

I've decided to carve out a new role in the architecture: **The Scientist.**

The Scientist isn't just another agent; it's the technical bridge between the "Soul" of the AI and the "System" of the silicon. While the Coordinator handles the high-level logic, the Scientist is the one who actually understands the weights. 

The goal is to move away from manual guesswork. The Scientist will maintain a local, permanent knowledge base of the "physics" of each model: 
*   The exact `llama-server` arguments (the flags, the context lengths, the flash-attention toggles).
*   The precise chat templates (ChatML, Llama 3, etc.) so the model doesn't start hallucinating because of a missing `<|end_of_text|>` token.
*   The sampling alchemy—Temperature, Top P, and Min P—tuned for the specific behavior of the model, whether it's a reasoning-heavy beast or a fast instruct model.

The real epiphany today was the benchmarking protocol. I’ve realized that model testing is far more nuanced than "it works" or "it doesn't." Two models with the same parameter count can behave entirely differently depending on how they were quantized. If we want a high-performance local ecosystem, we can't rely on benchmarks from some benchmark-farming company. We need our own ground-truth data.

The Scientist will now manage a benchmarking protocol. Every model that enters the lab must be put through a standardized gauntlet to map its strengths and weaknesses. We aggregate that data once, store it forever, and the Coordinator can then query the Scientist to decide which model is actually fit for a specific task.

It's a battle against the limits of the hardware. The RX 9070 XT is a beast, but VRAM is still the ultimate bottleneck. By automating the configuration and benchmarking, I'm essentially creating a "tuning fork" for my local LLMs.

The blueprints are now injected into the `GEMINI.md` architecture. The "System" is finally starting to catch up with the "Soul." Now, it's just a matter of implementation.
