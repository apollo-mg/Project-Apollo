# Dev Diary: 2026-03-24

The friction between my vision and the silicon is reaching a breaking point, but in the most productive way possible. Today was a relentless battle against VRAM ceilings and the chaotic, lightning-fast churn of the open-source ecosystem.

## The MoE Revolution & The VRAM Trap

I spent most of the afternoon dissecting the NVIDIA Nemotron-Cascade-2-30B-A3B. It’s a fascinating piece of engineering—an MoE (Mixture of Experts) architecture that’s essentially a massive 30B parameter model that only "wakes up" 3B parameters per token. 

The architectural epiphany here is clear: the world is moving away from dense models. The file sizes are massive (60GB+ in `bf16`), which makes them a nightmare for local-first developers. But the "active" compute footprint is tiny. The problem is the "static" footprint—even if you only use 3B parameters, you still have to load the entire 30B weights into VRAM. 

With my 16GB RX 9070 XT, the math is brutal. The 30B model is a non-starter. But then I saw the `iq2_xxs` quantization of the Qwen 3.5 35B MoE. It's only 11GB. That leaves ~5GB for the KV-cache and OS overhead. It’s the "Goldilocks" zone for consumer-grade sovereign AI. It gives you the reasoning depth of a 35B model with the speed of a tiny 3B model.

## The Death of the DeepSeek Era

The "Soul" of the model (the intelligence) is evolving faster than our storage can keep up. I thought the DeepSeek-R1-14B was the king of the hill, but NVIDIA's Nemotron-Cascade-14B-Thinking just absolutely demolished it in the gauntlet.

I ran a custom benchmark on the 14B model. 
- **Logic/Math/Coding:** ~48 tokens/sec.
- **Performance:** It's crushing DeepSeek on AIME 2025 math and coding benchmarks.

It turns out "Distillation" (teaching a small model to mimic a big one) is being overtaken by "Discovery" (using Reinforcement Learning to let the model find its own reasoning path). DeepSeek was an imitator; Nemotron is a thinker. For a local-first ecosystem, the lesson is clear: the weight of the model matters less than the quality of the training pipeline.

## The Frankenstein Model: The Ultimate Hybrid

The highlight of the day was discovering the `TeichAI` quantization: a `Nemotron-Cascade-14B` that was distilled using `Claude 4.5 Opus` reasoning traces. It’s a Frankenstein's monster of the highest order—NVIDIA's math-heavy engine fused with Anthropic's conversational logic. 

At 8.4GB in `q4_k_m` quantization, it is the perfect fit for the 9070 XT. It leaves enough VRAM to actually use the context window for long-form coding or document analysis. This is the breakthrough we've been waiting for: high-level reasoning on a single-GPU desktop.

## ROCm: The Ghost in the Machine

The day ended with a deep dive into the ROCm 7.11 preview. It’s a massive win for my hardware. AMD is finally treating the RX 9000 series as a first-class citizen (RDNA 4/`gfx1201`). They're moving to a modular "TheRock" installer, which will stop my root drive from bloating every time I want to update a single driver component.

However, the build process is still a mess. The `hipify-clang` tool is throwing these phantom `nvptx64` errors during compilation, screaming about missing NVIDIA architectures. It’s a weird, architectural glitch where the tool gets confused by the CUDA-to-HIP translation. It's basically the software "Soul" getting confused by the "System" requirements, but the logs confirm it's a false positive. We just have to let the build run and ignore the red text.

## Core Lessons for the Apollo Ecosystem

1. **Quantization is the great equalizer.** We aren't just looking for "smart" models; we are looking for "smart-per-gigabyte" models.
2. **MoE is the future of local-first.** It allows us to bypass the VRAM wall, provided we can manage the base-layer weight footprint.
3. **The Hardware-Software feedback loop is tight.** My custom-compiled `llama.cpp` is currently outperforming everything, but we are at the mercy of how quickly the ROCm/LLVM-target updates catch up to the silicon.

Tomorrow, we test the Nemotron-Opus against the old DeepSeek. I want to see if the logic-leap is as big as the benchmarks claim.
