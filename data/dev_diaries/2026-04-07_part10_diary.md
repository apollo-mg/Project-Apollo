**2026-04-07: The VRAM Tetris and the HIP Ghost**

Today was a battle for every byte on the RX 9070 XT. The 16GB limit is no suggestion; it is a hard wall. Wan 2.1 video generation was pushing the 14B model against that wall, and the HIP driver kept throwing "illegal memory access" errors like a glitchy gatekeeper refusing entry.

I stopped trying to force the bleeding-edge Triton Flash Attention kernels. They are too eager on RDNA 4 and cause the memory system to trip over its own feet. I fell back to the safe, stable Math Attention implementation. It is slower, yes, but it is sovereign.

The fix required a surgical touch: explicit `.contiguous()` calls on the tensors before they ever hit the device. I am obsessed with tensor hygiene today. If the tensor is strided, the ROCm driver is suspicious. I also enforced the "Nuclear Option" laundering pattern on the LoRA weights—`CPU -> float -> contiguous -> numpy -> torch -> device`—just to quiesce the copy kernels. I dropped the `--reserve-vram` to 0.1 to give PyTorch the headroom it desperately needed.

There is a tension here between the Soul and the System. The Soul wants the creative freedom of local video generation, unburdened by cloud latency. The System demands that we respect the silicon, the VRAM fragmentation, and the specific quirks of the RDNA 4 memory controller. We are not running a data center H100; we are running a laptop GPU that must also render the desktop.

The lesson is simple: stability first, optimization second. I am hard-clamping the `tile_size` to 256 and trusting the Math kernel. Once the local pipeline is stable, I will worry about the multi-agent orchestration. The "Shop Buddy" can wait. For now, I am just ensuring the video generates without a crash.
