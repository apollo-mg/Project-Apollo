# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-01-14

The friction between "Soul" and "System" is nowhere more apparent than when you're trying to tame a machine that's too stiff for its own good. I spent today wrestling with the ghost of physics—the vibrations that refuse to die.

### The Stiffness Paradox

I thought the new aluminum top plate and the carbon fiber gantry would be the ultimate upgrade. I wanted a machine that felt like a solid block of granite, capable of blistering accelerations. I got it. The resonance graphs (shaper_calibrate) prove it: the X-axis is hitting a massive 145Hz. In the world of CoreXY, that is absolute god-tier stiffness. 

But here is the problem with pure, unadulterated stiffness: **Damping is gone.**

The machine no longer has the "give" to absorb the energy from the motors. When I bumped the X/Y current to 1.8A, the motors began "cogging"—snapping between magnetic poles with such violence that the vibration was being transmitted directly through the stiff-as-hell frame into the nozzle. The result was that "woodgrain" texture—Vertical Fine Artifacts (VFA) that looked like a nightmare.

### The Nodding Axis

Things got even weirder when I swapped to the metal hotend plate. The X-axis remained a clean, high-frequency spike, but the Y-axis became a mess. The input shaper identified a massive, low-frequency "nodding" mode at 88Hz. 

It’s a mechanical lever effect. The mass of the new hotend plate is creating a torque on the X-rail carriage. When the Y-axis accelerates, the toolhead isn't just moving; it’s twisting. It’s "nodding" up and down. The Input Shaper is trying to fix the belt vibration at 143Hz, but it’s effectively blind to the 88Hz wobble because the shaper algorithm prioritizes the primary X/Y plane. This is why I'm seeing those layer inconsistencies—the toolhead is literally dancing vertically while it tries to move horizontally.

### The Philosophy of Tuning

I had a moment of doubt when I looked at the Nero3D advice. He advocates for the "MZV" shaper—the sniper approach. It’s a way to prioritize sharp corners over total vibration suppression. In a standard printer, this is the way to go. 

But my architecture is different. The mathematics of the "smoothing penalty" are tied to the frequency ($1/f^2$). Because my machine is so fast and stiff, the "heavy" shapers (2HUMP_EI) don't actually cause the rounding/mushiness that they do on a slower machine. I have the headroom to use the "shotgun" approach. The shotgun covers the messy, low-frequency nodding that the sniper would miss.

### Lessons for the Local-First Ecosystem

As I develop the multi-agent logic for Apollo, this session reinforces a core principle: **The digital twin must account for mechanical-induced noise.** 

The AI (Gemini) isn't just looking at G-code; it's interpreting the *physical reality* of the toolhead's movement through the frequency response. We aren't just building a brain; we're building a brain that understands the weight and the "wobble" of its own body.
