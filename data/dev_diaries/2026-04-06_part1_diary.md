# Project Apollo: Dev Diary Entry - 2026-04-06

## The $0.20 Miracle and the 40k Wall

I spent most of the morning staring at a progress bar that wasn't moving. 

The Gemma 4 26B MoE (Mixture of Experts) was currently "alive" according to `pgrep`, but it was effectively a ghost in the machine. The `llama-server` was completely unresponsive to API calls—a total thread lock. It turns out when you shove a 40,000-token architectural context into a MoE model, the VRAM bandwidth becomes the ultimate bottleneck. The routing calculations for those tokens are so massive they effectively choke the HTTP server thread.

The fans on the RX 9070 XT were screaming, but the VRAM was the real concern. I could hear the spooling, and the system was clearly under immense pressure. It’s that classic friction between the Soul and the System: I want this model to have the creative depth to synthesize 48 distinct proposals, but the Silicon is struggling to route the attention heads across that much context. 

I almost thought the ROCm driver had crashed—praying to avoid those dreaded `MUL_MAT_ID` segmentation faults—but the VRAM was still occupied, which meant the compute thread was just buried in the deep end of the evaluation phase. It’s what I’m calling the "$0.20 Miracle"—a cheap, local model performing high-level synthesis, provided we can survive the compute-heavy prompt evaluation.

## Architectural Epiphany: Logical Certificates

The most productive moment of the session came from a realization about the synthesis pass. We were getting coherent results, but the deduplication of ideas across the 48 different proposals was messy. 

I decided to pivot. Instead of just letting the model dump a raw Markdown file, I surgically injected a "Semi-Formal Reasoning" requirement into the `SYNTHESIZER_PROMPT` within `apollo_synthesizer.ts`. 

Now, the model is forced to go through a formal proof-like structure before it's allowed to call `file_write`:
- **PREMISES:** It must explicitly state the overlapping ideas found in the batch.
- **EXECUTION PATH:** It must trace the logic of how it plans to organize the tasks.
- **CONCLUSION:** It must provide a formal derivation that the resulting task list satisfies the original intent without redundancy.

By forcing this "Logical Certificate" into the thought trace, we aren't just asking for a summary; we are demanding a proof of coherence. It’s a massive increase in computational overhead, but the payoff in the structural integrity of the `master_action_plan.md` should be worth it.

## Hardware Friction and Thermal Management

The session hit a snag when the fans stopped spinning. I had to kill the entire stack, perform a CachyOS system update, and reboot to a fresh state just to clear the VRAM and reset the daemon. 

It’s a reminder that even with a high-end 9070 XT, we aren't playing by the rules of standard consumer hardware. The default fan curves are tuned for core/edge temps, but they are dangerously blind to VRAM-specific thermals. I’ve downloaded LACT (Linux AMD Control Tool) to implement a custom fan curve. If we're going to run MoE models with massive context windows, we cannot rely on the factory curves; we need to aggressively cool the memory controllers or the whole system will throttle into oblivion.

## Core Lessons for the Multi-Agent Ecosystem

1. **Context is the Bottleneck:** In local-first multi-agent systems, the "intelligence" is limited not just by parameter count, but by how much context the VRAM-constrained MoE can "look at" at once.
2. **Reasoning as a Constraint:** Adding a formal reasoning requirement to a prompt increases latency but drastically increases the "intelligence density" of the output.
3. **Local Hardware is Unpredictable:** Running cutting-edge models on consumer-grade ROCm drivers requires constant manual intervention and custom thermal management.
