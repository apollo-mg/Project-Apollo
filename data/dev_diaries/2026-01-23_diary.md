# Dev Diary: January 23, 2026

## The Thermal Ghost in the Machine

The session started with a fight against thermodynamics. My sensor was losing its mind because the coil resistance was climbing as the temperature rose. The signal was collapsing, making the system think the probe was floating in "Open Air." It was a classic case of the "System" failing because the "Soul"—the actual physical reality of the sensor—was being throttned by heat.

I had to manually crank the `reg_drive_current` in `printer.cfg` just to keep the signal alive. It’s a reminder that no matter how smart the software gets, the silicon and the copper are still bound by the laws of physics. Once the current was bumped, the sensor stabilized. The "Soul" was back, even if it was a bit too hot for its own good.

## The Dance and the Geometry of Chaos

After stabilizing the sensor, I decided to flex the hardware. I implemented a `BED_DANCE` macro—a little rhythmic "breathing" movement for the Tri-Z bed. It’s a purely aesthetic victory, but it serves a purpose: testing the mechanical limits of the spherical joints. I had to be careful with the amplitude; too much tilt and I'd bind the linear rails or bend the lead screws. 

But the real headache wasn't the dance; it was the geometry. I was trying to set the probe points, but I was thinking in "Target Probe Coordinates" while Klipper's `points` definition requires "Nozzle Coordinates." 

It's a constant mental tax. I was visualizing the probe touching a specific spot on the bed, but the math required me to subtract the offset (`15.8, 33.5`) to tell the nozzle where to go. This led to a collision with the bed boundaries (Y = -3.5). I had to recalibrate the math to keep the nozzle within the safe bounds of the machine. It’s a tedious, gritty cycle of: *Measure -> Math -> Error -> Re-measure*.

## The Resonance Revelation

This was the most significant technical breakthrough—and the most sobering. 

I ran the Input Shaper calibration for both X and Y. The X-axis is a beast. It's incredibly stiff, showing a clean, high-frequency resonance. But the Y-axis? The Y-axis is a disaster. 

The resonance graph for Y showed a massive, violent energy spike at 80 Hz. More importantly, the Z-axis (the bed) was picking up that exact same 80 Hz vibration. 

The diagnosis is clear: the bed assembly is vibrating like a tuning fork. When the Y-gantry jerks, it's shaking the entire frame/bed structure at 80 Hz. The "Soul" of the machine (the structural rigidity) has been compromised by the new Tri-Z geometry. The bed is no longer a rigid platform; it's a vibrating mass. This is the friction between the "Soul" (the ambitious 3-point Z-axis) and the "System" (the inherent vibration limits of the frame). 

## Final Thoughts

I’m currently debating whether to add a cooling fan to the EBB36 toolhead board. The chamber gets hot, and heat is the enemy of both the sensor and the driver. But every gram I add is a penalty to my Input Shaper. 

The architecture is evolving, but it's currently a heavy, vibrating, thermal mess. I have a high-performance X-axis trapped in a low-performance Y-axis world. Tomorrow, I tackle the bed rigidity. 

**Current Status:**
- Sensor: Stable (with high current).
- Z-Tilt: Calibrated (but physically vibrating).
- X-Axis: Rock solid.
- Y-Axis: A vibrating nightmare.
