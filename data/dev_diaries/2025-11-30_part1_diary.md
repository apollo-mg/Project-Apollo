# Dev Diary: 2025-11-30
## The Battle of the Bit: RDNA 4, RMSNorm, and the Ghost in the Machine

The architecture is stable, but the silicon is being temperamental. Today was a relentless cycle of chasing phantom crashes and wrestling with the physical limits of the RX 9070 XT. 

### The Crash: A Ghost in the VRAM
The session began with a crash during video generation. At first glance, it looked like a standard Out-of-Memory (OOM) error, but the logs were suspiciously silent. The `comfy_new_v*.log` files were truncated—the process was dying so fast it couldn't even flush its error buffer to the disk. 

I spent hours digging through the wreckage of `stderr` and `stdout`. I realized the issue wasn't just a crash; it was a hardware-specific instability. The "Illegal Memory Access" error I encountered is a recurring nightmare for RDNA 4 architecture. 

**The Breakthrough:**
The culprit was the `RMSNorm` implementation. On AMD hardware, the standard PyTorch `rms_norm` was hitting precision/overflow issues when handling the massive tensors of the Wan 2.1 model. 

I had to implement a "Safe RMSNorm" patch. By forcing an upcast to `float32` and introducing manual calculation, I bypassed the driver-level instability. This was a surgical strike: I patched `comfy/rmsnorm.py`, ensuring the math is done in a higher precision before casting back down. This resolved the "Illegal Memory Access" that was causing the entire pipeline to collapse during the model's weight transfer.

### The Memory Landscape: The Tiled Reality
We had a deep dive into the VRAM pressure of the Wan 2.1 workflow. It's a brutal landscape.
* **The Static Peak:** The 14B model is a behemoth. Even in FP8, it occupies ~14GB of our 16GB VRAM. This leaves almost zero "breathing room" for the actual computation (the KV Cache/Attention). This is why the `--reserve-vram` flag is non-negotiable; we have to manually carve out space for the driver, or the OS will kill the process.
* **The Dynamic Peak:** The VAE is the "Silent Killer." While the model is unloaded, the act of decoding a video frame-set creates massive, temporary 3D tensors. If you don't use `VAEDecodeTiled`, the VRAM demand spikes instantly, exceeding 16GB and triggering a hard crash.

### The Friction: Soul vs. System
There is a constant tension between the "Soul" (the creative potential of a 14B video model) and the "System" (the rigid, unyielding limits of 16GB of VRAM). We are building something incredible, but we are building it on a razor's edge. The software is trying to do something the hardware can barely comprehend.

### Lessons for the Local-First Ecosystem:
1.  **Hardware-Specific Patching is Mandatory:** We cannot assume "Generic AI" works on AMD. Our engine must be aware of the architecture (e.g., the `is_amd()` checks) to implement custom safety fallbacks.
2.  **Memory Management is the real UX:** For local-first, the user shouldn't care about VRAM, but they *will* care about the crash. Our job is to move the complexity from "The user must fix this" to "The engine manages this via Tiled/LowVRAM modes."
3.  **The Pathing Nightmare:** Even after fixing the core engine, the file system/YAML configuration remains a point of friction. The mapping of external storage (the 2TB drive) to the ComfyUI structure is still brittle.

**Current Status:** System is patched. RMSNorm is stable. VAE is tiled. The engine is ready to breathe again.

**Next Step:** Tackle the external drive mounting/YAML persistence issue.
