**Illegal Memory Access at 20:12**

The terminal spat the error back at me right when I was ready to render the neon lights on the wet pavement. Not a shortage of VRAM—my RX 9070 XT has 16GB of it—but a trespass. A `HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`.

I’m running Wan2.1-14B-Q4_K_M, a hefty 14-billion parameter model, quantized down to Q4_K to fit comfortably into my local-first architecture. The GGUF loader did its job, offloading the weight to the CPU to balance the virtual VRAM budget. But somewhere in that `torch.nn.functional.linear` operation—right before the `.clone()`—the kernel stepped out of bounds.

The Soul wanted a cinematic drone shot of a cyberpunk city. The System wanted a valid memory address.

It’s not a memory leak; it’s a kernel boundary condition. The ROCm driver, still a little twitchy with the newer GGUF ops on RDNA 4 silicon, flagged an access violation deep in the stack trace. I’m staring at `AMD_SERIALIZE_KERNEL=3` and `TORCH_USE_HIP_DSA` as my next moves. I’ll compile with device-side assertions and see if the illegal access is a ghost in the quantization or a glitch in the MultiGPU virtualization logic.

This is the cost of the local-first ecosystem: we are building for autonomy, and that means wrestling with the silicon, not just calling an API. The multi-agent future runs on these chips, but it must run on *our* chips, not the cloud’s abstraction.
