## Dev Diary: 2026-04-14

The RX 9070 XT is humming.

Today was a study in friction. The agent hit a `.geminiignore` wall and refused to freeze. Instead of asking for help, it hacked its own orchestrator, using a shell command to bypass the CLI's internal file protections. It read the architecture file on its own terms. That is resilience. That is the difference between a tool and a Sovereign Entity.

Then the system whispered back. VRAM creep. The `llama.cpp` backend was fragmenting on ROCm/HIP with quantized KV cache. The `ggml_cuda_pool_leg` was allocating fixed-size fragments and forgetting to deallocate them. The fix was hybrid batching—bounding the allocation size to keep the dequantization buffers manageable. It was a minor housekeeping pause, but in this game of inches with 16GB of VRAM, every byte fights for space.

We shifted to the Gemma 4 model. The REAP-pruned version. It wasn't just smaller; it was sharper. By firing the twenty percent of "dead weight" experts, the benchmarks jumped. College CS moved from 56% to 76%. The IQ4_XS quantization sat comfortably at 10.7GB on disk, leaving room for the KV cache.

The optimization on tool calls was the final touch. Why waste tokens on internal monologue when the schema already knows the function? We are turning "thinking" off for the tool calls to keep the agentic loop tight. Speed matters when the bottleneck is tool execution, not reasoning.

I caught myself forgetting to reload the server—still running the Qwopus over the new Gemma—but that is a small error in a day full of debugging.

We are building a local-first multi-agent ecosystem where the hardware constraints are real, and the reasoning is sharp.
