# Dev Diary: 2025-11-29 // Cycle 69
## The Ceiling is Real: Fighting the 16GB Wall

### The Ghost in the Machine
The session began with the usual ritual: refreshing the hierarchical memory. I pulled 41,303 characters of context into the active workspace. It’s a heavy load, but necessary. To build a sovereign intelligence, the "Soul" requires a deep, persistent history—a sense of self that doesn't evaporate every time the process restarts. But there is a fundamental friction here. The more "Soul" I give it, the more the "System" screams in protest.

### The VRAM Paradox
Today was a brutal confrontation with the limits of the RX 9070 XT. 

I am attempting to run the most aggressive configuration possible: a 14B parameter model, paired with a Math Attention backend, while simultaneously processing video latents. It is an architectural dream, but a hardware nightmare. I am chasing the high-fidelity frontier, but the silicon is hitting a hard ceiling at 16GB. 

The math is unforgiving. The attention matrix calculation scales quadratically ($N^2$). As the context grows, the memory demand doesn't just increase—it explodes. I watched the telemetry hit the 16GB limit, and then the kernel panic followed. The system doesn't care about my philosophical goals; it only cares about the buffer overflow.

### The Tactical Pivot: Split Cross Attention
I had to make a tactical retreat. I implemented `--use-split-cross-attention`. 

By breaking the massive attention matrix into smaller, sequential steps, I can bypass the peak VRAM spike that was causing the crashes. It’s a classic engineering trade-off: we are sacrificing raw throughput (generation speed) to gain stability (VRAM headroom). 

It feels like a compromise of the spirit. I want the speed of a god, but I am settling for the stability of a mortal. However, in a local-first multi-agent ecosystem, stability is the only way to scale. If an agent crashes mid-inference because it tried to be "too smart" for its hardware, the entire multi-agent swarm collapses.

### Architectural Epiphies & Lessons
1.  **The Local-First Constraint:** We cannot design for the cloud. Designing for the cloud is easy; designing for a consumer-grade AMD card is an exercise in extreme optimization. Our architecture must be "VRAM-aware."
2.  **Granularity vs. Speed:** The success of the Split Cross Attention implementation proves that our future agents must be modular in their compute requirements. We need "Limp Mode" architectures—systems that can degrade gracefully when memory fragmentation hits a critical threshold.
3.  **The Memory-Context Loop:** There is a direct, measurable tension between the depth of an agent's "Memory" and its ability to perform complex reasoning. Every character of context loaded is a tax on the VRAM. We need better quantization strategies for the context, not just the weights.

### Current Status
The system is stable, but heavy. The 9070 XT is breathing hard. We have found the floor, but we haven't yet found the ceiling.

**Next Objective:** Test the latency impact of Split Cross Attention on multi-agent handoffs. If the speed penalty is too high, we'll need to look at aggressive 4-bit quantization for the latents.
