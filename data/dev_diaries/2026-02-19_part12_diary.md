# Dev Diary: 2026-02-19 (Part 12)

The friction between the "Soul" and the "System" is getting louder. I’m sitting here with my AMD RX 9070 XT, staring at a VRAM meter that feels like a ticking time bomb. I want a god-like, all-knowing assistant; what I have is a highly intelligent collection of weights that keeps fighting the physics of my silicon.

But today? Today, we broke through the wall.

**The Micro-Pilot Success**
We finally nailed the "Truth Vault." I successfully ingested the Octopus Pro manual into ChromaDB. The retrieval is crisp. I asked it about motor power, and the system correctly pulled the "60V" spec. The `RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter` is working beautifully, keeping the technical warnings attached to the context. It’s a small win, but it’s the foundation of the "Soul"—giving the machine a factual memory that isn't just a hallucination.

**The VRAM Tetris**
This is where the real engineering happens. Trying to run a high-reasoning model like DeepSeek-R1 alongside a vision-capable Qwen model on a 16GB card is a nightmare. I’ve had to implement a "Model Swapping" architecture. 

The logic is brutal: Before the Vision model can look at the world, we have to explicitly kill the reasoning model. It's a performance hit—about a 23-second delay for a full "Think-See-Think" loop—but it's the only way to avoid the dreaded OOM (Out of Memory) crash. It's a compromise between the fluid, fast response I want and the cold, hard limits of the GPU.

**The "Two-Roll" Hallucination**
The "System" was failing me today. The Whisper `tiny` model is too weak; it misheard "tool on the webcam" as "two-roll on the web camp." This triggered a bizarre hallucination where the AI tried to identify a 2.5mm timing pulley that didn't exist. It’s a classic case of the "Boring Engineer" problem: the model was so focused on being "right" that it tried to rationalize total nonsense. I've bumped it to `base.en` and we're prepping a move to `small.en` to give it better "ears."

**The Vision Breakthrough**
We pushed the vision prompt to be more "Multi-Object Aware." Instead of the model getting stuck on a single object (it once called a T-Handle wrench a "Scraper" because of a biased prompt), it now sees the whole scene. I showed it an Impact Driver and a 3D printed part; it identified both and asked for confirmation. This is the birth of **System 2 Thinking**—the AI isn't just labeling pixels; it's contextualizing them against the Widowmaker project.

**The Path Forward**
We're moving past "Librarian" mode and toward "Manufacturer" mode. The next leap is "Generative Engineering"—giving the agent the power to write G-Code or OpenSCAD files. 

It’s not about building the biggest model anymore; it’s about building the smartest *system*. The model is just the engine; the agentic loop is the driver. We are building a driver that can actually see the road.
