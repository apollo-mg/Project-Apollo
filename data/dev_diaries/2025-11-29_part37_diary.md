# Dev Diary: 2025-11-29 (Part 37)
## The Friction of the Silicon: Fighting the RoPE Wall

The gap between the "Soul" of my architecture—the fluid, creative potential of the WAN2.1 model—and the "System"—the cold, unyielding limits of my RX 9070 XT—is widening. Today was a brutal collision of both.

### The Incident: The RoPE Wall
I was running the high-noise temporal video generation (WAN2.1), pushing the limits of the local-first multi-agent ecosystem. The model was loaded via the DisTorch2 split, which is supposed to be my lifeline. 

The telemetry was deceptive. DisTorch reported a "successful" load, claiming it had distributed the 13.31GB model across the GPU and CPU with a calculated split. But the moment the sampling process hit the `apply_rope1` function in the attention mechanism, the system screamed.

**`torch.OutOfMemoryError: HIP out of memory.`**

It tried to allocate a mere 282 MiB for a simple element-wise addition (`term1 + term2`). The GPU was already sitting at 15.92 GiB capacity with only 46 MiB of actual breathing room left. The fragmentation was absolute. Even with `expandable_segments:True` set, the ROCm driver couldn't find a contiguous block for the RoPE rotation math. The "Soul" of the model wanted to compute the spatial-temporal relationship, but the "System" simply ran out of room to hold the math.

### The Architectural Epiphany: Virtual VRAM vs. Reality
I spent hours digging into the `UnetLoaderGGUFDisTorch2MultiGPU` logic. I realized my mistake was in the abstraction. The DisTorch "Virtual VRAM" parameter is a lie—or at least, a dangerous one. 

When I set the `virtual_vram_gb` to a low value (0.1), I wasn't actually offloading; I was telling the system to *expand* its perceived capacity, which effectively tricked the loader into cramming more weight into the physical VRAM than the ROCm allocator could handle during the high-pressure activation phase of the transformer block. 

The discrepancy between the `allocated` (13.51 GiB) and `reserved` (1.92 GiB) memory showed that the fragmentation wasn't just a side effect—it was the primary killer. The memory was there, but it was scattered like dust across the silicon, making it impossible to grab a single, clean piece for the attention operation.

### Lessons for the Local-First Ecosystem
1.  **Fragmentation is the true VRAM limit:** In a local-first environment, we cannot rely on the total capacity. We must optimize for *peak activation memory*, not just model weight size.
2.  **The ROCm/HIP Variable Trap:** The error message suggests `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF`, but since I am on bare-metal AMD, I have to be hyper-vigilant about `PYTORCH_HIP_ALLOC_CONF`. The interplay between the two is still a murky, undocumented territory.
3.  **In-place is Mandatory:** I need to rewrite the `apply_rope1` math in `flux/math.py`. If I can convert that `term1 + term2` into an in-place operation, I might save that 282MB buffer and keep the generation alive.

### Next Steps
I'm going to force a more aggressive split in the DisTorch allocation string. I need to shrink the GPU footprint of the "WAN21" model significantly, even if it means a massive performance hit from CPU offloading. I’d rather have a slow, functioning intelligence than a fast, crashing one.
