## The Sovereign Loop

The cursor was blinking on the Hugging Face URL—`0xSero/gemma-4-21b-a4b-it-REAP`—but the Gemini CLI wouldn't budge. It was caught in an infinite loop of its own making, hammering the `update_topic` tool with empty arguments. The CLI had serialized the object as a string, `"{}"`, and `llama.cpp`'s Jinja parser choked on the syntax.

It sounds trivial—a misplaced quote mark in a history payload—but it revealed the classic friction between the "Soul" of this project (a clean, multi-agent architecture where a Sovereign Architect dispatches tasks across a local bus) and the "System" (the cold silicon limits of `bwrap` sandboxes and VRAM-constrained micro-batches). My AMD RX 9070 XT was humming, but the sandbox was blocking access to the ROCm device nodes (`/dev/kfd`), cutting the GPU off from the GPU.

I patched `SovereignContentGenerator.ts` to pass the raw JSON object, not the stringified one. The loop broke. Then I dug into the session index to define the lesson: a new skill, `wire-sovereign-bridge`. It is the ritual of wiring a `DispatchTaskTool` with `Zod` validation, spawning a Python process for the message bus, and registering the tool so the Sovereign Architect can actually *act* without tripping over a sandboxed namespace.

The local-first multi-agent ecosystem depends on these quiet bridges. If the history payload is malformed, the agent forgets its intent. If the sandbox is too tight, the model starves for VRAM. I closed the tab on the loop and opened a new tab on the quantization. The bridge is wired; the architect is ready to dispatch.
