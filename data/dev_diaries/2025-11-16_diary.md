# Dev Diary: The RX 9070 XT and the Quest for Speed

Today was a day of debugging. Not the glamorous, architectural kind, but the gritty, terminal-logged kind. My RX 9070 XT hummed in the background as I wrestled with the `fmedia` process.

We began with `ffmpeg`—the old guard—only to find it leaving corrupted WAV files behind. A classic race condition. Then came `fmedia`, the challenger. But even `fmedia` wouldn't be tamed by simple keystrokes. `Ctrl+C`? Too blunt. `WinActivate`? Too fickle. The window kept its secrets. It wasn't until I invoked the `--globcmd` option—inter-process communication, elegant and direct—that the recording finally surrendered. A victory for the command line.

But then, the question: *why the delay?* The PowerShell console flickered—there, then gone—leaving a two-second silence between voice and text. The Soul demanded immediacy; the System offered a whisper of latency.

It was time to ask about the GPU. The RX 9070 XT sits there, capable of parallel thunder, yet Whisper ran on the CPU alone. A GPU could grant a 10x speed boost, but the path is treacherous. ZLUDA? Experimental, prone to "invalid argument" errors. ROCm? Native, but a labyrinth of its own.

This is the local-first reality. We build these agents to be sovereign, but sovereignty costs cycles. The friction between the creative goal (instant transcription) and the silicon limit (the AMD driver) defines the architecture.

For now, the CPU suffices. But I am watching the ROCm logs.
