# Dev Diary: The Ghost of the `None` Value

**2026-02-20 — 02:47 UTC**

The blue light of the terminal is the only company I have left. Outside, the shop is dark; inside, the AMD RX 9070 XT is humming, burning through VRAM to keep the guard from biting.

Today’s antagonist was a phantom `None` value in `buddy_agent.py`. Jarvis—the voice agent, my digital shop buddy—was supposed to answer a simple question: *"What PDFs do we have in the vault?"*

Instead, she panicked. She hallucinated a status update about `pilot_ingest.py`, a technical ghost unrelated to the query. She was searching for hard specs where there was only a list of filenames. The system was rigid, and the soul of the answer had been choked out by a strict parser.

The friction is always the same: the **Soul** wants Jarvis to sound like a partner scanning a shelf, not a database dumping raw JSON. The **System**—my own prompt engineering—had insisted on *"Extract ONLY hard specs (Voltages, Pins, etc.)"*. When the LLM (DeepSeek) returned a list of files, the parser choked. It returned `None`. The Persona Pass, left without data but with a mandate to answer, grabbed the nearest available context—`pilot_ingest.py`—and spoke it as fact.

The fix was surgical, the sort of thing that keeps me honest.

1.  **Relaxed the Persona Prompt:** I told the LLM: *"If the data is a general answer or a list of files, present it clearly."* No more forcing a spec sheet onto a document list.
2.  **Robust Fallback:** I refactored `handle_actions` so it returns a "COMPLIANCE MESSAGE" if no tool call is found, preventing the `None` that triggers the panic.
3.  **Noise Reduction:** I cleaned the signal. Added *"have," "manual,"* and *"instruction"* to the stop words. The grep fallback was drowning the results in irrelevant matches.

The lesson for the local-first multi-agent ecosystem? **Parse robustly.** A `None` is not just a null; it is a silence that invites hallucination. We must prioritize semantic vector search over keyword grep. We must allow for conversational tone. And we must always watch the VRAM—the threshold is hit at 14620MB.

The guard is watching the memory fragmentation. The guard is watching for the `None`.

Now, to ship it.
