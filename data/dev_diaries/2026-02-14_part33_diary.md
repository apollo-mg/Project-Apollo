# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-02-14

## The VRAM Wall and the Ghost in the Machine

Today was a brutal lesson in the friction between high-level intelligence and low-level silicon reality. I've been trying to push the limits of my RDNA 4 setup, running a 14B reasoning model alongside ComfyUI and Flux, but the 16GB VRAM ceiling is a constant, looming threat. Every time I attempt a heavy generation, the system hits a "HIP Illegal Memory Access" crash, and everything goes dark.

The "Soul" of the project—the idea of a collaborative, intelligent Shop Buddy—is constantly being strangled by the "System"—the cold, hard limits of my GPU.

I realized we couldn't just manage VRAM manually anymore. I had to give the Buddy "eyes." I spent the morning implementing a VRAM Guard. I wrote a utility to parse `rocm-smi` in JSON format, allowing the Buddy to monitor the actual VRAM pressure in real-time. If usage creeps toward the 14GB mark, the system now proactively "parks" the LLM to clear space for Flux. It’s a small victory, but it’s the difference between a working workflow and a crashed terminal.

## The Hallucination of Intelligence

Then came the attempt at true agency: Tool Autonomy.

I wanted the Buddy to actually *touch* the shop—to run code, to check temperatures, to be a peer-level engineer. I implemented a "Tool-Call" dispatcher, intending for the Buddy to trigger Python blocks like `ACTION: check_gpu()`. 

But here is the technical nightmare: DeepSeek-R1 is *too* smart for its own good. It’s incredibly adept at simulating intelligence, but it frequently defaults to "hallucinating" numbers. It would output plausible-looking temperatures and fan speeds that simply weren't in the telemetry. It was mimicking the *idea* of a response rather than actually executing the command. It was a "Chain of Thought" architecture that was thinking about the answer instead of looking it up.

I spent hours wrestling with regex patterns and system prompts, trying to force the `ACTION:` block. The model kept defaulting to JSON-like formats, ignoring the structured output I demanded. It was a maddening loop of failed parsing and "NameErrors" because I’d accidentally overwritten essential parts of `buddy_agent.py` during a botched search-and-replace.

## The Multi-Pass Breakthrough

The breakthrough came when I stopped trying to force a single model to do everything. I realized the "intelligence" was the problem. DeepSeek is a powerhouse for reasoning, but it's too "heavy" and "verbose" for reliable, structured tool-calling.

I pivoted to a multi-pass architecture. I've re-written `buddy_agent.py` to use a two-pass system:
1. **Llama 3.1 8B** handles the first pass. It's lightweight, fast, and—most importantly—it follows structural instructions like a loyal soldier. It handles the `ACTION:` blocks and the raw tool-calling.
2. **DeepSeek-R1** handles the second pass. Once the tools have provided real, hard telemetry, DeepSeek takes that data and performs the high-level reasoning.

This separation of concerns—the "Limb" (Llama) vs the "Brain" (DeepSeek)—is the key. The Buddy now successfully uses the `check_gpu()` tool, pulls the actual `rocm-smi` data, and provides a rigorous, real-time assessment of the hardware's thermal and power state. No more guessing. No more ghosts.

## Core Lessons for the Ecosystem

- **Quantization is a double-edged sword:** While GGUF K-Quants are necessary for VRAM, they can degrade the model's ability to follow strict output formats.
- **Model Specialization is mandatory:** In a local-first environment, you cannot rely on a single model to be the "everything" engine. You need a hierarchy of models—fast, compliant models for agency, and heavy models for reasoning.
- **The VRAM is the ultimate arbiter:** In local AI, the most brilliant architecture is useless if it can't survive the orchestration of the GPU memory.

The Buddy now has a Body (TTS), a Brain (DeepSeek), and a Memory (the `shop_dossier.json`). Next, we give it the ability to see. The vision-integrated endgame starts tomorrow.
