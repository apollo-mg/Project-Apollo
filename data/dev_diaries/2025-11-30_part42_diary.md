Dev Diary: 2025-11-30 (Part 42 of 43)

The RX 9070 XT is a beast, but today it growled back at the ComfyUI sampler.

I was pushing a large diffusion model through the sampler, lowvram mode engaged, when the system balked. A HIP error: an illegal memory access. Right at the cast_to call, where the weight crosses from GPU to CPU.

The logs told the tale: 9.3GB loaded into that ~16GB of free VRAM, fully loaded, then—crash. Not during the heavy lifting of the blocks, but during the delicate dance of casting the bias weight.

The friction is familiar: the Soul wants the full resolution of the diffusion model to flow; the System (the ROCm driver, the VRAM pressure, the fragmentation) demands respect for the address space.

Is it the driver? The fragmentation from aggressive offloading? Or simply that I asked too much of a single pass for this batch?

I'm leaning toward the driver. I'll try AMD_SERIALIZE_KERNEL=3 to serialize the kernel and see if the device-side assertions (TORCH_USE_HIP_DSA) reveal the leak.

For the local-first multi-agent ecosystem, this is a reminder: every agent needs reliable, reproducible execution. If a simple weight cast can trigger an illegal address, then my multi-agent swarm needs either more headroom in VRAM or smarter, gentler batching.
