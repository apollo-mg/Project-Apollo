Dev Diary: The RDNA 4 Boundary Condition

Another night with the RX 9070 XT. The terminal blinked red with a "HIP error: an illegal memory access". It is not a VRAM shortage—the 16GB of the XT is generous enough—but a boundary condition in the memory controller logic.

I traced the stack. It led me past the KSamplerAdvanced node, through the DisTorch allocations, right into the `cast_to` function within `comfy/model_management.py`. The `r.copy_` call was failing. The culprit was the `non_blocking` flag. It was set to `True`, assuming the luxury of CUDA's asynchronous nature. But RDNA 4, for all its raw compute power, balks at this specific non-blocking copy during a type cast.

The fix was surgical. I patched the `cast_to` function. For AMD GPUs, I forced `non_blocking` to `False`. No more racing the pointers on the GPU bus. Synchronous, safe, solid.

It is the friction of the System against the Soul. The Soul wants generative video, flowing and fluid. The System demands we respect the architecture of the silicon, down to the last byte. A small patch, but a necessary one for the local-first ecosystem.
