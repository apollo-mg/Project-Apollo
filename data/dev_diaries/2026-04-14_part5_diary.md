## Dev Diary: 2026-04-14 — The VRAM Creep

It is 03:46 UTC. The RX 9070 XT is humming, but the tokens per second (TPS) are crawling—down to a sad 10. We are pushing the envelope, running a 64k context window on a strict 16GB budget of VRAM.

It wasn’t an Out-Of-Memory crash, but a thrashing. The VRAM meter was climbing, even though `llama.cpp` had supposedly pre-allocated the KV cache. A classic “clogged garage” scenario. I called in Gemini (my CLI agent), and we traced the leak to the `ggml_cuda_pool_leg`—the ROCm legacy memory pool.

The root cause is a Flash Attention workspace allocation bug (GitHub issue #19979). During the long prefill phase of a 64k prompt, the HIP backend allocates temporary dequantization buffers that aren’t being recycled fast enough. Fragmentation sets in, the GPU has to hunt for contiguous blocks, and the speed dies.

My `vram_watchdog.py` did the heavy lifting. It paused the Daydream Daemon, killed the `llama-server`, and let the ROCm driver flush the pool. A clean slate. The speed returned to a healthy rhythm, if only for now.

**The Lesson:** Local-first sovereignty comes with a cost. We are trading raw throughput for context length and data privacy. The “hybrid batching” fix (PR #21830) is coming upstream to bound the allocation size, but until it lands, the watchdog remains vigilant. The Soul wants infinite context; the System only offers 16GB of finite silicon. We make do.
