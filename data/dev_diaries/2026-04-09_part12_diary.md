### The Daydreamer’s Purity

The RX 9070 XT hummed all evening, a low, steady thrum under my desk. The SDMA cache stress test finally settled: `--cache-ram 2048` holds steady, the `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=1` environment variable locked in tight. The PCIe bus breathes. But the real victory wasn’t the hardware—it was the architecture.

I had almost made the mistake. I almost gave the Daydreamer a web browser. You know the temptation: let the daemon just fetch the latest ROCm patch notes from GitHub, right? Wrong. The Daydreamer is Apollo’s Default Mode Network. It is the neural reflection, the closed-eye synthesis of what already lives in the ChromaDB. If it goes browsing the web, it stops being a synthesizer and becomes a distracted Wikipedia crawler.

The lesson struck me hard, as it usually does at 23:00 hours. Separation of concerns. The Daydreamer stays air-gapped, pure, hunting for hidden connections in the logs I’ve already scraped. If it finds a gap in the knowledge, it doesn’t fill it itself—it flags it. It calls for the Librarian.

And the Librarian is a different beast. A small, cheap script running on that Pi 5, chewing on the Bonsai-8B like a hungry terrier. Three tokens per second. Who cares? It’s not writing an essay; it’s harvesting facts. It will grind through a whitepaper while I sleep, verify the specs against the deepseek-r1:14b, and drop the JSON into the vault before breakfast.

That is the poetry of local-first AI. The cheap, slow hardware does the heavy lifting of filtering. The expensive, fast VRAM does the reasoning. Distributed, sovereign, and finally—quietly—stable.
