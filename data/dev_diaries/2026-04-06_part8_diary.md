## Dev Diary: 2026-04-06 (Part 8)

The RX 9070 XT is finally purring.

It has been a session of VRAM Tetris. The Wan 2.2 video model refused to run on the 16GB RDNA 4 silicon, throwing the dreaded `HIP illegal memory access` errors in the attention layers. The ROCm 7.1 driver was not wrong, just finicky about the stride of the tensors.

I stopped fighting the Flash Attention backend and enforced `.contiguous()` on the tensors before the `CausalConv3d` forward pass. I had to clamp the `tile_size` to 256 and drop the `--reserve-vram` to 0.1 to keep the model from OOMing. It was a micro-optimization, but on the 16GB card, every fragment counts.

This is the friction of the local-first life. The **Soul** of Project Apollo demands sovereignty—running the heavy video generation on the metal without cloud crutches. But the **System** demands respect for the hardware limits and the quirks of the RDNA 4 math kernels.

**The Lesson:** The `.contiguous()` call is not just a function; it is a contract with the silicon. The architecture is not just about the models we load, but how we lay them down in memory.

**Next:** Now that Wan 2.2 is stable, I can trust the `--use-split-cross-attention` flag for the long haul. Time to move to Part 9.
