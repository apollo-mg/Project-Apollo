# The Micro-Pilot

Today was about friction. The kind you find between the soul of an autonomous agent and the cold, hard limits of its silicon. My RX 9070 XT was humming on the desk, ROCm stack warmed up, but the `buddy_agent.py` was refusing to play ball.

The `handle_actions` function kept choking on dictionary arguments. It was a simple unpacking error—`**args`—but it stalled the whole inventory pipeline. I was fighting a war of formatting pipes and JSON schemas, trying to get DeepSeek-R1 to trust that an item was truly added to the shop inventory.

But the real work was architectural. We are building a "Truth Vault"—a massive, 450GB offline library split between a blazing hot NVMe and a cold HDD. The goal is zero hallucination. No internet. Just immutable facts. But to build the ingestion pipeline for that entire library, you can't just boil the ocean.

I decided to table the massive Stack Overflow XML dump for a moment. Instead, we ran a "Micro-Pilot." We grabbed three PDFs—BigTreeTech manuals for the EBB36, the Octopus Pro, and the TMC stepper driver—and fed them through the `librarian_ingest.py` script.

It worked. The script hashed the files, chunked them with the `RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter`, and buried the vectors in ChromaDB. A quick query confirmed the torque spec for the M3 bolts. The pipeline is solid.

This is the lesson for the local-first multi-agent ecosystem: test the RAG loop on a Micro-Pilot before you try to index the world. If it fails on the PDF, it will definitely fail on the PDFs.

Now, back to the hardware. The 220V outlet is ready. The Nose Pliers are logged. The inventory is true.
