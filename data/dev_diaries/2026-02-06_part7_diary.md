# Dev Diary: 2026-02-06 (Part 7)

## The Ghost in the Machine: Solving the 48Hz Mystery

Today was one of those days where the "System" and the "Soul" finally stopped fighting and started talking to each other. 

I spent most of the session wrestling with the resonance data from the Y-axis. My X-axis is rock solid—80Hz, tight, crisp. But the Y-axis? It was hitting a wall at 48Hz. It felt like the machine was hitting an invisible barrier, a physical limitation that no amount of software tuning could bypass.

I ran a series of Shake&Tune tests to see if it was the belts. Using the `COMPARE_BELTS_RESPONSES` macro, I was looking for a mismatch in the diagonal sweeps. The result was actually... beautiful. The similarity was 98.9%. The belt tensions were practically identical. The "ghost" wasn't in the belts.

The 48Hz resonance showed up in both diagonals equally. That was the epiphany. It wasn't matter which belt was pulling; the entire Y-axis assembly was oscillating like a tuning fork. The culprit? The printed XY joiners. My "Soul" (the custom, lightweight CF-gantry vision) was being betrayed by the "System" (the physical compliance of the plastic mounts). The CF tube is incredibly stiff, but it was essentially "bouncing" on the plastic joiners. It was a structural bottleneck that made all my high-speed dreams impossible until I swapped them for metal.

## The Pivot to Geometry

Once I realized the hardware was the bottleneck, I pivoted to the next big architectural hurdle: the Z-tilt. 

The geometry of the bed is a work of art—a true Tri-Z free-floating design using GE5C spherical bearings and ball-on-dowel mounts. It's designed to let the bed expand and contract without stressing the Z-actuators, which is a level of mechanical honesty most hobbyists never touch. But that elegance comes with a high computational cost. To calibrate the `z_tilt` accurately, I need to map the exact coordinates of those three kinematic supports.

I've decided to pause the software calibration for a moment. I'm going to physically modify the hardware first—cutting down the lead screws to gain more Z-travel. There's no point in mapping a geometry that's about to shift.

## Technical Lessons & Next Steps

- **The "Joint" Signature:** I learned that resonance peaks aren't just about mass; they are about the "damping" of the joint. The wide, fuzzy peaks in the Y-axis spectrogram were the smoking gun for the plastic compliance.
- **Kinematic Mapping:** The complexity of mapping a 3-point floating bed is significant. The `z_positions` (the actual pivot points) and the `points` (where the probe lands) are interdependent. I'll need to measure the physical locations of the GE5C bearings relative to the nozzle to get the math right.
- **Immediate Action:** 
    1. Cut lead screws (Angle grinder + chamfer tool).
    2. Re-measure the new physical geometry.
    3. Map the `z_tilt` with the new coordinates.
    4. Apply the refined Shaper values (80Hz X / 48.2Hz Y) now that the mechanical-stiffness-per-mass ratio is understood.

The machine is finally becoming a coherent entity. The friction between my ambitious architecture and the limitations of my current components is finally being smoothed out. The "Soul" is winning.
