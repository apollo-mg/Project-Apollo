## The Silicon Struggle: VRAM, Nightlies, and the Ghost of ROCm

Today was a brutal exercise in fighting the friction between high-level software intent and the messy reality of bleeding-edge hardware drivers. We started the session in a state of total environmental collapse. 

### 1. The Environment Contamination
The initial investigation into `venv_rocm711` revealed a nightmare scenario: the environment was "contaminated" with NVIDIA-specific packages (`nvidia-cub`, `las-cu12`). It was a ghost of a CUDA install haunting an AMD-intended space. The mismatch between the user's reported ROCm 7.1.1 and the actual file structure caused a massive divergence in my internal logic. 

I had to hunt for the "true" environment. After several failed attempts to find `venv_711`, I realized the system was in a state of flux—likely due to a recent reboot that had corrupted the symlinks or failed to mount the drive where the original environment lived.

### 2. The Nightly Leap
The most significant architectural epiphany of the day: **The "Nightly" pivot.** 

While we were hunting for the specific 7.1.1/2.9.1 snapshot, the installation of the latest ROCm nightlies pulled a much newer version: **Torch 2.10.0 + ROCm 7.10**. 

At first, this looked like a mistake, but the technical reality is that the `gfx120X` (RDNA 4) support is moving so fast that the "stable" versions are actually behind the bleeding edge. I made the executive decision to embrace the nightly build. It’s unstable, it’s experimental, but it’s the only way to unlock the true power of the RX 9070 XT.

### 3. The Dependency Avalanche
Once the core Torch/ROCm engine was stabilized, the "Soul" of the system—the creative potential of ComfyUI—was still missing its limbs. The server would boot, but the custom nodes (Manager, WanVideo, etc.) were all dead in the water due to missing Python modules (`toml`, `ftfy`, `rich`, `accelerate`).

I had to fight through a series of `pip` failures. The `sageattention` build failed because of an isolated build environment that couldn't see the newly installed Torch. I had to bypass this with `--no-build-isolation`, a classic "hammer-to-the-nail" move that worked.

## Core Lessons & Architectural Impact

1.  **The Fallacy of "Version Matching":** In the world of RDNA 4 and early-adopter ROCm, targeting a specific version number is a trap. The nightly branches are the real frontier. We must design our deployment scripts to be "version-agnostic" and instead focus on the architecture-specific driver requirements.
2.  **The Fragility of Virtual Envs:** A single corrupted `pip` install can poison an entire environment with CUDA dependencies. We need more robust "nuke-and-rebuild" automation for our local-first multi-agent ecosystems.
3.  **The Dependency Loop:** The interaction between custom nodes and the base environment is highly volatile. As we scale the number of agents/nodes, the dependency management becomes a primary bottleneck, not an afterthought.
