# Dev Diary: 2026-02-14 (Part 11)

## The Silicon Struggle: Broken Environments and Ghost Mounts

Today was a masterclass in why building local-first AI isn't just about the model weights—it's about the absolute nightmare of the underlying plumbing. I spent the first hour fighting a ghost. My ComfyUI launch script was pointing to a virtual environment (`venv_711`) that apparently didn't exist anymore, or at least wasn't visible to the shell. 

I had to dive into the shell script, realize the symlinks or the pathing was botched, and redirect it to `venv_rocm711`. It's a classic "System" failure. I have the AMD RX 9070 XT sitting there, ready to crunch numbers, but if the Python environment can't even find `torch` or `typing_extensions`, the most powerful GPU in the room is just a very expensive paperweight.

## The VRAM/Alignment Paradox

Once I got the environment stabilized, I hit the real bottleneck: the VAE. 

The `hipErrorIllegalAddress` error is the bane of my existence on ROCm. It’s a memory access violation that happens deep within the convolution layers during the decoding phase. I thought I’d mitigated it by forcing a `.clone().contiguous()` on the input tensors, but the "Soul" of the model—the actual creative reconstruction—was still hitting a wall of "System" instability. It turns out the memory layout was still messy before it even hit the convolution. It's a reminder that even with 16GB of VRAM, the way we manage memory fragmentation on RDNA 4 architectures is still incredibly finicky.

## Unlocking the "Uncensored" Potential

Then came the philosophical pivot. I started testing the Flux.1 models. The base `schnell` variant is fast, sure, but it's heavily safety-aligned. It’s "polite." If you want it to go off the rails—to capture raw human anatomy or more visceral, uncensored concepts—the base model resists you. 

It’s a fascinating tension. The model's "intelligence" is actually a form of restraint. But the beauty of the local-first movement is that we aren't beholden to a corporate API's filter. I found that by using LoRAs (Low-Rank Adaptations), I can surgically inject new behaviors. I integrated the `NSFW_master.safetensors` LoRA into the workflow, essentially teaching the model a new vocabulary of concept-specific weights without needing to re-train the whole damn thing. 

## The Storage Abyss

Just when I thought I was in the clear, the hardware fought back. The drive containing my 2TB of models—the very lifeblood of this setup—suddenly vanished from the system. 

The `lsblk` output was a mess of orphaned mount points and "dirty" NTFS partitions. It looks like a stale directory (`/media/mark/TG 2TB`) was preventing the actual partition (`nvme1n1p2`) from mounting correctly. This is the reality of local AI: you aren't just a developer; you're a sysadmin, a storage engineer, and a driver wrangler. 

My plan for the next hour is a surgical strike on the file system:
1. Run `ntfsfix` to clear the dirty bit on the NVMe.
2. Purge the stale, root-owned mount directories.
3. Re-mount the drive and patch the `extra_model_paths.yaml` to match the new mount point.

It’s messy, it’s frustrating, and it’s exactly why I love this. We aren't just calling an API; we are building a living, breathing machine in our own living rooms.
