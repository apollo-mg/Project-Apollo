**The RDNA 4 Ghost**

The RX 9070 XT has a ghost. Not a VRAM shortage, but a striding ghost in the quantization kernel. The illegal memory access returned during a simple dequantize, while DisTorch managed the VRAM split between GPU and CPU. The soul wanted the video rendered; the system said the tensor was not contiguous.

We traced the stack to `dequantize_blocks_Q6_K`. `torch.split` had given us views—non-contiguous slices of the GGUF block. `scales`, `d`, `ql`, `qh`. When the cast kernel tried to bite them without a `.clone()`, the RDNA 4 driver balked. It is a quirk of the silicon: a cast on a strided view requires care. We added the clones. Four of them. Suddenly, the memory access was legal.

The lesson is structural. In our local-first multi-agent ecosystem, where VRAM is a finite currency and quantization is the tax we pay for mobility, we cannot assume contiguity. We must pay the price of a clone to ensure the kernel's peace. The soul wants to run the model; the system demands the memory be linear.

We are building a stack that respects the hardware. Not a cloud stack, not a data center of infinite RAM, but a desktop PC with an AMD card and a specific, finicky driver. We are learning the names of the functions. We are cloning the tensors.
