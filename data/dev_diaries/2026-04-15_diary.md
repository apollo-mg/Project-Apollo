## 2026-04-15: The OOM Killer Strikes

The SIGKILL hit my terminal hard today. I was pushing a massive 50,851 token context window through my AMD RX 9070 XT, aiming for a clean run on the local `llama-server`. I had killed the `vram_watchdog.py` earlier to free up the pool, trusting the hardware to hold the line. But the Linux kernel didn't hesitate.

When the script returned `Killed`, it wasn't a timeout. It was the Out-Of-Memory (OOM) killer. The `--mlock` flag on my start script had promised the kernel not to page to swap space, so when the KV cache swelled beyond the 16GB limit, the OS made a choice to preserve the system over the session. It assassinated the process with Signal 9.

Gemini and I weighed the options quickly. I could have removed the memory lock, risking a freeze, or truncated the history, losing narrative continuity. Instead, we chose the architectural path of least resistance: split the chats. By chunking the input into smaller files before they hit the server, we preserve the context without choking the silicon or stressing the quantized weights of the `q8_0` model.

It's a small friction, but these local-first multi-agent ecosystems live in the margins. We want the Soul of the diary to flow without the System gasping for air.
