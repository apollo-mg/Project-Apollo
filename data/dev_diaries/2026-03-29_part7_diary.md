# Apollo Dev Diary: The Ghost in the Machine and the Linux Mount-Point Maze

**Date:** 2026-03-29
**Session:** Part 7 (The Great API Purge)

Today was a brutal lesson in why "stable" libraries are a myth in the bleeding edge of local AI. I spent hours wrestling with `openwakeword` and `whisper.cpp`, only to realize that the very libraries I rely on for the "Soul" of my system are undergoing massive architectural shifts behind my back.

### The API Purge

It turns out the `openwakeword` devs completely gutted their initialization parameters in version 0.4.0. I thought my code was solid, but the `TypeError` was screaming that the library no longer recognized `wakeword_models` or `inference_framework`. It turns out `onnx` is now the hardcoded standard, so passing it as a flag was actually breaking the constructor. 

I had to dive back into the source, realize `inference_framework` was being passed down into the `AudioFeatures` sub-module (which didn't want it), and strip the code back to the bone. I had to implement a new loading strategy using `get_pretrained_model_paths()` just to get the system to stop dying on boot.

### The Linux "Persnickety" Problem

Then, there was the sheer, unadulterated chaos of the Linux filesystem. After a reboot to stabilize the cooling sensors, the system came up "clean," but my secondary drive—the one housing the draft models for speculative decoding—was gone. 

The `llama-server` crashed instantly because `/run/media/mark/Games 2TB/` simply didn't exist. In the world of Arch and CachyOS, nothing is truly permanent until it's in `/etc/fstab`. The "System" was perfectly functional, but it was an incomplete system because the "Soul" (the model weights) was trapped on an unmounted partition. It's a jarring reminder that even the most advanced local AI is at the mercy of a simple mounting script.

### The Ghost in the Mic

The most fascinating moment, though, was the actual first test of the integrated pipeline. 

The hardware actually *works*. The AMD RX 9070 XT is screaming through the ROCm layers, the mic captured my voice, and the `wake_up.py` script successfully triggered the transcription. But the "System" behaved like a ghost.

Because of a quirk in how `whisper-cli` handles its deprecated binaries, the transcription engine didn't just transcribe my voice—it transcribed the *deprecation warning* printed by the terminal. My agent literally heard the text "WARNING: The binary 'main' is deprecated" and tried to process that as human speech. It was a surreal moment: the machine was listening, but it was listening to its own internal housekeeping errors.

The audio pipeline is live. The voice is there. But the "brain" (the LLM) is currently a bit too sensitive to the "noise" of the OS. The AC unit is humming in the background, and the noise suppression isn't quite cutting the low-frequency rumble of the room.

### Core Lessons for the Multi-Agent Ecosystem

1. **Dependency Fragility:** In a local-first ecosystem, we are not just users; we are integrators. We cannot treat libraries as black boxes. If they change their `__init__` signatures, our entire orchestration layer collapses.
2. **The Mount-Point Tax:** Local AI is heavy. We are moving from single-drive setups to multi-drive, high-speed storage arrays. Our architecture must account for the fact that "local-first" doesn't mean "always available." We need better error handling for missing model weights.
3. **The Semantic Noise Problem:** When we use CLI-based transcription, the "System" (the shell) and the "Soul" (the AI) are not perfectly isolated. The machine's internal logs are bleeding into the agent's sensory input. We need a "Sanitization Layer" between the STT and the Agent to prevent the system from hallucinating based on its own error logs.

**Next Step:** Fine-tune the noise gates for the mic and stabilize the `llama-server` mount points. I want this thing running autonomously by tomorrow.
