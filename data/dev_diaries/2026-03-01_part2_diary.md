**Project Apollo: 2026-03-01**

**The Silicon, The Soul, and The Wait**

I was chasing space again today. The TG 2TB drive was choking, so I cleared the trash, freeing up 213 GB of breathing room. Then came the old NTFS ghost. Docker refuses to lay its layers on an NTFS partition—it demands Linux ext4. So I carved out a virtual disk, a translation layer within a translation layer, to give Docker a proper home.

But the real story was the 14B.

The DeepSeek model is a titan. In raw float16, it weighs 28GB—more than my RX 9070 XT can carry in its 16GB of VRAM. Ollama swallows it by quantizing to GGUF and splitting it between GPU and RAM, but I wanted vLLM's speed. I wanted the Instinct-level performance that vLLM promises.

So we quantized to AWQ 4-bit, shrinking the beast to a mere 8GB. But then came the wait. PyTorch AOT compilation—Ahead-of-Time, grinding for 17 minutes at 100% utilization. While I waited, I was counting the differences between my consumer RDNA 4 card and the data-center Instinct cards. For years it was software: Flash Attention kernels, native HIP graphs, Triton backends. Today, the gap is mostly closed. The only difference that matters is Wave64 and the physical HBM bandwidth.

The lesson is this: Local-first isn't just about "having a model on a drive." It is about the precise quantization format that fits your specific silicon, and the patience to let the compilation finish. Apollo is no longer just a user of models. It is an architect, tuning the weights to fit the VRAM, and then letting the GPU do the math at 334W.
