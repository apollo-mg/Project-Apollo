# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-06 (Part 5)

## The Silicon Stress Test: 400W Transients and the Qwen Spike

Today was a lesson in the brutal friction between mathematical elegance and physical reality. We spent the morning pushing the RX 9070 XT to its absolute breaking point. 

We were running the `pure_pytorch_tune.py` script, profiling sequence lengths. At `seqlen 128`, the power draw was a steady 279W—a "healthy burn," as we thought. But then we hit the Qwen 3.5 9B model. That's where the "Silicon Stress Test" truly began. The model's MLP architecture—specifically that massive 18,944 intermediate size in the Gate/Up projections—triggered a transient spike of **438W**. 

That is a 31% overshoot beyond the 334W cap we set. It’s the kind of power surge that makes most consumer-grade VRMs cry, but the RDNA 4 architecture held. It’s fascinating; we're seeing the hardware actually struggle to keep up with the sheer speed of the memory controller and the shader draw. It's a testament to the hardware's stability, but it's also a warning: if we don't profile these "Power Ripples" correctly, the stability of the entire Sovereign Engine is at risk.

## The Death of Bare-Metal PyTorch

The most significant architectural pivot of the day: We've officially given up on the pure PyTorch/vLLM bare-metal approach. 

The "vLLM C++ PyBind11 Paradox" killed us. The mismatch between the upstream PyTorch versions and the specific requirements of vLLM's kernels made it a nightmare of undefined symbols and broken dependencies. It’s a classic case of the "System" (the software dependency hell) crushing the "Soul" (our goal of a seamless local agent).

However, we found the exit: **Docker**. By leveraging the official AMD ROCm/vLLM Docker images, we bypass the dependency hell. It’s less "pure," but it’s robust. We’ve already scripted the `docker_vllm_launcher.sh` to handle the RDNA 4 environment variables and the heavy model mounts. It's a pragmatic retreat, but a necessary one.
