# Dev Diary: 2026-02-27 (Part 17/25)

The friction between the "Soul" (the vision of a sovereign, local-first AI) and the "System" (the cold, unyielding reality of protocol errors and VRAM limits) was on full display today. I spent the better part of the afternoon wrestling with the identity of my "Resident Receptionist."

### The Latency Reality Check
I needed to know exactly how much performance headroom I was working with on the RX 9070 XT. I ran a latency benchmark on the `llama3.2:1b` model—the tiny, hyper-fast model I'm using for the Receptionist role. 

The results were startling. The routing latency clocked in at around 225ms. Compared to Alexa’s cloud roundtrip, which can drag anywhere from 1 to 3 seconds, the local model is nearly 10x faster. We aren't just competing with cloud latency; we're obliterating it. Even under a stress test—forcing 10 simultaneous tool calls in a single turn—the system processed them in under a second. This is the "Sovereign OS" advantage: the silicon is ready; the software just needs to catch up.

### The Authentication Wall
The real fight began when I tried to bring Gmail into the local ecosystem. I wanted to achieve true data sovereignty—downloading the inbox locally so the model could "see" it without ever touching a Google server again.

I hit a massive snag: `Error: IMAP wants more steps despite successful SASL authentication.` 

It was a classic case of protocol friction. I spent a significant amount of time digging through the `.mbsyncrc` configuration, trying to figure out if it was a SASL mechanism mismatch or a malformed App Password. I tried tweaking the `AuthMechs` to `PLAIN` and `LOGIN`, and even disabled `PipelineDepth` to satisfy the server's picky handshake. 

It turned out to be a simple, human error: I was using an old, cached email address in the config instead of the actual target `mgalyan@gmail.com`. Once corrected, the `mbsync` command finally shook hands with the server. 

### The Scaling Problem
The scale of the data is daunting. The sync is currently chugging through over 160,000 emails. It's moving, but it's slow. This is exactly why the "Librarian" architecture is vital. We can't just dump 160k emails into a context window; we need the `librarian_ingest.py` pipeline to feed this into the local vector database (ChromaDB) incrementally.
