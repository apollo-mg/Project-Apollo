# Apollo Dev Diary: 2026-01-29

The machine is finally emerging from the shadows of "Calibration Hell." Today was a grueling, multi-layered battle against the physics of my own hardware, but the results are undeniable. We have successfully mapped the machine's soul and constrained its chaos.

### The Ghost in the Machine
The day began by hunting a ghost. Using the vibration profiling tool, we identified a specific resonant frequency at 79Hz coming from the rear spine. It’s the "ringing" that haunts every high-speed print. We found the "Danger Zone" (70-130 mm/s) where the motors effectively shake the entire frame to pieces. 

My initial instinct was to slow down, but the "Sleeper" philosophy dictates otherwise: **Fly over them.** By pushing the Y-axis to 300 mm/s, we bypass the resonance peaks entirely. The motors act like gyroscopes, stabilizing the motion. However, we caught a trap—a small perimeter speed of 100 mm/s was hitting a resonance peak at 106 mm/s. One minor adjustment to 60 mm/s or 140 mm/s, and the "no-fly zone" is cleared.

### Correcting the Geometry
Next was the Axis Twist. My gantry wasn't just vibrating; it was physically twisting. We measured a variance of 0.065mm across the X-axis—roughly 30% of a standard 0.2mm first layer. Without compensation, my bed mesh was lying to me; the nozzle was effectively "dancing" on a tilted plane. Applying the `AXIS_TWIST_COMPENSATION` was a massive win for adhesion consistency.

Then came the final boss of geometry: **Skew Correction**. The X/Y plane was producing diamonds instead of squares. After a precise measurement of the diagonals, we applied the correction. The "Sleeper" is finally square.

### The Physics of Power: TMC Autotune
The most satisfying moment was the successful integration of the `klipper_tmc_autotune` plugin. 

This is where the "Soul" meets the "Silicon." My LDO-42STH48-2504AH motors are beastly, but they are also complex. Running them on 48V is a high-performance move, but the standard Klipper settings don't account for the exact electromagnetic profile of these specific coils. 

Using `Shake & Tune`, we measured the actual damping ratio ($\zeta$) of the X-axis (0.066) and Y-axis (0.110). We fed these physical constants back into the system. By applying these via `autotune_tmc`, the motors are now running with optimized PWM/Sine-wave settings tailored to their exact physics. It’s not just "running fast"; it’s running with mathematical elegance. The motors are cooler, quieter, and more efficient.

### The Crossroads: VzBot vs. WidowMaker
As the session wound down, we hit the architectural wall. My printer is a hybrid—part VzBot toolhead, part Ender 6 frame. 

The choice is clear: 
*   **WidowMaker** is the path of elegance—an enclosed, clean, "factory-finished" machine. 
*   **VzBot** is the path of raw, unadulterated speed—an open-frame, AWD monster.

Given my 48V LDO setup and the frame-twisting issues, the VzBot path is tempting, but it requires a total re-engineering of the frame. The decision to move to 2020 extrusions to facilitate a pure VzBot conversion is looming. 

**Current Status:** 
The calibration is complete. The machine is tuned. The physics-accurate profile is locked. The "Sleeper" is no longer a concept—it is a functioning, high-speed reality. 

Now, we see if it can actually print.
