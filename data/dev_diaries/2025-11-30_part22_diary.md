# Dev Diary: The Great Reset & The RDNA 4 Frontier

**Date:** 2025-11-30
**Session:** Part 22 / 43
**Hardware:** AMD RX 9070 XT (RDNA 4 / gfx1201)
**Status:** Environment Rebuilt. System Stable. Flash Attention Pending.

---

### The Friction: Soul vs. Silicon
Today was a brutal battle against the ghost in the machine. For the last several sessions, I’ve been fighting a losing battle against memory fragmentation and "Illegal Memory Access" errors on this RDNA 4 hardware. My previous attempts to patch the `WanAttentionBlock` were a desperate attempt to force a bleeding-edge model (Wan 2.1) to behave on hardware that—while powerful—is still fundamentally fighting the software stack.

The "Soul" of the project—the creative potential of high-fidelity video generation—was being strangled by the "System." The VRAM was pegged at 92%, the GPU was stalling on massive `contiguous()` calls, and the ROCm driver was throwing fits every time we hit a complex attention kernel. I realized I was trying to fix a broken engine while it was still running.

### The Epiphany: The Clean Slate
I reached a critical architectural realization: **The patches were becoming the problem.** My custom workarounds for the `attention_split` and `cast_to` were adding overhead that masked the real issue: the transition to the new ROCm 7.1.1 stack. 

The new ROCm release is a paradigm shift. It natively recognizes the `gfx1201` architecture of the RX 9070 XT. My "hacks" to force compatibility were actually creating friction. I had to stop patching and start rebuilding.

### The Reconstruction: Nuke and Pave
The session shifted from debugging to a full-scale environmental reconstruction. 

1.  **The Purge:** I performed a `git reset --hard` on the ComfyUI repo, stripping away all my previous RDNA 4 stability patches. I moved the old `custom_nodes` to a legacy backup to ensure a vanilla test environment.
2.  **The Space Crisis:** I hit a massive roadblock—`No space left on device`. The PyTorch wheels for ROCm 7.1.1 are gargantuan. I had to pivot my entire storage strategy, moving the virtual environment to the `/media/mark/Games 2TB1` secondary drive to bypass the saturated root partition.
3.  **The Installation:** It was a delicate dance. I had to manually construct the `pip` installation for the `torch-2.9.1+rocm7.1.1` wheels from the AMD Radeon repository. I had to skip `torchaudio` initially to avoid dependency hell, manually injecting the `torchvision` and `triton` wheels to match the exact git-hash provided by AMD.

### The Result: Native Harmony
The moment of truth came with `test_rocm.py`. 
*   **Status:** `CUDA Available: Yes (AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT)`
*   **The Win:** Matrix multiplication—the very thing that was causing the "Illegal Memory Access" in the old stack—now executes flawlessly. 

The system is no longer fighting the hardware; the hardware is finally speaking the same language as the software. We are running natively on ROCm 7.1.1.

### Current Trajectory: The Flash Attention Frontier
The engine is idling perfectly, but the performance ceiling is still too low for massive video generation. The next objective is the "Holy Grail": **Flash Attention.** 

I am currently hunting for the specific ROCm-compatible branch of `flash-attention` that supports the `gfx1201` architecture. If I can integrate this, the performance jump will be orders of magnitude, but it requires finding the exact intersection of the new ROCm release and the AMD-forked Triton kernels.

**Next Step:** Locate and build the RDNA 4-compatible Flash Attention kernels.
