**Project Apollo Log: 2025-11-29**
**Subject: Conv3d, Contiguous Memory, and the Return of SDMA**

Today’s session was a skirmish with the `hipErrorIllegalAddress` beast. It ambushed me right at the finish line—during the VAE decode of the Wan 2.1 model, specifically inside that stubborn `F.conv3d` operation. The RX 9070 XT, with its new RDNA 4 architecture, is picky about memory layouts.

The diagnosis was classic silicon friction. The `CausalConv3d` forward pass was tripping over non-contiguous tensors. I patched the `comfy/ldm/wan/vae.py` file, enforcing `x.clone().contiguous()` before the layer forward call. The logic is simple: force the tensor to be contiguous in memory, clone it to avoid aliasing issues, and the illegal access vanishes.

With that fixed, the ROCm 7.1.1 stack is singing. Flash Attention is active, and I’ve successfully re-enabled **SDMA** (System Direct Memory Access). The stress test passed cleanly, so we can lift the `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=0` restriction. This should give us snappier model loading and unloading times when swapping between the text encoder, diffusion model, and VAE.

This brings me to the question I left in the terminal: **Do we still need `--lowvram`?**

Given that the VRAM fragmentation is behaving and the DisTorch loading is stable (98.9% on GPU), I suspect we might not. But for now, I’ve updated `run_comfy_final.sh` with SDMA enabled and the `--lowvram` flag intact. It’s a safe harbor.

It feels good to have the local-first ecosystem humming again—Wan 2.1 for video, F5-TTS ready for audio, and no more illegal addresses. Just pure inference.
