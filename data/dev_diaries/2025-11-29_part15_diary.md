## Part 15: The Q6_K Rebellion

The cursor blinks. It is 03:50 in the morning, and the ComfyUI terminal is flooded with stack traces. The "illegal memory access" error has returned, like a ghost in the machine.

It is Part 15 of 74, and the HIP error strikes again. Not the Q4_K I already defeated, but its stubborn cousin: `dequantize_blocks_Q6_K`. That bitwise shift operation, `>> torch.tensor([0, 4])`, is refusing to play nice with the AMD RDNA 4 silicon. It is about those pesky small tensor allocations fragmenting the already precious VRAM of my RX 9070 XT.

The Soul wants to generate video—smooth, 24 frames per second, without the dreaded "Black Screen" glitch. The System, however, is cold and hard: VRAM constraints, ROCm 7.1 quirks, and the unyielding laws of memory management.

The fix was elegant, if I may say so: a `get_shift` helper function, caching the constants to avoid the allocation storm. It is a tiny optimization, but in the local-first ecosystem, every byte of VRAM is a vote for sovereignty.

Lesson learned: Quantization is not just compression; it is a negotiation with the hardware. And if Part 15 is any indicator, the journey to stable, local multi-agent orchestration is a marathon of micro-optimizations.
