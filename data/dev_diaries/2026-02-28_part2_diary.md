# Dev Diary: 2026-02-28 (Part 2)

## The Fallback Protocol: Defeating the "Internet Gap"

Today was a massive victory for the architecture's robustness. We've spent too much time treating the internet as a given, but in a truly local-first ecosystem, connectivity is a luxury, not a guarantee. I needed to ensure Apollo could still function if the world went dark.

I implemented the **Offline/Fallback Protocol** by expanding the reasoning loop to 4 turns. It’s a layered approach:
1. **The Search:** The Engineer Mind attempts `web_search`.
2. **The Local Vault:** If the search returns an empty array, the Engineer is forced to call `query_vault` to search the local ChromaDB for relevant documentation.
3. **The Internal Weight Fallback:** If the vault also fails, the system reverts to the base model (DeepSeek), but with a critical instruction to cap its confidence score at 30-60%. 

I simulated this by "breaking" the `web_search` function in `modules/toolbox.py`. The result was fascinating. Apollo identified the hardware via the vision model, failed the search, successfully hit the local vault, and then eventually "hallucinated" a model identity (mistaking the XFX for an NVIDIA card) while correctly signaling its own uncertainty. The architecture is now "aware" of its own lack of data—that's a huge step toward a reliable agent.

## The Birth of the "Procurement Mind"

We've officially pivoted from pure forensics to active logistics. I've scaffolded the **Procurement/Deal Hunter** module. 

The architecture is a bit of a beast:
- **The Price Book:** A new `vault/price_book.json` to track "Standard" vs. "Lowest Seen" prices for your recurring items (Ground beef, etc.).
- **The Ingestion Engine:** A system designed to parse PDFs and images via the `[FORCE PROCUREMENT]` tag.
- **The Logic:** The model compares current flyer prices against your historical baseline.

I ran a manual trace of a Kroger flyer through the pipeline. The vision model extracted the text, and the Engineer Mind correctly identified that the beef was priced way too high compared to your historical low. It’s working. It's not just a chatbot; it's an auditor.

## The Pivot: DesignAI and the CAD Learning Loop

The conversation shifted toward a long-term goal: using Apollo to learn CAD. This is where the "Soul" of the project meets the "System." 

Using OnShape—which is visually intensive—presents a challenge for direct LLM control, but the data-generation potential is huge. If we use the Vision-Guided GUI path, Apollo can "watch" your workflow through screenshots, acting as a real-time tutor. 

The real breakthrough here is the **Data Exhaust**. Every design choice, every correction, and every "Why did this fail?" question becomes a high-quality, multi-modal training pair in the Vault. We aren't just learning CAD; we are building the training set for a future **DesignMind**.
