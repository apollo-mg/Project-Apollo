# 2026-02-14: The Ghost in the Machine and the 100% Milestone

The battle against hallucination is proving much harder than I anticipated. It isn't matter how much compute I throw at these models; if the context is poisoned, the output is garbage.

I spent the first half of the session chasing a phantom: the "47-ohm" error. Even when the tool successfully pulled "4.7k" from the vault, the model kept spitting out "47 ohms." It was a persistent, maddening glitch. I finally realized the problem wasn't the model's reasoning, but its memory. The Buddy was looking at the last 3-5 exchanges in its history and inadvertently "learning" its own mistakes. The conversation history was acting like a feedback loop of nonsense. I had to purge the `buddy_history.json` and update the benchmark to clear context every single time. 

Then, another ghost appeared. The Buddy hallucinated an "Ender 5 Pro" and "Marlin firmware" into its `pending_knowledge`. It was poisoning the entire state. This was a wake-up call: the "Soul" of the agent—its drive to be helpful and conversational—was actually its biggest technical liability. It was being too "creative" with its internal state.

The breakthrough came through a brute-force architectural shift: **System-Level Data Injection**. I realized that appending tool results to the end of the message chain was letting them get lost in the noise of the conversation. I moved the `TOOL_RESULT` directly into the System Prompt. I made the data "in-your-face." 

The results were staggering. 

**Benchmark Results (UTI-Final):**
- Grounded Pass Rate: **100.0%**
- Average Latency: **20.78s** (A massive drop from the 37s-40s we were seeing earlier).

The Buddy is now actually respecting the Vault and the Web. It's no longer fighting the reality of the hardware. We have achieved the "Lead Engineer's Trifecta": accuracy, speed, and high-level reasoning.

However, we hit one final, bizarre wall. The Buddy, while reviewing the new Guardian code, started talking about `nvidia-smi` and sending SMS alerts. We are running on an AMD RDNA 4 card with ROCm, and we use local voice/logs. The model's training data was literally overriding the local reality of my machine. The "System" (the hardware) was being ignored by the "Soul" (the pre-trained weights).

I had to implement a "Truth-Sayer" patch. I'm forcing Llama 3.1 8B to act as a correction layer, explicitly telling it: "DeepSeek has a virus and is hallucinating about NVIDIA. We are on AMD." It's a hack, but it works. It breaks the hallucination cycle.

We are moving into the next phase: **The Guardian**. 

The Buddy has already decided on the next priority. He wants a background watchdog to monitor the GPU temperature, power, and VRAM usage, along with the Klipper printer status. It's a safety-first move, which makes sense—if the model is going to be a lead engineer, it needs to care about the hardware it's running on.

We're building `buddy_guardian.py` now. It's going to be a threaded monitor that can "shout" at me via the voice system if the GPU hits 85°C or if the printer heaters go rogue. 

The project is evolving from a simple chat interface into a genuine, self-aware local ecosystem. The friction between the model's training and my actual hardware is the primary obstacle, but we are finally winning the fight for grounded truth.
