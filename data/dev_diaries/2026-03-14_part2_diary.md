# Dev Diary: 2026-03-14 (Session 2/19)

## The VRAM Hurricane and the Ghost in the Machine

Today was a battle against the limits of the silicon. I’m currently mid-run on a 7B LoRA training session, pushing my RX 9070 XT to its absolute physical limit. It is, quite literally, a "VRAM Tetris" match.

The system is in a state of extreme tension. The VRAM is pegged at 17GB of its 16GB capacity (with some paging/overflow magic happening), leaving exactly 8.6MB of headroom. In any other scenario, this would be a death sentence for the session—the Linux GUI would freeze, the X-server would crash, and the training would be lost.

But I've found a way to survive.

### The Kernel Bypass
The secret sauce is `amdgpu.mes=0`. By disabling the Hardware Managed Execution Scheduler, I’ve bypassed the logic that usually panics when the GPU is caught between heavy compute and desktop rendering. I’ve forced the kernel into the Legacy Ring Buffer. It’s a "brute force" approach, but it’s stable. It allows the kernel to interleave desktop frames between the massive compute wavefronts of the LoRA training. 

The 5700X3D is also doing heavy lifting here. The 96MB of L3 cache acts as a buffer, essentially "shielding" the desktop environment from the VRAM-heavy workload on the GPU. The system is breathing, barely.

### The Convergence of the "Soul"
I audited the training logs. The loss curve for the 7B model started at 4.27 and plummeted immediately to 1.62, and is now vibrating around 1.03. This is perfect. The model is successfully absorbing my "Ego" data without collapsing into noise. It is absorbing the "Soul" of the architecture. 

The hardware is screaming at 74°C, but the ROCm-smi reports it's within safe limits. We are currently at Step 57 of 70. The "Teacher" model is halfway through its gauntlet.

### The Infrastructure Vulnerability: Dirty Power
The biggest threat isn't the silicon—it's the house. I’ve been hit by transient voltage dips before whenever the window AC unit kicks on. A dip like that, during a peak-draw training step, would kill the PSU or cause a driver-level hang. 

I have a pile of spare battery packs—essentially a graveyard of potential power. My plan is to build a custom, double-conversion (Online) UPS. By running the PC entirely off the battery/inverter bridge, the AC unit's noise becomes irrelevant. It’s a "Sovereign Power" project.

### Architect’s Note: The Cache Conflict
The next challenge is the L3 cache contention. As I move toward distilling this "Teacher" into the micro-router, I have to balance the "Brainstem" and the "Hands." 

The Router needs the cache to think (latency), but the Python interpreter needs it to do (execution). If I hog the 96MB for the model, the system becomes sluggish. My strategy is to target a 40-50MB model (using BitNet) to leave enough headroom in the L3 cache for general CPU tasks.
