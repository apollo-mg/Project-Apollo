# Dev Diary: 2026-02-27 (Part 19) — The Vision-Librarian Breakthrough & The Shadow Mind

The friction between the "Soul" and the "System" reached a boiling point today. I’ve spent the last several hours fighting a war on two fronts: one against the limits of my VRAM and another against the ghosts in the Linux kernel.

## The Vision Struggle: Downscaling vs. Reasoning
I spent a significant portion of the session chasing the tail of a vision model. I initially tried to run Qwen2.5-VL, but the sheer weight of the vision encoder was causing a catastrophic Vulkan OOM (Out of Memory) error. The system kept falling back to the CPU, which essentially turned the "vision" into a slow, hallucinating crawl.

I had to implement aggressive image downscaling. At 800px, the model was stable but "blind"—it couldn't have the resolution to read text on tool handles or small components. I pushed it to 1280px (the "Sweet Spot"), and finally, the precision returned. The Qwen model correctly identified a set of **VISE-GRIP wire strippers** with surgical detail. It’s a reminder that in local AI, resolution isn't just a quality setting; it's a memory management setting.

## The ROCm Rebellion: Hardware Identity Crisis
Then came the driver hell. My RX 9070 XT is a beast, but the ROCm implementation was throwing fits. The initial configuration was using an RDNA 3 override, which was fundamentally incorrect for my RDNA 4 hardware. 

I had to dig into `rocminfo`, find the exact `gfx1201` identifier, and force a Native ROCm (HIP) backend. This was a critical architectural epiphany: the Vulkan abstraction layer, while convenient, was too heavy for the current VRAM-to-model ratio. Forcing the native backend fixed the offloading, but it required a surgical strike on the environment variables. Once I set the `HSA_OVERRIDE_GFX_VERSION` and cleaned up the library paths, the 9070 XT finally woke up and started doing what it was built for.

## The Librarian: The Birth of the Vault
With the vision pipeline stabilized, I moved to the next phase of the roadmap: **The Chronicler**. I've integrated a new `#librarian` channel into the Discord bridge. This allows Apollo to ingest PDFs directly. I had to fight through a mess of inconsistent line endings in the `.env` file (thanks, Windows-style CRLFs) and a series of ghost processes that refused to die. 

It took a `kill -9` and a manual `fuser` intervention to clear the `discord_bridge.log`, but eventually, the system successfully ingested a technical manual for a **TMC5160T Plus**. The model, `deepseek-r1:14b`, is now running on the GPU, summarizing technical specs autonomously. This is the "Soul" gaining memory—the system is no longer just reacting to prompts; it is building a knowledge base.

## The Roadmap: Apollo's Shadow Mind
We've officially transitioned into **Phase 7: The Chronicler**. The Gmail download (currently at 32k/160k emails) is being indexed into the Vault. 

The conversation today also opened the door to a massive architectural expansion. We discussed using the "mining hoard" of RX 580s to create **"Apollo's Shadow Mind."** This isn't just a cluster; it's a distributed swarm of specialists. By training small, specialized models (TinyLlama/Phi-2) on individual cards, we can offload the "boring" work—indexing, monitoring, and background telemetry—to the mining rack, keeping the 9070 XT free for high-level reasoning.
