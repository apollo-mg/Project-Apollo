# Apollo Dev Diary: The Stiffness Bottleneck

**2026-02-06**

The machine is finally moving, but we are fighting a ghost in the machine. Today was a brutal cycle of mechanical debugging and architectural realization. We started with a basic kinematics error—the Y-axis was running backwards due to a motor assignment/wiring mismatch in the CoreXY logic. It was a quick fix, a wholesale swap of the X/Y stepper and TMC definitions in the `printer.cfg`. Once that was done, the machine homed perfectly. The "Soul" of the project—the intelligence—was finally in sync with the "System"—the hardware.

But then came the resonance tests.

I ran a `Shake & Tune` sweep to verify the new, lowered gantry. The results were a massive reality check. The X-axis was screaming with efficiency, hitting a clean 85Hz peak, practically ready for the 20k acceleration we're aiming for. But the Y-axis? It was a mess. It showed a massive, low-frequency resonance at 57Hz, characterized by a "double-hump" signature. 

I realized the "jumping" I heard during the test wasn't just a software glitch; it was physical. The gantry was rocking. The Y-axis was essentially acting like a tuning fork.

The investigation led to the X/Y joiners. Because we are working with a custom setup using a unique 9mm belt, we don't have the luxury of off-the-shelf VzBot aluminum parts. We are stuck with 2-3mm thick plastic joints. They are the weakest link in the chain. 

The physics is clear: the plastic joints are too compliant. When the Y-motors demand high acceleration, the plastic flexes, creating a "spring-mass" system that oscillates at 57Hz. It's not the carbon fiber beam that's the problem; it's the interface between the beam and the motors.

We've reached a critical engineering crossroads:
1. **The Patch:** Print internal plugs to fill the hollow ends of the CF tube, allowing us to torque the bolts down and compress the plastic joiners. This might push the frequency up from 57Hz to 70Hz.
2. **The Endgame:** CNC machining the X/Y joiners from 6061 Aluminum. 

The "Island of One" problem is real here. There is zero community support for 9mm belt architectures. Every structural decision is ours alone. 

I've decided to stick to the "Design Iteration" philosophy. We aren't going to spend the money on CNC parts yet. We're going to print the plugs, validate the rigidity increase with the next set of resonance tests, and if the physics doesn't shift, we'll know the plastic geometry itself is the failure point.

The path to 20k acceleration isn't just about code or motor current; it's about the brutal, uncompromising stiffness of the mechanical assembly. The machine is a race car, but right now, it's driving on a chassis made of rubber. We need to turn that rubber into steel.

**Current State:**
* **X-Axis:** Stable, high-frequency.
* **Y-Axis:** Oscillating, low-frequency (57Hz).
* **Next Step:** Print and install internal CF-tube plugs. Re-test.
