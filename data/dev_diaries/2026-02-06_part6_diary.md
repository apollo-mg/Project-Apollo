# Dev Diary: The Ghost in the Config & The Y-Axis Mystery

**Date:** 2026-02-06
**Session:** Part 6/7

The day started with a battle against a corrupted soul. I was deep in the weeds of Klipper configuration, trying to get the new BigTreeTech (BD) sensor integrated. The system was throwing a tantrum—`SAVE_CONFIG` was failing silently. No errors, no restart, just... nothing. 

It turns out the `printer.cfg` was corrupted. A ghost in the machine. The `SAVE_CONFIG` block at the bottom of the file had become malformed, likely due to a messy transition from the old Eddy sensor setup to the new BD sensor. It’s the classic friction between the **System** (the file structure) and the **Soul** (the calibration data I was trying to preserve).

I had to perform a surgical strike: delete the corrupted block, wipe the local config, and pull a fresh, clean copy from the Raspberry Pi via `scp`. It was a reminder that in a local-first ecosystem, the "source of truth" isn't your desktop—it's the edge device. If the Pi is stale, your local work is fiction.

Once the file was cleaned and the system was breathing again, I moved into the resonance testing. The belt comparison was the first big hurdle. I was worried the printhead wasn't being centered, which would ruin the physics of the test. In a CoreXY system, the belts are like guitar strings—their frequency is tied to their length. If the head isn't at the mechanical center, the tension readings are lies.

But the results were... startling. The belt comparison came back with a 92.6% similarity. The frequencies matched within 1.6Hz. Even with the off-center positioning, the mechanical balance is nearly perfect. My belt tensioning is a work of art.

Then came the Input Shaper calibration. 

The X-axis is a dream. Carbon fiber gantry, extremely stiff, a single clean peak at 80Hz. I can push 18,000 mm/s² there without the system even flinching.

But the Y-axis? The Y-axis is a problem. It’s hitting a resonance at 48Hz. 

Here’s the technical friction: I’ve swapped the heavy Stealthburner for a lightweight CF tube and a VzBot toolhead. By all laws of physics, the Y-axis should be behaving like the X-axis. It's light. It's stiff. But the data is screaming that it's "heavy" or "loose."

**The Hypotheses:**
1. **The Compliance Theory:** Is the CF tube actually *too* stiff? Is it inducing a high-frequency vibration in the gantry that the sensor is picking up as a low-frequency mass signature?
2. **The Path Theory:** The belt path for the Y-axis might be interacting with the frame in a way the X-axis isn't. Is there a resonance in the frame itself being excited by the Y-axis motion?
3. **The Mechanical Slack Theory:** Even with a light toolhead, is there micro-play in the Y-axis linear rails or the belt tensioner that's creating a "wobble" that mimics mass?

I'm left with a massive gap between my architectural intent (a lightweight, high-speed machine) and the silicon's reality (a 48Hz limiting factor). I need to dig into the spectrograms. There’s a secondary bump at 70Hz on the Y-axis that isn't in the X-axis. That’s not mass; that’s something else.

**Next Steps:**
- Deep dive into the Y-axis spectrogram.
- Check for mechanical play in the Y-axis rails.
- Manually tune the Y-axis shaper to see if the peak shifts or disappears.

The machine is beautiful, but the machine is lying to me. Time to find out why.
