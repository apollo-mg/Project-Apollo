# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-02

The transition to V3 is officially complete. It’s a strange feeling—one moment I’m wrestling with the sheer weight of a 160k email ingestion task, and the next, I’m watching my architecture achieve a level of "reflexive intelligence" I didn't think possible on a single-GPU local setup.

The Cascading Router is the crown jewel of this update. We've successfully implemented a tiered intent classification engine that is essentially immune to hallucination. It’s a sub-billion parameter gatekeeper that operates at ~400 Tokens Per Second. It’s terrifyingly efficient. It catches the "vague/system" queries instantly, then escalates to the heavy-hitter models (Qwen3:8B or DeepSeek-R1) only when the task demands actual cognitive heavy lifting. 

The friction between the "Soul" and the "System" remains our biggest battle. I was pushing the limits of the 16GB VRAM on my RX 9070 XT, and the safety locks actually *tripped*. The `vram_management.py` script saw the 10.5 GB being hogged by the email ingestion daemon and refused to load the 30B model to prevent a driver crash. It’s a brutal reminder: the software is getting smarter, but the silicon has hard, unyielding limits.

I've also successfully migrated the "Brain" to the Qwen3 family. The 0.6B model is a revelation—it has a native reasoning architecture that, despite its tiny footprint, handles simple classification with near-instant latency. It’s the "Reflex" layer of Apollo.

But the physical side of the project—the "Body"—is still a ghost. 

I spent a significant portion of the session wrestling with the Raspberry Pi 5 (the "Nervous System"). It's a bit of a headache. The network-level communication was a mess of blocked ports and failed SSH attempts until I realized I had to manually forge a bridge. I finally managed to generate an Ed25519 key and push it to the `gemini` user on the Pi. The Pi is now officially part of the "Tailnet." It's a small victory, but it means the foundation is laid for the "Shadow Mind Swarm."

The email ingestion is still chugging along in the background (8.3 GB and growing), thanks to Google's throttled IMAP speeds. Once that's done, the "Memory" will be fully populated, and Apollo will finally have its history.

**Core Lessons & Architecture Evolution:**
* **The Multi-Tiered Approach is mandatory for local-first.** You cannot run 30B models for every trivial task. The "Reflex vs. Logic" split is the only way to survive VRAM constraints.
* **Hardware-Software Symbiosis:** My VRAM management scripts are just as important as the LLM itself. If the software doesn't respect the silicon, the "Soul" dies in a crash.
* **Network Sovereignty is the next frontier:** Moving from a single workstation to a distributed "Swarm" (Pi 5, satellite nodes) requires a robust, encrypted mesh (Tailscale) from day one.

**Current Status:**
* **Architecture:** Apollo V3 (Qwen3-based) - **Verified.**
* **Ingestion:** 8.3 GB / 160k emails - **In Progress.**
* **Physical:** Pi 5 integrated into Tailnet - **Success.**

Next up: Building out the "Shadow Mind" mesh and seeing how much more we can squeeze out of this hardware.
