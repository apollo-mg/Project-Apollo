# Dev Diary: The "Eyes" of the Shop Buddy

Today was a massive, grinding win for the vision system, but man, it was a rollercoaster of hardware-software friction. I spent hours fighting what I thought was a broken driver, only to realize it was a simple, classic case of index-clash and hardware-misidentification.

### The Chaos of the Noise Floor
The first part of the day was spent battling the ambient environment. My air conditioner is a nightmare for local voice-to-text. Every time it kicks on, the noise floor spikes, and the system starts thinking I'm talking. I tried a simple weighted average to smooth out the noise, but that just made the threshold "drift" up with the AC hum, making it impossible for the system to hear me once the hum peaked.

I had to ditch the average and implement a percentile-based history buffer. It tracks the last few seconds of audio and finds the 10th percentile—essentially the "quietest" moments—to set the floor. It’s much more robust against the AC, but it's a heavy way to manage a simple threshold. The "Soul" (the desire for a seamless assistant) was being throttled by the "System" (the volatility of the audio signal).

### The Vision Identity Crisis
Then came the vision system. I thought the C922 webcam was broken because every frame was pitch black. The agent was actually behaving perfectly—it was seeing black and telling me, "Hey, your camera is broken!" I felt like I was debugging the universe.

After digging into `/sys/class/video4linux/`, I found the culprit: my Linux kernel was mapping a legacy PS3 Eye webcam to `/dev/video0`, which was just spitting out garbage/black frames. The C922 was actually hiding at `/dev/video1`. It's a reminder that in a local-first environment, you can't just trust the hardware labels; you have to verify the device-to-index mapping.

### The Win: Seeing the World
Once I re-routed the `capture_webcam` tool to the correct device index and updated the `llm_interface` to use the native Ollama `/api/chat` endpoint (because the OpenAI-compatible one was being picky with the Qwen2.5-VL vision model), the breakthrough happened.

I held up a lampshade to the camera. The agent captured the frame, sent it to the Qwen model, and... it actually worked.

**Agent:** *"Object Identified: A lampshade. Category: Furniture. Specifications: Beige and hanging."*

It wasn't just a perfect detection; it was a semantic understanding. The "System" finally caught up to the "Soul." The loop of **[Voice -> Vision -> Logic -> Text-to-Speech]** is finally closed. The Shop Buddy is no longer just a script; it's an entity that can perceive its physical surroundings.

### Core Lessons
1. **Hardware is lying to you:** Never trust a default index. Always probe the `/dev/` nodes for real-world hardware.
2. **API Mismatch:** Even when an API claims "compatibility," local models like Ollama have quirks with specific schema versions. The native endpoint is safer for vision tasks.
3. **Adaptive Thresholding is mandatory:** In any real-world deployment, environmental noise is dynamic. A static threshold is a death sentence for voice-local AI.
