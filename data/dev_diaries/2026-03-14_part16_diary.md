# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-14

## The Mechanical Symphony of the Forge

Today was one of those days where the "Soul" of the project—the vision of a sovereign, unchained intelligence—collided violently with the "System"—the crushing reality of hardware bottlenecks and the chaotic volatility of the 2026 hardware market.

I am currently running a massive, 35B parameter Mixture of Experts (MoE) model in BF16. The sheer weight of it—roughly 70GB—is a direct affront to my 16GB VRAM limit. I am witnessing the "Physical Cost of Sovereignty" in real-time. As the model loads, my system is screaming. The HDD is grinding out a rhythmic, mechanical wail as it drags these weights from the slow, mechanical platters into the NVMe, through the SysRAM, and finally attempting to "Tetris" them into the VRAM.

The I/O saturation is so intense that my YouTube playback is stuttering; the interrupt latency is hitting the CPU so hard that the OS is losing its grip on the desktop. It’s a beautiful, gritty chaos. It is the "Sound of the Forge."

## The Memory Black Hole

The realization hit me hard: the DRAM price charts are absolutely broken. The market has been cannibalized by the HBM3E-driven AI arms race. Every bit of high-performance memory is being vacuumed up by the hyper-scalers, leaving consumer-grade DDR5 as a luxury. I am essentially paying a "Memory Tax" just to exist in this era. 

This confirms the necessity of my current architecture. If I cannot afford the massive DRAM banks required for a dense 70B model, I must rely on the sparsity of the MoE. The MoE strategy isn't just a technical choice; it is my only way to bypass the VRAM-to-DRAM bottleneck. By using the 3D V-Cache on my Ryzen 5700X3D as a tiered "L3 cache" buffer, I can rotate the "Experts" through the L3-to-VRAM pipeline, essentially bypassing the massive DRAM price-spike by relying on the on-chip cache for the "Decision" logic.

I’m turning "Trash" into "Intelligence."
