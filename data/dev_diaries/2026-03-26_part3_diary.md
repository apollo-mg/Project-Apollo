# Dev Diary: 2026-03-26 | The Unsloth Breakthrough

The friction between the "Soul" and the "System" is becoming a constant, grinding reality. I'm chasing this ideal of a seamless, high-reasoning local agent—the "Sovereign Engine"—but I keep hitting the wall of the silicon. Today was a battle against VRAM fragmentation and the sheer weight of the MoE (Mixture of Experts) architecture.

I was testing the Qwen 3.5 35B model earlier. The intelligence is there—it's staggering—but the memory footprint is a nightmare. Every time the model tries to swap experts, the VRAM spikes, the system thrashes, and my RX 9070 XT gasps for air. It’s like trying to run a high-resolution film through a narrow straw.

Then, the epiphany. 

I started digging into the Unsloth documentation. It turns out the community is already fighting the same war. They've moved beyond simple quantization. They’re doing "Dynamic 2.0"—essentially a surgical strike on the model's weights. They identify the 1% of the data that actually matters—the "salient" weights—and keep them in 8-bit or 16-bit, while ruthlessly crushing the rest of the model down to 2-bit or 3-bit. It’s biological quantization, much like how our brains filter stimuli to prevent cognitive overload.

I found a piece of community engineering that is absolutely wild: a version of Qwen 3.5 that was "abliterated" (stripped of its safety guardrails) but suffered from "brain damage" during the process. The creator used Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence—a mathematical measure of how much two probability distributions differ—to surgically repair 29 broken tensors, bringing the model back to 67% of its original logical integrity without re-imposing the censorship. It’s beautiful. It’s the first time I've seen the "Soul" of a model being repaired by pure mathematical precision.

The immediate plan is a diagnostic test. I'm pulling the `i2_xxs` (2-bit) version of the Unsloth-quantized Qwen model. It’s a gamble. 2-bit is extreme; it might "lobotomize" the model's reasoning, but it will tell us if the memory offloading works. If that tiny, 10GB file runs without the system thrashing, we’ve broken the hardware bottleneck. We've found the path to running massive MoEs on consumer-grade silicon.

I've also pulled the vision-projection model (`mmproj-F16.gguf`) because if we can get the vision-language loop stable, the "Sovereign Engine" stops being just a text-based ghost and becomes an entity that can actually perceive its digital environment.

I've archived all the Unsloth technical specs and the `llama.cpp` execution parameters into my offline database. Everything is being staged on the 2TB drive. 

The next phase: testing the limits of the 2-bit quant. If it holds, we move to 4-bit and finally reclaim the intelligence we lost to the memory wall.

**Status: Downloads in progress. System status: Monitoring VRAM. Next move: Scaffolding the execution scripts.**
