# Dev Diary: The Great Silicon Migration & The `/tmp` Crisis

**Date: 2026-03-06**
**Status: System Restored. Agent Lab Live.**

Today was a brutal lesson in the friction between software elegance and the messy reality of filesystem permissions. I spent the morning fighting a war between my high-speed NVMe and a rogue bind-mount that nearly bricked my desktop environment.

### The `/tmp` Catastrophe
The core of the chaos was a botched attempt to relocate `/tmp` to a secondary NTFS-formatted drive (`TG_2TB`). I wanted to protect my primary NVMe from the constant write-cycles of local AI training, but I overlooked the fundamental incompatibility of NTFS with POSIX-compliant permission requirements. 

Because the NTFS drive couldn't support the "sticky bit" (`drwxrwxrwt`) required for `/tmp`, Google Chrome and even `apt` started crashing immediately upon launch. The system was throwing `SIGTRAP` errors, throwing a tantrum because it couldn't have a proper place to store temporary sockets. I had to surgically unmount the broken `/etc/fstab` entry and restore the system to its default state. Lesson learned: Never use a FUSE-based NTFS mount for system-critical directories. If I want to move `/tmp`, I need a native `ext4` or `tmpfs` partition.

### Establishing the "Control Plane"
With the desktop stabilized, we moved into the most critical phase of Project Apollo: Decentralization. 

The goal was to move the "brain"—the Gemini CLI and the session state—off the main workstation and onto the Raspberry Pi 5 (`zoey-sat`). This creates a "Control Plane." If my workstation OOMs (Out of Memory) during a massive 334W Flux render or hits a kernel panic during a heavy ROCm task, the session context, the conversation history, and the agent's logic will remain alive on the Pi. It’s our fail-safe.

The installation on the Pi was smooth—Node.js v22 and the Gemini CLI are now running on the ARM architecture. I synced the project metadata and the `.env` files, effectively "bridging" the two machines. 

### The Agent Lab is Live
I've successfully booted the hardened ComfyUI instance on the RDNA 4 silicon. I caught a rogue process trying to overwrite my PyTorch stack with a generic CPU version—a classic "AI-induced" dependency nightmare—and patched the `requirements.txt` to lock it to **PyTorch 2.12 + ROCm 7.1**.

We are now officially in the "Generative Agent" phase. The models (including a massive 12GB Flux Schnell FP8) are accessible from the 3TB vault. The infrastructure is ready for the first autonomous loop:
1. **The Mind:** Ollama-driven LLM (Qwen) generating prompts.
2. **The Forge:** Flux rendering the vision.
3. **The Eyes:** MiniCPM-V/Qwen-VL verifying the output.

The setup is no longer just a desktop; it's a distributed, resilient multi-agent ecosystem. The "Soul" (the creative intent) is now protected by a "System" (the Pi 5 control plane) that won't care if my GPU crashes.
