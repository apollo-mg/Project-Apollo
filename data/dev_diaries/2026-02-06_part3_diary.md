# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-02-06 (Part 3)

## The Ghost in the Machine

Today was a brutal lesson in why "digital precision" is a lie when your physical world is falling apart. I spent the better part of the afternoon wrestling with the physical geometry of the Ender 6, and let me tell you—the friction between the mathematical elegance of my coordinate-shifting logic and the messy reality of a broken belt is enough to make anyone quit.

### The Broken Link
A sudden, jarring snap of an EPDM belt. One of my brand new belts, severed mid-loop. It was a gut punch. I managed a desperate, "MacGyver" fix—splicing it back together with thin wire and pliers—but for a high-speed machine aiming for 20k acceleration, that's a massive variable. It’s a brave little soldier, but I have no idea how it'll hold under tension.

### The Coordinate War
The core of my struggle today was the coordinate system. I was trying to redefine the origin (0,0) to match the physical bed corner. It became a recursive nightmare. I thought I had the offset:
- X-axis: Shifted based on the 22mm offset.
- Y-axis: Shifted based on the -22mm offset.

But the machine wouldn't listen. I was chasing the tail of a ghost. Every time I updated the `position_endstop` and the `position_max`, Klipper reported a different error or a new, slightly different offset (X=24, Y=-23... then X=25, Y=-25). 

Then, the realization hit: I wasn't actually changing the machine's brain. I was editing files on my desktop, but the "brain" (the Raspberry Pi) was still running the old, corrupted code. I was performing high-level calculus on a ghost configuration.

### The Silicon Rebellion (The Eddy/BD Conflict)
Once I finally synced the files, a new error emerged: `Unknown config object 'mcu eddy'`. 

This was a classic architectural collision. I had been trying to calibrate the new Eddy induction sensor, but we had decided to revert to the standard BD sensor. The system was caught in a state of "half-existence"—the `printer.cfg` was trying to call an Eddy probe that wasn't fully defined, or the `SAVE_CONFIG` block was injecting ghost parameters from a previous calibration attempt. 

The sheer frustration of seeing a "success" message followed by a "halted" error is the reality of local-first development. You aren't just fighting code; you're fighting the memory of the hardware.

### Lessons from the Trenches

1.  **The Sync-Fail Trap:** Never assume your local environment and your hardware-attached environment are in sync. In a multi-agent ecosystem, if the agent's "worldview" (the config) isn't match the "physical reality" (the hardware), all the intelligence in the world is useless.
2.  **The Anchor Strategy:** When the math goes circular, stop chasing the delta. Anchor to the physical hardware. I tried to chase the offset, but I should have anchored to the actual bed size (290mm) from the start.
3.  **Hardware is Unforgiving:** You can't "undo" a cut belt. You can only mitigate it. The gap between my theoretical X-travel (262mm) and the actual bed (290mm) is a permanent scar on this machine's capability.

**Current Status:** The coordinate system is (theoretically) locked, but the system is still throwing errors regarding the Eddy probe. The machine is "alive" but refuses to breathe. Tomorrow, we hunt the ghost in the `eddy_macros.cfg`.
