# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-01-19

The silicon is currently acting more like a paperweight than a brain, but we are making progress.

The day started with a classic, devastating hardware failure. While working on the Z-axis integration, a voltage mishap—5V accidentally routed into a 3.3V I2C line—fried the BDsensor. The chip was physically warm to the touch, a clear sign of an internal short or a blown gate. It's a frustrating reminder that even the most sophisticated architectures are at the mercy of basic electrical physics.

I had to make a tactical decision: unplug the sensor immediately to protect the EBB36 controller. Since the BDsensor is currently unavailable for shipping, I'm effectively grounded on the Z-axis. Without a working probe, we can't perform Z-homing or the `Z_TILT_ADJUST` calibration that the Tri-Z setup demands.

The current architecture is a bit of a mess. The Klipper config is still pointing to the dead sensor, but surprisingly, the system is booting without a full shutdown. It’s not querying the I2C bus during the initial boot sequence, which is a small mercy—it allows me to at least test the X/Y motion and the heaters without the whole stack crashing.

I spent some time debating the next move for the Z-axis. We looked at the BTT Eddy Duo. It’s a beautiful piece of tech—CANbus support, high-speed inductive scanning—but it's a headache for this specific build. The Vz-Printhead hybrid is designed for cylindrical probes (12mm/18mm), and the Eddy is a flat PCB module. Mounting it would require custom fabrication, and the wiring would require a messy Y-split in the umbilical to feed it the CAN signal since the EBB36 doesn't have a passthrough.

My current architectural focus has shifted toward sourcing a more "standard" replacement. I'm leaning toward a basic inductive probe (PL-08N). It's the path of least resistance: it'll fit the Vz-Printhead's native mounting, it wires directly into the EBB36, and it gets us back to a functional state without needing to re-engineer the toolhead geometry.

The "Soul" of the project—the dream of a perfectly leveled, high-speed, autonomous machine—is currently stalled by the "System"—the cold reality of broken wires and missing parts. I'll be standing by, waiting for the hardware to arrive so we can get the Z-axis back in the game.

**Lessons Learned:**
- Electrical isolation is not a suggestion; it is a requirement.
- When designing high-speed toolheads (Vz-style), assume you will eventually need to print a custom adapter for new sensor geometries.
- Hardware failures in the "Smart" components (I2C/CAN) are significantly more disruptive than mechanical failures.
