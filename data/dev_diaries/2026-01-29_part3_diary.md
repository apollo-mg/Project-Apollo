# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-01-29

The internal struggle between the "Soul" and the "System" has reached a fever pitch. For weeks, I've been fighting a phantom: that 79Hz resonance in the Y-axis. It’s a ghost in the machine, a vibration that mocks every attempt at higher acceleration. My current hybrid setup—a Frankenstein’s monster of an Ender 6 frame, a heavy LGX Lite head, and a custom-built X-rail—is physically incapable of handling the torque. The frame is twisting, the spine is racking, and the math is relentless.

Today, I finally broke the loop.

The debate was between the "VzBot 330" and the "WidowMaker." The VzBot is the industry standard for high-speed, high-acceleration performance. It's "proven." But it’s a trap for my specific build. It demands 6mm belts. My toolhead is a heavyweight; 6mm belts would be too floppy, too much "spring" in the system. I’d be trading stiffness for the luxury of a "kit."

Instead, I'm leaning into the WidowMaker. It’s the design of a friend, and it’s fundamentally more robust for my hardware. The WidowMaker uses 9mm belts—providing the massive tensile stiffness I need to manage the inertia of my heavy toolhead. Most importantly, the CAD reveals the secret to my resonance problem: the "Lower Deck" subframe. It's not just a gantry; it's a structural hoop that braces the vertical columns. It turns the machine into a rigid cage.

The engineering decision is clear: I am building the "WidowMaker Low-Rider."

It’s a true "Sleeper." By mounting the motion system on this subframe, I can drop the center of gravity and potentially fit the entire assembly under the stock acrylic lid. It’s a high-risk, high-reward move.

The friction, however, remains in the fabrication. The "System" is demanding parts that don't exist in the standard consumer world. I looked for 5mm internally threaded dowel pins for the idlers—the "holy grail" for rigid shaft mounting—and found only imperial garbage on Amazon. The "Soul" wants a perfect, threaded, custom-machined pin. The "System" says that's an engineering bottleneck.

I'm pivoting. I won't hunt for impossible parts. I'll use a standard 5mm hardened steel dowel pin, press it into the printed mounts, and let the plastic geometry capture it. It's cleaner, it's standard in the Voron ecosystem, and it's robust.

I have the tools. I have the drill press. I have the 3.9-meter length of 2020 extrusion for the subframe. I'm not just an assembler anymore; I'm a fabricator.

The plan is set:
1. Fabricate the 2040 subframe.
2. Re-engineer the idler mounts for 5mm pins.
3. Bridge the gap between the "WidowMaker" design and my "Ender 6" skin.

I’m not just building a printer. I’m evolving a design. I'm going to see if I can push this "WidowMaker Evo" past the limits of the original.

**Status: Phase 2 (Fabrication) initiated.**
