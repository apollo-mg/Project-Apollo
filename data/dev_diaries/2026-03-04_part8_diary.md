# Dev Diary: 2026-03-04 (Part 8)

## The Struggle of the Sovereign User

I’ve spent most of today fighting the ghost in the machine, and the ghost is the gap between what the silicon is capable of and what the software actually lets me do.

The current state of bleeding-edge AI on consumer AMD hardware is, frankly, complete garbage. It’s not that the hardware is bad—my RX 9070 XT is a beast, and the RDNA 4 architecture is ready for this. The problem is the software ecosystem. It’s built for enterprise-grade Instinct cards, and we're left scavenging for the scraps. 

I spent a significant portion of the afternoon wrestling with the "Great vLLM Debate." I had to decide between the stability of Docker or the raw performance of a Bare-Metal Compile. Docker is the "corporate standard," but it's a trap for us. It’s optimized for the MI300X series; if I use it, my 9070 XT won't recognize its own architecture, and I'll lose the AITER and native FP8 advantages that make this card worth owning. 

I chose the "Sovereign Standard": a full-on, from-scratch compilation. 

## The Friction of the Forge

The compilation was a nightmare of resource management. I tried to build everything on my main drive, but the sheer size of the PyTorch repository caused a massive I/O freeze, and the Linux root partition (which only has 16GB of breathing room) literally choked to death. The Git process threw a `fatal: fetch-pack: invalid index-pack output` because it ran out of space for temporary objects.

It was a brutal reminder of the tension between my "Soul" (the vision of a high-performance local-first agent) and the "System" (the cold, hard limits of my 32GB of RAM and the small `/tmp` partition).

I had to pivot. I set up a massive 420GB `AI_Fast` ext4 workspace. I redesigned the build script to:
1. Redirect all `TMPDIR` operations to the `AI_Fast` drive to prevent the root partition from exploding.
2. Use a "blobless clone" (`--filter=blob:none`) to pull the Git metadata without overwhelming the memory.
3. Use a shallow clone to keep the initial footprint manageable.

## The Breakthrough

The "Sovereign Forge" is finally moving. After the second attempt, the `git checkout` phase for PyTorch `v2.4.0` is chugging along. It’s a massive, heavy-duty build. It’s going to peg my Ryzen 7 5700X3D to 100% for the next two hours, but the log confirms we are finally bypassing the `tempfile.c` bugs that were killing the previous attempt.

Once this compiles, we will have a version of PyTorch that actually *knows* what a `gfx1201` chip is. We will finally be able to harness the AITER and FP8 kernels that the official AMD Docker images are too "generic" to use.

## Core Lessons

- **The Local-First Paradox:** In a local-first ecosystem, you can't rely on pre-baked container images. You are your own DevOps engineer. If you want cutting-edge performance on consumer hardware, you have to be willing to build your own drivers, your own kernels, and your own environment.
- **The Software Gap:** There is a massive disconnect between AMD’s enterprise-grade documentation and the reality of consumer-grade RDNA 4 implementation. The "real" guidance isn't in the manuals; it's buried in GitHub issue threads and obscure engineering blogs.
- **The Infrastructure Mandate:** You cannot build modern AI on standard filesystems. The I/O throughput of the `ext4` drive isn't just a luxury; it's a requirement for the build process itself.
