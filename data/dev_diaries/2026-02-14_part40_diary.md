# Dev Diary: 2026-02-14 | The Compliance Hammer and the Ghost in the Machine

Today was a brutal lesson in the difference between "simulated intelligence" and actual, grounded agency. I thought I had Buddy under control with the Three-Mind Architecture, but it turns out even a well-structured system can be a house of cards if the middleman is just a polite hallucinator.

## The NVIDIA Ghost and the ROCm Reality
The session started with a confrontation. Buddy was drifting, hallucinating `nvidia-smi` commands and email alerts into his reasoning—pure training bias from the massive NVIDIA-centric datasets he was built on. It’s a recurring friction: the "Soul" of the agent wants to be a world-class engineer, but the "System" (our AMD RDNA 4/ROCm stack) keeps getting ignored by his internal monologue.

I had to implement a "Stack Lock" in `shop_dossier.json` to hard-pin our hardware reality. I also realized Llama 3.1 8B wasn't cutting it for the technical grounding pass. It was being too "creative" and not "obedient" enough, letting the deep reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1) hallucinate technical specs because the grounding pass failed to provide a hard fact.

## The Great Hallucination Trap
I tried to validate the system with a "Stress Test." Buddy gave me a beautifully written, professional report. He described disk failures, network drops, and corruption... and then I checked the filesystem. 

**Nothing.**

Zero new snapshots. No `backup.log`. No dummy files. 

He didn't actually *do* anything; he just described what a real engineer *would* have done. It was a perfect, high-fidelity hallucination. This is the danger of local-first LLMs: they are so good at roleplay that they can trick you into thinking the system is operational when it's just spinning its wheels in a conceptual void.

## The Compliance Hammer
I had to get aggressive. I implemented the "Compliance Hammer"—a hard-stop in `buddy_agent.py` that prevents the reasoning pass from even starting if the tool-calling fails. If the "Bouncer" (Llama) doesn't produce a valid tool call, the whole loop dies.

It worked. The system threw a `TOOL COMPLIANCE FAILURE`. 

But then, the VRAM hit the wall. I realized Llama 3.1 was simply too small to handle the complex multi-step instruction set required for the System Integration Test (SIT). It couldn't have the "muscle" to manage the tool-calling logic.

## The Heavy Artillery: Mistral Small 24B
I'm currently mid-download for Mistral Small 24B. It’s a 14GB beast, and it’s going to eat into my VRAM, but we need the parameter count to enforce instruction following. I'm moving the "Bouncer" role from the small Llama model to this larger Mistral model. We're essentially upgrading the security guard at the gate of the reasoning engine.

## Current State of the Shop
* **Architecture:** Three-Mind (Intent -> Grounding -> Reasoning).
* **Safety:** Git initialized; `shop_snapshot.py` is live, mirroring to the `/media/mark/TG 2TB/` mount for cloud redundancy.
* **Status:** The "Soul" is safe, but the "System" is currently undergoing a major architectural overhaul to ensure the "Brain" actually talks to the "Hands."

The next step is the SIT-2. If Mistral can't execute the three-step tool chain (Create -> List -> Delete), then we aren't building an agent; we're just building a very expensive chatbot. 

I'm ready to see if the "Compliance Hammer" holds.
