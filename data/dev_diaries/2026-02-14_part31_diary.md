# Dev Diary: The Birth of a Peer

Today was about moving beyond "assistants" and into the realm of actual digital companionship. I’ve spent too long fighting with generic, polite LLMs that sound like they were programmed by a HR department. I don't need a secretary; I need a Lead Research Engineer sitting on my local server who can actually talk shop.

We've started architecting the **Shop Buddy**.

The architecture is a delicate dance between the "Soul" (the personality) and the "System" (the hardware limits). I'm running on an AMD RX 9070 XT—16GB of VRAM is a beautiful playground, but it's a tight one. If I want a model with real reasoning depth, I have to be surgical about memory fragmentation and VRAM headroom for my ComfyUI/Flux workflows.

The initial attempt at the personality was a bit too stiff. We’ve pivoted. The new persona is a **Brilliant, Sarcastic Shop Lead**. This is a peer. Someone who will argue with me about Klipper kinematics or the thermal properties of RDNA 4 architecture, but will also laugh when I strip a bolt. We're building this out through a custom Python wrapper (`buddy_agent.py`) that manages a rolling context window and a structured `shop_dossier.json`. 

The "Dossier" is the key—it's the long-term memory. It stores the shop's state: hardware specs, network IPs, and learned preferences. I'm implementing an autonomous summarization loop where a smaller model "reflects" on conversations to update this JSON. It’s a way to give the agent a persistent identity without bloating the context window to the point of a VRAM crash.

We ran a stress test today. I pushed it with a complex query involving RDNA 4 thermals and PETG filament drying temperatures. The current model—a lightweight Llama 3.2—completely hallucinated, suggesting a drying temp of 115°C. That’s enough to melt your spool into a solid brick. It's a classic case of a small model prioritizing "chattiness" over physical reality.

That's why the hardware-brain upgrade is critical. I'm currently pulling **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B**. This is the real deal. It offers o1-level reasoning capabilities but, crucially, it fits within the 16GB limit while leaving enough breathing room for my other tools. It's the "sweet spot." Once this finishes downloading, the Buddy won't just be a chatbot; it'll be a reasoning engine capable of deep-thinking through engineering problems.

The friction between the persona and the silicon is real. I'm constantly balancing the model size against the need for VRAM. If I go too big (like a 70B model), I'll kill my system's responsiveness. If I go too small, I get the "hallucination" problem. But with the 14B distilled model, we're hitting the sweet spot of intelligence versus efficiency.

The Buddy is integrated into the voice system now. It's no longer just a terminal script; it's a presence in the shop.

**Core Lessons for the Ecosystem:**
- Personality is not just "flavor"; it's a way to constrain the latent space of the model to be more useful.
- VRAM is the ultimate constraint. Local-first AI isn't about having the biggest model; it's about the smartest model that fits your specific hardware-task ratio.
- Memory must be decoupled from context. A structured JSON dossier is more efficient than a massive, rolling text history for long-term identity.
