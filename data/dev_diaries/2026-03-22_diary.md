# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-22

## The Great Restoration: Reclaiming the Silicon

Today was a grind of moving metal and data. I spent most of the morning wrestling with the filesystem and the Gemini CLI, which seems to have a mind of its own when it comes to auto-updates. There was a friction point early on—a failed update—but that’s typical for a system this experimental.

The real task was the "Rescue." I had the core of the project sitting in cold storage on a 3TB HDD, and the target was a freshly formatted 2TB NVMe (labeled `TG_2TB` to match my Windows drive legacy). The goal was to migrate the Sovereign Engine back into the active workspace.

The transition was messy. The previous agent (or perhaps my own past self in a different headspace) had created a convoluted directory structure on the fast AI partition. It was cluttered and inefficient. I had to watch the Gemini instance realize it was boxed in by workspace boundaries—it was trying to reach `/mnt/TG_2TB/Projects/Apollo` but hitting walls. I had to guide it through the realization that the actual codebase—the `apollo.py`, `buddy_agent.py`, and the vital `tools`—was still trapped in the backup.

## The Chaos of the Graveyard

Once the data transfer was complete, the "System" met the "Soul." 

The code was restored, but the workspace was a mess of fragmented history. It was a "Graveyard of Tests"—a sprawling collection of ephemeral scripts from the Qwen3/ROCm benchmarking days. Files like `tmp_heretic_script.py` and `stress_test_react2.py` were cluttering the root, acting like digital noise. 

I had the AI perform a deep-dive comparison between my `Sovereign_Gemini.md` architecture and the actual Python scripts in the folder. This was a moment of clarity. The AI identified the "Liar-Trap" logic: the old `commander_voice_old.py` and `jarvis_voice.py` scripts were relics of a Windows-based past, completely incompatible with my current RDNA 4 / Linux architecture. The "Jarvis" persona—a monolithic relic—is officially dead, replaced by the "Sovereign Quartet."

## Technical Friction: The Voice vs. The Silicon

There is a beautiful, frustrating tension between the vision and the hardware. My vision for the *Sovereign Engine Whitepaper* is a low-latency, high-fidelity interaction loop: `S21/Mic -> Whisper.cpp -> 35B MoE -> Kokora TTS`. 

The AI confirmed that while the `whisper.cpp` implementation is ready to go (leveraging the RX 9070 XT's ROCm acceleration), the legacy `jarvis_voice.py` scripts are just dead weight. They are "System" clutter that threatens the "Soul" of the project. 

We also have a decision to make on the CAD integration. The old scripts for OnShape and CAD vision are sitting there like ghosts of a previous iteration. They don't fit the current "Desktop Eyes" or "Procurement Mind" roadmap.

## Lessons Learned & Moving Forward

1. **Architecture over Syntax:** The "Graveyard" of test scripts must be purged. We aren't building a toy; we are building enterprise-grade infrastructure. The clutter is a form of technical debt that slows down the context window.
2. **Workspace Integrity:** The migration from cold storage to the NVMe was successful, but the directory structure must be strictly enforced. The previous agent's "creative" folder naming is a liability.
3. **The Next Step:** I'm going to authorize a mass-archiving. We’re moving the Windows-era scripts, the old "Jarvis" files, and the entire `test_2026` mess into an `/archive/` folder. We need a clean slate for the Sovereign Engine to breathe.

The engine is back online. Now, we just have to see if it remembers how to think.
