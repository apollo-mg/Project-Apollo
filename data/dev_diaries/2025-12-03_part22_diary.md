# December 3, 2025: The RDNA 4 Baseline

We tried to go clean. 

The goal was simple: strip away the layers of custom patches and hacks we’ve been layering onto the system to see if the "Soul" of the project—the actual creative output—was being choked by our own complexity. We wanted a baseline. A stock ComfyUI environment, pure and untouched, to see if Wan 2.1 could even breathe on this hardware without us holding its hand.

It was a disaster from the first command.

The "System" fought back immediately. We hit the classic dependency hell. `torchaudio` was missing, a standard enough headache, but then the hardware-level friction started. We tried to pull a nightly wheel to fix it, and the disk hit a wall. A 4GB wheel download triggered a "Disk Full" error mid-install. It’s a frustrating reminder that even when we’re dreaming of massive multi-agent local ecosystems, we are still tethered to the brutal reality of partition sizes and cache management.

We made a tactical decision: skip the audio. We don't need sound to fix the video glitching. We prioritized the visual "Soul" over the completeness of the suite.

Then came the "odd" output. 

Even with a clean environment, the video was garbage. Distorted. Tiled. It looked like a psychedelic fever dream rather than a coherent video. I knew it wasn't just a driver quirk; it was deeper. It was the silicon itself struggling with the way the code was asking for memory.

On RDNA 4 (gfx1201), the memory geometry is different. The Wan 2.1 VAE uses `Conv3d` operations that rely on sliced, non-contiguous chunks of video data. On NVIDIA, this is a non-issue. On our RDNA 4 beast, the hardware was reading those strided memory accesses incorrectly. It wasn't crashing—which is almost more annoying—it was just producing visual garbage. It was reading the wrong parts of the tensor.

We had to perform surgery.

First, we applied the **VAE Contiguity Patch**. We injected code into `vae.py` to force the tensors into a contiguous memory layout before the convolution hits the hardware. It’s a performance tax, but it’s the tax we pay for sanity.

But the distortion didn't stop there. The motion was still "flashy"—strobe-like artifacts that looked like the video was fighting itself. This was the attention mechanism. The stock PyTorch attention kernels were playing havoc with the RDNA 4 instruction set. 

The final strike was the **Attention Patch**. We had to manually force the `mem_efficient` kernel in `attention.py`. We had to teach the software how to actually talk to this specific architecture.

The result is a strange, hybrid beast. A "Clean" installation that is, ironically, highly patched. It’s a minimal, stable baseline, but "minimal" is a relative term when you’re working on the bleeding edge of local AI. We’ve built a foundation that isn't "stock," but it’s *our* stock. 

We finally have a baseline. Now we see if the machine can actually create, or if we’re just endlessly patching the holes in the silicon.
