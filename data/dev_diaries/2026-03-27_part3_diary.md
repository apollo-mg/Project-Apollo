# Dev Diary: 2026-03-27 (Part 3)

The day was a rollercoaster of efficiency-chasing and philosophical dead-ends. I spent most of the afternoon testing the "intelligence density" of my local models, and the results are both fascinating and deeply frustrating. 

### The Efficiency Paradox: Speed vs. Density

I spent the first part of the afternoon benchmarking the GigaChat 10B MoE. It’s a speed demon. On my RX 9070 XT, it clocked in at a staggering 120 tokens per second. It’s "blink and you miss it" fast—faster than I can even read. But it’s a hollow speed. It's essentially a high-speed processing engine that lacks the cognitive weight for complex tasks. It's a "Computer's LLM"—perfect for background log-scanning or high-speed routing, but it’s not something I’d want to talk to.

Then I moved to the Crow 9B. It's much slower (~45 t/s), but it feels *heavier*. It's a dense model, meaning every single token is the result of all 9 billion neurons firing. It’s smarter, but the VRAM footprint is almost identical to the GigaChat. I'm starting to see the fundamental trade-off of local-first AI: you don't get a free lunch. You either get a fast, lightweight agent or a slow, heavy thinker.

### The Intelligence Trap: A Lesson in Over-Engineering

The highlight (or lowlight) of the day was a direct comparison of how these models handle a "trick" riddle. I gave them the classic water jug problem, but with a twist: I demanded they over-engineer the solution before giving the obvious answer.

The 35B MoE model was brilliant. It didn't just solve the puzzle; it entered a deep, pseudo-scientific spiral. It actually started discussing Diophantine equations and the thermodynamics of water density before realizing the solution was trivial. It showed actual *meta-cognition*—it realized it was being silly.

The GigaChat 10B, however, fell into a classic "hallucination trap." It attempted the "complicated" part by imagining a world of laser-guided sensors and pressure-valves, but it lost its grip on reality halfway through. It tried to "pour water off a scale"—a physical impossibility that exposed its lack of grounded logic. It was "thinking," but it wasn't "reasoning."

### Implementation: The Crow Benchmark

I’ve officially set up the benchmark script for the Crow 9B HERETIC. I've integrated the Unsloth parameters—specifically the aggressive "Thinking Mode" (high temperature, heavy presence penalty). It’s a heavy-duty setup, but it’s the only way to see if the "Intelligence Density" of the 9B dense model can actually out-reason the 35B MoE.

### Architectural Takeaway

The day's experiments have solidified my vision for the Apollo architecture. I'm not building a single-model system. I'm building an **Agentic Swarm**.

The "Soul" of the project is the high-density models like Crow—the ones that provide the reasoning and the nuance. The "System" is the high-speed engines like GigaChat—the ones that handle the grunt work. I'm learning that "Local-First" isn't just about privacy; it's about managing the friction between these two layers. The 9070 XT is a beast, but it's a beast that requires very careful orchestration of VRAM.

Tomorrow, I'll see if the Crow 9B can handle the jug riddle. If it can, the "Brain" of Project Apollo is officially alive.
