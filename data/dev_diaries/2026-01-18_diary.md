# Project Apollo: The Tri-Z Calibration Grind

**2026-01-18**

Today was about the friction between the dream and the silicon. I finally got the Tri-Z hardware mounted on the modified Ender 6 frame. It’s a beast of a setup—three massive Z-axis motors, but the complexity of the driver-to-motor mapping is already causing a headache.

I'm working with Gemini CLI to manage the configuration. The machine is currently running an Octopus Pro, and I’ve got a mixed bag of drivers: two TMC2209s and a TMC5160A. This is the first real test of the architecture's ability to handle heterogeneous hardware.

The "System" (the Klipper firmware) was initially confused by the driver assignments. I had to map out the exact physical slots:
- Slot 3: Z (Front-Left) - TMC2209
- Slot 4: Z1 (Rear) - TMC2209
- Slot 5: Z2 (Front-Right) - TMC5160A

The mismatch between the different driver protocols (UART vs SPI) was a major point of friction. I had to manually define the `[tmc5160]` section for the Z2 driver, specifically worrying about the sense resistor value. Gemini and I cross-referenced the BTT TMC5160T Pro v1.0 datasheet, and luckily, the `0.075` resistor value holds up. 

The real technical bottleneck, however, is the mechanical math. I initially had a placeholder `rotation_distance` of 40, which is standard for belt-driven systems. But I'm running TR8x8 lead screws. If I don't fix that, the motors will move at a snail's pace or grind themselves into dust. I've corrected the configuration to a `rotation_distance` of 8 to match the lead screw pitch.

Now comes the "Soul" of the machine: the `z_tilt` calibration. This is where the geometry of the machine meets the logic of the firmware. Klipper needs to know the exact X/Y pivot points of the lead screws to understand how the bed is tilting. It's a brutal amount of manual measurement. I need to find the exact coordinate of the center of each screw relative to the nozzle (0,0).

It's a tedious, manual process, but it's the only way to bridge the gap between the physical reality of the hardware and the digital precision required for a perfect print. The transition from a single-Z cantilevered bed to a true Tri-Z is a massive leap in mechanical stability, but it turns the configuration into a high-stakes geometry problem.

**Current Status:**
- Hardware: Mounted (Tri-Z setup)
- Drivers: Mapped (Mix of 2209/5160)
- Firmware: Config updated (Rotation distance fixed)
- Next Step: Precise measurement of lead screw pivot points for `z_tilt` calibration.
