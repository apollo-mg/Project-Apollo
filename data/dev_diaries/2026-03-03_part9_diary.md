### THE SILICON AWAKENING (vLLM Build)

For weeks, we’ve been shackled to the Ollama runtime layer. It’s convenient, but it’s a bottleneck when you’re trying to squeeze every last bit of FP8 performance out of the RX 9070 XT. I decided to stop playing nice with the dependencies.

The build process was a war. CMake threw a tantrum over version mismatches, refusing to acknowledge the Ninja generator. The cache was corrupted by previous, half-hearted attempts. I had to nuke the `CMakeCache.txt` and the `_build` directories, essentially digging a fresh grave for the compiler to bury itself in.

But when it finally linked? Magic.
We are now running a custom-compiled `vllm==0.16.1rc1` against `gfx1201`. This isn't just "it works." This is the **FP8 WMMA hardware accelerators** firing on all cylinders. We bypassed the quantization overhead that was chewing up our VRAM. The system feels lighter, faster. The friction between the "Soul" (our intent) and the "System" (the hardware) has finally been bridged.

### THE HOARD & THE COLD STORAGE

My SSD was choking. I had it at 100% utilization, bleeding out space to accommodate the growing hoard of models. It was a matter of time before the whole stack collapsed.

I made the hard call: **Tiered Storage.**
We moved the heavyweights—the 80B MoE, DeepHermes-3, and the new GLM-4.7—out of the fast NVMe and onto the **2TB Cold Storage HDD**. It’s slower, sure, but it’s massive. I reclaimed 180GB by emptying the hidden `.Trash-1000` folder that was sitting there like a ghost, consuming space. The SSD is breathing again. The hoard is organized, sequential, and no longer threatening to crash the root partition.

### THE TUNING PROTOCOL & THE PATH PROBLEM

We’re entering the phase of **Offline TunableOp**. The idea is simple: instead of letting the GPU guess the best kernels on the first run (which causes that annoying "first-run penalty" where the system hangs), we manually benchmark and cache the optimal paths.

I tried to run this against `lovedheart/Qwen3.5-9B-FP8`. The script choked. Why? Because the `HF_HOME` variable was pointing to a path that didn't exist (`/media/mark/TG 2TB/hf_cache` vs `/media/mark/TG 2TB/model_hoard`). The model was there, but the system couldn't find it. I had to manually hunt down the files, verify the GGUF integrity, and then re-attempt. It’s a messy process, but necessary. We need those CSV tuning profiles saved alongside the models so we never have to re-tune on a fresh install.
