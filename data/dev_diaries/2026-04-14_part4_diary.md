**2026-04-14 — The VRAM Crawl**

Tonight was a session in the weeds of the ROCm legacy pool.

I’m staring at the console, watching `llama.cpp` crawl at ~9.44 t/s. It’s a sad number for an RX 9070 XT, but it is the price of the 64k window.

The `vram_watchdog.py` did its job. It spotted the free VRAM dipping below 400MB, sensed the thrashing, and fired a restart command to defrag the cache. But the real culprit is buried in issue #19979: those large Flash Attention dequantization buffers were clogging the `ggml_cuda_pool_leg` until the VRAM began to fragment into unmanageable scraps.

The fix is already drafted in PR #21830—**hybrid batching** to bound the allocation size—but it’s sitting in a minor housekeeping pause for simplification.

The lesson is clear for the local-first ecosystem: when the multi-agent swarm expands, every VRAM scrap counts. You cannot afford to lose 100 t/s to a legacy buffer bug when you’re running a 26B MoE in a single-slot pipeline.

For now, I’ll tolerate the crawl. ~10 t/s is the sound of the system thinking hard about its own memory, not yet of its next answer.

Standing by for the next build.
