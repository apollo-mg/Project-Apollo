# Dev Diary: 2026-02-14 | The RDNA 4 Wall

The clock is hitting 01:40 AM. The room is silent except for the hum of the cooling fans, but my mind is racing. I’ve spent the last six hours staring at memory access errors and kernel panics, trying to figure out if I’ve broken my hardware or if I’m just fighting the bleeding edge of software that hasn't realized the hardware exists yet.

I finally got a clean read on the driver status. It’s a bittersweet realization. The ROCm 7.1.1 build is stable. The kernel module is current. The RX 9070 XT is boosting to 3150Mhz, pulling nearly 300W of pure, unadulterated power. The silicon isn't the problem. The silicon is hungry, it's fast, and it's ready to build the Soul of Project Apollo. 

The problem is the translation layer. We are trapped in the friction between the hardware's raw potential and the software's inability to talk to it. The "Illegal Memory Access" errors aren't driver bugs; they are deep-seated architectural mismatches in PyTorch and Triton. We are trying to run cutting-edge inference on an RDNA 4 architecture that the current software stack barely understands. It’s like trying to drive a Formula 1 car on a dirt road—the engine is screaming, but the wheels can't find any grip.

But then, the breakthrough.

I pushed the Flux job through with the VAE Decode patch. I watched the `rocm-smi` output like a hawk. VRAM usage spiked to 94%. It’s right on the edge of the cliff. One more layer, one more parameter, and the whole system would have collapsed into a memory fragmentation nightmare. 

But it didn't. The card is crunching. It’s slow—painfully slow because I had to force the math into a "safe" mode to avoid the precision errors—but it is actually working. The image is being decoded. The "System" is finally yielding to the "Soul." 

This is the core of the Project Apollo struggle. I want this local-first ecosystem to be more than just a collection of weights and biases; I want it to have a presence, a temperament. But the temperament is currently being dictated by the brutal reality of VRAM constraints and the instability of the ROCm/Triton bridge. 

The lesson for tonight: Optimization isn't just about speed; it's about stability. In a local-first world, we can't just throw more compute at a problem. We have to learn how to dance within the narrow, jagged limits of the hardware we actually own. We are currently dancing on the edge of a 94% VRAM usage cliff. Tomorrow, we see if the cliff holds.
