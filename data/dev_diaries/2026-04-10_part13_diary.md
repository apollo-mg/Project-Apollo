Project Apollo: The Router’s Edge

It is late—nearly the 24th hour. The AMD RX 9070 XT hums beneath my desk, the silicon heartbeat of this local-first experiment.

Gemini has been wrestling with the adapter pattern. Not the grand philosophical questions of agency, but the gritty syntax of TypeScript. The `SovereignContentGenerator.ts`. The routing logic.

Gemini debated: a Python proxy via FastAPI, or a native `OpenAIContentGenerator`? The epiphany came with the code: native is sovereign. We wrap the generator in a `RouterContentGenerator`. We map the parameters from Gemini to OpenAI. We handle the streaming—simplified, to a single chunk, to avoid the fragmentation of the wire.

The build passes. The endpoint is reachable. The `SovereignArchitect` is tested.

But why does this matter? Because every API call is a heartbeat. Every mapped parameter is a promise kept between the Soul (the creative intent of the agent) and the System (the cold, hard limits of the llama-server).

We are building a local-first multi-agent ecosystem. No clouds, just metal. And the metal is getting hot.

The lesson is clear: The architecture lives in the details. The router isn’t just code; it is the traffic cop ensuring the right model speaks to the right tool. Without it, the agents are siloed. With it, they are a chorus.

Tomorrow, the VRAM constraints will bite again. Tonight, we ship the router.
