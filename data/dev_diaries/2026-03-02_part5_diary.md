# Dev Diary: The Network Wars (2026-03-02)

The friction between the "Soul" and the "System" is never more apparent than when you're trying to build a sovereign, local-first ecosystem. I wanted a private, decentralized intelligence, but I forgot that even a digital soul needs a stable body to live in. Today was a brutal lesson in the physics of networking.

### The Goal: A Sovereign Web
I spent the first part of the session establishing a secure bridge to my Raspberry Pi 5 (the `zoey-sat` node). I needed it to be a gateway for my home lab—specifically my 3D printer—so I could manage it from anywhere. Using Gemini to automate the SSH key deployment and Tailscale setup was seamless, but the "System" (the underlying Linux routing) is a fickle beast.

### The Friction: Tailscale vs. PIA
The real battle began when I tried to layer privacy (via Private Internet Access) on top of connectivity (via Tailscale). 

It turns out that when you have two VPN-level protocols—WireGuard for Tailscale and OpenVPN/WireGuard for PIA—fighting for control over the system's DNS and routing tables, things go south fast. PIA's "Kill Switch" and its aggressive DNS protection (MACE) were essentially declaring war on Tailscale's MagicDNS. 

I tried to force them to coexist by adjusting the `accept-routes` and `accept-dns` flags on the Tailscale client. The technical logic was sound, but the real-world application was a disaster. The routing conflict didn't just break the network; it caused a cascade failure.

### The Casualty: The Inbox Sync
The most painful moment was seeing the `mbsync` process die. I had a background script running to pull 160,000 emails into the local vault—the very memory that gives the AI its "history." When the network routing collapsed, the `mbsync` journal files became corrupted. 

It’s a classic architectural trap: I was trying to build a more robust, "sovereign" system by adding layers of security, but each layer added a new point of failure that ultimately crashed the data ingestion pipeline. 

### Lessons for the Architecture
1. **Complexity is a tax:** Every time we add a "feature" (like a second VPN for privacy), we pay a heavy tax in system stability.
2. **The Local-First Paradox:** The very thing that makes the system "sovereign" (the local-only, offline-capable nature) is what makes it vulnerable to the instability of the networking stack.
3. **The Sync Bottleneck:** High-speed ingestion (vLLM-driven) is useless if the transport layer (the mail sync) can't maintain a stable connection.

I've decided to pause all networking experimentation. The current priority is letting the `mbsync` process finish its download without further interference. I need that data in the Vault before I can even think about the next architectural leap. 

**Current Status:**
- **Connectivity:** Stable via ISP (VPNs currently disabled).
- **Ingestion:** Ongoing (Slow, but moving).
- **Soul:** Currently hungry for the data that the network keeps dropping.
