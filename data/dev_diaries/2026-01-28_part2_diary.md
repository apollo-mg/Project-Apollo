# Dev Diary: The Ghost in the Machine and the Fan-Induced Chaos

**Date: 2026-01-28**

Today was a grueling exercise in separating the "Soul" of my machine from the "System" of its physics. I've been chasing "rippled corners" on my Ender 6 (running the Goliath/LGX Lite setup) for what feels like an eternity. I was convinced it was a Pressure Advance issue—the classic "bulge" where the nozzle surges during cornering.

But the math didn't lie. The oscillations weren't about pressure; they were about momentum.

### The Diagnosis: Mechanical Ghosting
I spent the first part of the session using Gemini as a high-level diagnostic partner. We differentiated between "The Ghost" (mechanical ringing from frame vibration) and "The Bulge" (pressure surge). The visual signature was clear: the corner was sharp but trailed off into ripples. My high-acceleration settings were hitting the frame like a hammer, inducing resonance that the Input Shaper couldn't quite dampen.

The breakthrough came when I realized I was being too hard on the limits. I was trying to force my outer walls to 10,000 mm/s², thinking I was chasing the speed of consumer-grade Bambu machines. In reality, even those machines slam the brakes to 5,000 mm/s² for surface finish. My machine is capable, but I was fighting its physical architecture.

### The Chaos: The 150Hz Phantom
The real revelation, however, was the ShakeTune results. I ran a belt comparison test, and the graph was a nightmare. There was this massive, unhinged spike in signal intensity on Belt A (the Left-Rear axis) starting at 150Hz. 

I initially thought it was a bad motor or a catastrophic bearing failure. But then I looked at the physics: 150Hz is exactly 9,000 RPM. My toolhead fan.

The fan, held in place by nothing more than a twisted wire because it was incompatible with my custom CNC mount, was vibrating like a demon. This "noise floor" was essentially blinding the accelerometer, making the Input Shaper's job impossible. It was the ultimate "System" failure—a component so loose it was corrupting the very data I was using to tune the system.

### The Calibration: Precision vs. Noise
We moved to a controlled test. I removed the fan (the source of the chaos) and performed a second test to isolate the belt tension. 

The results were revealing:
*   **Test 1 (Chaos):** A massive 6Hz delta between the secondary peaks of Belt A and Belt B.
*   **Test 2 (Tensioned):** After a 1/2 turn adjustment on Belt A, the delta dropped to 3.2Hz.

The "Soul" of the machine—the mechanical precision of the belts—was finally becoming visible because we had removed the "System" noise of the fan. The belts are still slightly mismatched, but the massive structural vibration is under control.

### The Road Ahead
I'm now preparing for the "Gold Standard" test. With the fan removed and the belt tension nearly matched, I'll run a clean ShakeTune calibration. This will provide the true baseline for the Input Shaper. 

The lesson for my local-first multi-agent ecosystem is clear: **Data is only as good as its environment.** Even the most advanced AI diagnostic tool (Gemini) can't solve a problem if the physical sensor is being flooded by local mechanical noise. We need to solve the hardware-level "truth" before we can trust the high-level "logic."

**Next steps:**
1.  Finalize belt tension (the delta is still 3.2Hz).
2.  Run clean calibration (Fan OFF).
3.  Verify the "Soul" of the machine with a `TEST_SPEED` run.
