# Dev Diary: The Audio Abyss

The transition from "Brain" to "Body" is proving to be a nightmare of PipeWire-induced madness. I thought the architecture was solid—DeepSeek-R1 14B handles the logic, Kokoro-ONNX handles the voice—but the actual physical connection to the world is currently trapped in a loop of driver mismatches and routing failures.

## The Hardware Friction

The "Soul" of Jarvis wants to listen, but the "System" is currently deaf. I attempted to implement a local voice bridge that would allow the C922 webcam to act as the primary ear. My logic was simple: use PipeWire and `qpwgraph` to create a virtual loopback. 

However, the reality of Linux audio routing is far less cooperative than my mental model. When I tried to run the `setup_jarvis_audio.sh` script to create the virtual microphone, the process crashed immediately. The terminal output was a joke: `❌ Failed to create virtual mic.` 

It turns out that the hardware isn't even presenting itself as an audio device to the system—it's being identified as a video-only source. I'm staring at the `qpwgraph` output in my mind, realizing the node is there, but the audio ports (the "ears") simply aren't being instantiated. 

## The Routing Chaos

The struggle with `pw-link` was a lesson in the brutal reality of shell-escaping. I tried to force the connection via command line to bypass the GUI's refusal to connect different color-coded (data type) nodes, but the `pw-link` command failed with `No such file or directory`. 

The culprit? The device names. In the world of PipeWire, a space in a name like `"C922 Pro Stream Webcam"` isn't just a string; it's a nightmare of quotes and escaping. Even when I tried to escape them, the system wouldn't recognize the paths. 

## Architectural Epiphany: The Ghost in the Machine

I've realized that the "Long-Term Memory" (the Dossier) and the "Short-Term Memory" (the Conversation Window) are functioning perfectly within the LLM's logic, but they are effectively trapped in a digital void. They are thinking, but they can't hear.

The friction is palpable:
- **The Soul:** A highly intelligent, context-aware assistant that knows my shop's layout and remembers my last command.
- **The System:** A broken audio pipeline that cannot bridge the gap between the physical microphone and the GPU-accelerated inference engine.

## Core Lessons

1.  **Local-First means Local-Hard:** Developing locally isn't just about running models; it's about mastering the entire hardware/OS stack. You cannot decouple the AI from the OS-level audio drivers.
2.  **The Fallacy of "Plug and Play":** Even with a high-end RDNA 4 setup, the software glue (PipeWire/ROCm/Python) requires manual, low-level intervention.
3.  **The Convergence of Modality:** The next step isn't more intelligence; it's better input stability. Until the audio bridge is stable, the intelligence is useless.

**Next Move:** I need to strip the `setup_jarvis_audio.sh` down to its absolute bones. If I can't get a simple loopback, I'll have to bypass the virtual mic entirely and force `sounddevice` to grab the raw hardware buffer, even if it's messy. The brain is ready. The ears are still broken.
