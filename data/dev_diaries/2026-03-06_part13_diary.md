# Dev Diary: The 16GB Wall and the RDNA 4 Breakthrough

**Date:** 2026-03-06
**Project:** Apollo
**Status:** Stabilized (with caveats)

The transition from "Experimental" to "Production-Ready" is never a straight line. Today was a violent collision between high-level architectural goals and the brutal, unyielding limits of consumer-grade silicon. We finally broke through the vLLM bottleneck, but at the cost of a massive realization: we are fighting for every single megabyte of VRAM.

### The vLLM Breakthrough
We spent the first half of the session pushing the RX 9070 XT to its absolute limits. The goal was to validate the RDNA 4 FP8 compute capabilities. The result was nothing short of spectacular. Using the specialized `rocm/vllm-dev:rocm7.2_navi` Docker image, we hit **198.5 TPS** on the Neural Magic Qwen 2.5 7B model. This is the "Gold Standard" for our local-first ecosystem. It proves the hardware is capable of high-throughput, production-level inference.

The "Soul" (the intelligence) and the "System" (the 16GB VRAM) are currently in a deadlock. We achieved the performance we wanted, but the overhead of the resident Gatekeeper (0.6B) and the OS makes scaling larger models a nightmare.

### The Image Generation Odyssey
After securing the text-based intelligence, we pivoted to "Art Sovereignty." We successfully deployed a clean ComfyUI instance, mapped to the massive 3TB model archive. This was intended to be the next pillar of Apollo—high-speed, batched image generation.

But the "AMD Consumer Curse" is real. We hit a hard OOM (Out of Memory) crash immediately upon attempting to run the Flux.1 Schnell (FP8) benchmark. 

The math is unforgiving:
- **Flux UNet (FP8):** ~12.0 GB
- **CLIP/T5 (FP8):** ~4.6 GB
- **Resident Gatekeeper + OS Overhead:** ~2.0 GB
- **Total Required:** **~18.6 GB**

We are trying to fit a 19GB brain into a 16GB skull. The crash wasn't a failure of the code, but a failure of the *strategy*. We were trying to run "High-VRAM" mode, which attempted to pin all weights to the GPU simultaneously.

### Engineering Pivot: The "Sovereign Shift"
We are correcting the course. We've implemented a dynamic swapping strategy:
1. **Kill the Gatekeeper:** We'll temporarily sacrifice the resident model to clear the VRAM floor.
2. **Enforce `--normalvram`:** We'll force ComfyUI to offload the heavy T5 encoder to system memory, freeing up space for the UNet to sample.
3. **Optimization Flags:** Using `--pin-shared-memory` to mitigate the latency of these swaps.

### Core Lessons & Path Forward
- **Hardware Reality:** The RX 9070 XT is a beast for throughput, but its 16GB VRAM is a hard ceiling for modern multi-model pipelines. We cannot run "everything at once." Our architecture must become more modular—swapping models in and out of VRAM rather than trying to maintain a resident multi-agent state.
- **Environment Management:** The recent crash caused a drive remount (`AI_Fast1`), highlighting the need for more robust mount-point handling in our automation scripts.
- **Next Milestone:** Re-validate the Flux FP8 generation with the new memory-management strategy. If we can get Flux to run without a crash, we've officially mastered the RDNA 4 pipeline.

**Current Status:** The system is stable, the logs are clean, and the "Art Lab" is ready for a second attempt.

**End of Entry.**
