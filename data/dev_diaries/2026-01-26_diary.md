# Dev Diary: 2026-01-26

## The Silicon vs. The Structure

I spent the early hours of this session fighting the ghost in the machine. I'm currently working on a custom Klipper build for the Ender 6/Goliath hybrid, and even with the RX 9070 XT doing the heavy lifting on the simulation side, the physical reality of the machine is proving to be a stubborn, unyielding bastard.

The input shaper calibration was a revelation, but a frustrating one. I ran the X-axis tests and the results were, frankly, boring. The Carbon Fiber 2020 gantry is so stiff that the resonance peaks are pushed way up past 140Hz. It’s essentially "perfect" in the eyes of the software.

But the Y-axis... that's where the soul of the machine is breaking.

The resonance graphs showed a massive, sharp spike at 78Hz. It's a structural coupling issue—likely the bed assembly fighting the frame. It’s the classic battle: I want a high-speed beast, but the laws of physics want a heavy, vibration-prone anchor. Gemini (my CLI agent) identified the 3HUMP_EI shaper as a way to "wrap" around that 80Hz noise. It’s a mathematical trick—using a wide-bandwidth filter to suppress the low-frequency chaos. It worked. It allowed us to jump from a pathetic 5,000 mm/s² to 15,000 mm/s². It’s a win, but it feels like we’re just masking a fundamental flaw rather than fixing it.

Then came the Pressure Advance (PA) disaster.

I ran the calibration tower, expecting the usual subtle tuning, but the results were ugly. Huge, rounded bulges at the corners. My initial thought was that the filament was just acting up, but after digging into the JSON profiles, it became clear: I'm a victim of my own speed.

The `square_corner_velocity` was set to 5. The printer was practically crawling through the corners, giving the pressure in the nozzle time to equalize naturally. It was "hiding" the real pressure lag from the test. It's like trying to tune a racing engine while driving it at 20 mph in second gear—you aren't actually testing the limits; you're just watching it idle.

The rings/ripples on the flat faces are still a headache. Even with the input shaper acting up, those ripples suggest the mechanical vibration is still fighting the motion plan. We've hit a ceiling where the software-driven vibration suppression can't quite keep up with the raw kinetic energy of the high-acceleration moves.

**Lessons for the next sprint:**
- **The Physics of Corners:** High-speed calibration is useless if the motion planner isn't "stressing" the extruder. We need to force those corner velocities up to see the real pressure lag.
- **Structural Bottlenecks:** The Y-axis is still the weak link. The 3HUMP_EI filter is a band-aid, not a cure. 
- **The Convergence:** The goal is a local-first, autonomous tuning agent. We're seeing the friction between the "intelligence" (Gemini's analysis) and the "physicality" (the printer's resonance). The agent can suggest the fix, but the machine's hardware is what ultimately dictates the ceiling of our ambition.

The system is stable, but it's not "fast" yet. We're fighting the limits of the silicon-to-mechanical interface. Next, we tackle the corner velocity.
