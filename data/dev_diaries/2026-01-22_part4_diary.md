# Dev Diary: The Inductive Indirection (2026-01-22)

The silicon is behaving, but the physics are fighting me. I’ve spent the better part of today wrestling with the BTT Eddy, and it has been one of the most frustrating, yet enlightening, hardware-software integration loops I've faced in a while.

### The Ghost in the Frequency
The core issue was a fundamental mismatch between the sensor's physics and the software's expectations. My LDC1612 was throwing an `Invalid probe_eddy_current height` error immediately upon homing. 

In the raw telemetry, I saw the frequency reading was hovering around 12 MHz, while my calibration curve was expecting values near 3.3 MHz. The logic check in Klipper was seeing a frequency-to-distance mapping that was mathematically "out of bounds" because the sensor was seeing "Open Air" (infinity) at a frequency that the curve thought was physically impossible.

I tried everything:
*   **The Current Swing:** I cycled `reg_drive_current` through 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20. It was like chasing a ghost. The sensor's dynamic range was shifting, but it never hit the "sweet spot" where the open-air frequency was valid.
*   **The Macro Loop:** I suspected the `homing_override` was causing a positioning error, so I swapped to `safe_z_home`. No change. The error was instantaneous.

### The Physical Revelation
The breakthrough didn't come from a line of code, but from a ruler. 

I realized the physical gap between the nozzle and the Eddy sensor was ~4.5mm. The calibration curve was only mapping up to 4mm. When the toolhead tried to home, the sensor was already reading "Infinity," which fell outside the curve's bounds. I was trying to run a high-precision inductive sensor with a gap that was wider than its entire calibrated range. It's like trying to use a ruler to measure a mountain when the ruler only goes to 12 inches.

I physically re-mounted the sensor to a 2.2mm gap. 

### The Final Calibration
With the physics corrected, the digital logic finally caught up. I had to:
1.  Reset the `reg_drive_current` to a manual value of **15** (to stabilize the frequency).
2.  Wipe the calibration data entirely.
3.  Perform a fresh `LDC_CALIBRATE_DRIVE_CURRENT` and `PROBE_EDDY_CURRENT_CALIBRATE`.

The result? Success. The `G28` command finally completed without the "Invalid Height" error. The system finally understood where "zero" was because the physical reality now matched the induction field's reach.

### The Lessons
The friction between the **Soul** (the dream of a seamless, automated, high-speed bed mesh) and the **System** (the rigid, unforgiving reality of inductive frequency curves) is real. 

1.  **The Gap Matters:** In local-first AI and high-precision hardware, "close enough" is not a thing. If your physical mounting doesn't match your calibration range, the software will reject the world.
2.  **Parameter Overrides:** The `z_offset` in the main config is just a placeholder; the `SAVE_CONFIG` block is the true source of truth. This is a common pitfall when debugging.
3.  **Complexity vs. Stability:** I spent hours trying to fix KAMP integration when the issue was actually the sensor's drive current. We often chase the most complex thing (the software wrapper) when the simplest thing (the hardware mounting) is the culprit.

The machine is finally functional. It's running a native Adaptive Mesh with a `rapid_scan` method. The "Soul" has finally been captured by the "System." Now, to see if it can actually print.
