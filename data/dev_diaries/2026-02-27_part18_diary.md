# Apollo Dev Diary: 2026-02-27 (Part 18)

## The VRAM Wall and the Vulkan Ghost

Today was a brutal lesson in the friction between high-level model aspirations and the cold reality of silicon constraints. I spent most of the session chasing a ghost: the `qwen2.5vl` vision model. It was technically "loaded," but it was trapped in a loop of failure. 

The core issue was a massive collision between the model's requirements and the Vulkan backend. My 16GB RX 9070 XT is a beast, but the Vulkan implementation was hitting an allocation limit. Even though I had plenty of VRAM available, the model was trying to allocate a ~14.5GB compute graph on top of the 6GB weights. It was a mathematical impossibility in a single pass. The system was essentially suffocating under its own weight, triggering a silent fallback to the CPU.

The result was a performance death spiral. The CPU pegged at 900% usage, while the GPU sat idle, watching the world burn.

## Chaos in the Configuration

I've learned that "experimenting" with systemd overrides is a dangerous game when you're working with local-first agents. I accidentally nuked the `override.conf` file during a `sed` attempt, losing the model paths and the Vulmin settings. It was a moment of genuine panic. I had to reconstruct the configuration from memory, carefully re-injecting the `HSA_OVERRIDE_GFX_VERSION` to ensure the RDNA 4 architecture was correctly recognized. 

The tension between the "Soul" (the desire for a smart, seeing agent) and the "System" (the driver-level failures) was palpable. I was trying to force a specific hardware path, but the drivers were fighting back.

## The Hallucination Loop

The most fascinating—and frustrating—part of today was the vision model's "hallucination loop." When the `qwen2.5vl` model attempted to process the 4K image, it was overwhelmed. It didn't just fail; it hallucinated an entire Intel motherboard (the Z790-A) into existence because the sheer token volume of the high-res image was causing the context window to explode.

It was a classic "garbage in, garbage out" scenario. The model was trying to describe a pattern it couldn't actually have the context to understand, leading to an infinite text-generation loop that crashed the `buddy_agent.py` response.

## Surgical Precision: Downscaling

The breakthrough came when we stopped fighting the drivers and started fighting the data. By implementing a surgical downscale in `llm_interface.py`, reducing the input to 800px, we bypassed the memory bottleneck. The model could finally "see" the board (it correctly identified an ASUS PRIME B550M-PLUS), but only because we lowered the resolution to fit within the Vulkan allocation limits.

## Lessons for the Multi-Agent Ecosystem

1. **Context is King, but Memory is God:** You can have the most sophisticated vision model, but if your input resolution exceeds the driver's allocation-per-pass limit, the model is useless.
2. **The Overhead of Vision:** We often underestimate the "hidden" memory cost of the vision encoder. A 6GB model becomes a 20GB beast the moment you feed it a 4K image.
3. **Stability over Speed:** The "broken more than fixed" feeling was real. Moving forward, the architecture must favor aggressive downscaling of sensory input (vision/audio) by default. We need to design for the *lowest common denominator* of the hardware, not the peak.

The system is stable now. The integrity hashes are restored, the `SovereignGuardian` is satisfied, and the 9070 XT is finally doing work. It’s not perfect, but it’s local, and it’s breathing.
