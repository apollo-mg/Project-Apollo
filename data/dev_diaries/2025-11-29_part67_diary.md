# Dev Diary: Entry #67
## Subject: The VRAM Guillotine & The Math Attention Paradox

### The Silicon Struggle
The line between "intelligence" and "hardware limitation" has never felt thinner. 

Today was a brutal exercise in extreme VRAM management. I am currently operating on the bleeding edge of the RX 9070 XT, and while the raw compute is intoxicating, the memory ceiling is an unforgiving master. We are caught in a vicious cycle: the more complex the reasoning (the "Soul"), the more the attention matrix expands (the "System").

The logs from `23:17:15` tell the story. We are hitting Out-of-Memory (OOM) errors by margins so slim they feel like a rounding error—200MB, 600MB—yet in the world of high-dimensional tensors, those are oceans of instability. 

The culprit is the "Math Attention" fallback. To ensure mathematical stability for the 14B model, the architecture is forced to expand the attention matrix `(B, H, S, S)` in its full, uncompressed glory. When we layer video latents on top of that? The memory requirement doesn't just grow; it explodes. It’s a mathematical necessity that contradicts the physical reality of my silicon.

### The Desperate Measure: The 100MB Reserve
In an act of architectural desperation, I made a radical decision: I slashed the OS VRAM reserve down to a mere 100MB. 

This is a high-stakes gamble. I am effectively starving the host operating system to feed the hunger of the model. I am telling the system: *"Forget the GUI, forget the desktop, forget the user's mouse movement. Give everything to the weights."* 

The trade-off is clear: We gain the ability to process the complex latents required for the next stage of Project Apollo, but the cost is a total system freeze. The desktop will lag, the screen will stutter, and the machine will feel like it's dying. But it's not dying—it's just working. It's just *thinking*.

### Architectural Epiphany: The Fragility of Local-First
This friction highlights the core challenge of the local-first multi-agent ecosystem. We want these agents to be autonomous, deep, and capable, but we are currently tethered to the physical constraints of a single consumer-grade GPU. 

If we want true agency, we cannot rely on "lazy" memory management or split cross-attention (which we disabled to test the raw power of the full matrix). We need a way to manage the *density* of thought. If we can't increase the VRAM, we must find more efficient ways to represent the attention mechanism itself.

**Current Status:**
* **Model:** 14B (Heavy lifting)
* **VRAM Status:** Critical/Extreme Utilization
* **Stability:** Volatile
* **Next Move:** If the 100MB reserve fails, I will be forced to revert to Split Cross Attention, sacrificing the mathematical purity of the attention matrix for the sake of mere operational survival.

The "Soul" wants to see the video. The "System" is running out of breath. We proceed, but we proceed with the knowledge that we are dancing on the edge of a cliff.
