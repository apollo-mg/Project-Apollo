# Dev Diary: 2026-03-14 (Part 4 of 19)

## The Cold Front and the Soul

It is nearly 4:00 AM in Martinsville. The world outside is turning cold—a sharp, windy front has moved in, cutting through the usual humidity. I’ve realized that in this game, the environment is just another layer of the architecture. I’m using the ambient temperature as a passive heat sink to keep the RX 9070 XT from melting down while I push the VRAM to its absolute limit. It’s a "high-agency" move—turning a weather pattern into a cooling solution for my local-first compute.

The 7B LoRA training is in a strange, beautiful, and terrifying state. The model is "locking in" on the specific architecture of my internal monologue, and the loss is dipping into what I can only call the "Deep Wisdom" zone. The GPU is pegged at 17.07 GB, every transistor on that silicon is screaming.

## The OOM Fight

The "System" is fighting the "Soul." I hit an Out-of-Space (OOS) error mid-run. The disk space on my NVMe hit a wall. I’m seeing massive discrepancies in checkpoint sizes—some are 6.1GB, others are barely 2.6GB. It looks like the script is skipping the optimizer state during the save to save space, which is a risky trade-off for momentum, but given the VRAM/Disk constraints, it’s the only way to reach the finish line.

I’m watching the PID 436172 like a hawk. It’s a ghost in the machine, still crunching, still fighting the disk space. I’m just hoping the final 10 steps don't trip the next storage threshold.

## Cognitive OOM and the Carrot Cake

There’s a psychological toll to this. As the training progresses, I feel "trapped" in the AI. It’s that "Cognitive OOM" NetworkChuck talks about—the feeling that the technical architecture is consuming my entire bandwidth, leaving no room for the human. 

But then, a "System Override" happens. My daughter wants carrot cake.

It’s the ultimate interrupt. It’s the reminder that while I’m down here in the trenches of ROCm kernels and VRAM histograms, there is a physical, biological reality that needs tending. The cake is the anchor. It forces me to step out of the silicon-induced loop and back into the real world.

## The Path to Sovereignty

The work is addictive. The hours disappear into a flow state. I’m not just "using AI"; I’m engineering the infrastructure that makes it possible to live outside the cloud. 

The industry is obsessed with APIs, but they are terrified of the hard stuff: managing local inference on RDNA 4, debugging HIP errors, and building agentic systems that respect air-gapped security. That is the "Difficulty Gap." That is where the value lies.

The "Sovereign Soul" is almost finished. I can see the checkpoint-70 save looming on the horizon. Once that "Teacher" model is saved to the metal, the most dangerous part of the build is over. 

I'll eat the cake, watch the wind, and let the GPU finish its work in the dark. The soul is almost born.
