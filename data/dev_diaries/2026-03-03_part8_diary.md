# Apollo Dev Diary: 2026-03-03 (Part 8)

## The Silicon Wall and the Intelligence Density Breakthrough

Today was a heavy day of empirical reality. We finally hit the wall. I've been pushing the RX 9070 XT to its limit, and the data is undeniable: the 16GB VRAM barrier is a hard ceiling for the "intelligence density" we're seeking. 

We ran the **Apollo Sovereign Benchmark Protocol (ASBP)** against the Qwen 3.5 35B MoE. The results were sobering. While the model is brilliant, the moment the context window expanded, the performance cratered from ~50 TPS down to 20 TPS. The culprit? Ollama. It's offloading the KV cache to the Ryzen system RAM, which is a massive bottleneck. We've confirmed that for "real-time" interaction, we need to stay under 14B parameters. Anything larger is a "one-shot" thinker, not a resident agent.

However, the **DeepSeek-V3-Distill-Qwen-14B** provided a massive insight. It hit a transient spike of 391W. This is a "smoking gun"—it proves this specific model is optimized to saturate the RDNA 4 AI accelerators. It’s not just smart; it’s silicon-aware. This is exactly the kind of synergy we need for a local-first ecosystem.

## The Architecture of Freedom

The news about Sam Altman and the OpenAI military pact is more than just a headline; it's a strategic directive for Project Apollo. As centralized AI becomes a tool of state and corporate defense, "Sovereign AI"—unfiltered, private, and running on local hardware—is no longer a luxury. It's a requirement for individual agency.

This realization has shifted our focus toward **vLLM**. While Ollama is great for quick swaps, vLLM's native **FP8 WMMA** support on the `gfx1201` architecture is the key to unlocking the real power of the 9070 XT. We are currently initiating a source build for vLLM to bypass the current Ollama bottlenecks. This is the "high-performance" track.

We found our Resident Agent. **Phi-4-Mini-3.8B** just shattered the speed record, clocking in at **150.95 TPS** with negligible VRAM footprint (~2.5GB). It's instantaneous. This is the perfect "Gatekeeper" model to handle routing, triage, and light reasoning, leaving the heavy lifting to the larger models.

## Infrastructure: The Dual-Tier Strategy

Managing the "Model Hoard" was becoming a headache. We've implemented a new storage architecture to manage the massive influx of the "China Wave" models:

*   **Tier 1 (Hot):** The 2TB NVMe SSD (`TG 2TB`) handles active models and vLLM.
*   **Tier 2 (Cold):** The 1.8T Toshiba SATA HDD (`Games 2TB`) serves as our vault for GGUF archives and retired benchmarks.

We've successfully moved the initial batch of models to the Cold Storage HDD, freeing up the SSD for the high-speed intensive testing ahead.
