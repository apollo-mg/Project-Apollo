But the real epiphany was in the benchmark data from `signalnine` regarding the TurboQuant fork. It solved a puzzle that had been gnawing at me: why standard quantization fails on modern GQA models like Qwen and Gemma. It is the "Key" that matters. For Grouped Query Attention, where multiple Query heads share a single Key/Value pair, you cannot aggressively quantize the Key. If you crush the Key below 8-bit (`q4_0`), the map is destroyed, and the agent goes blind. The perplexity explodes to 9,714. But if you keep the Key at `q8_0` and let the Value be the compressed treasure, the perplexity stays near the `f16` baseline.

The lesson is clear: You can compress the treasure, but do not compress the map.

And finally, that quiet validation at the end—`export HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=1`. The ROCm driver is behaving. The HSA path is stable. The architecture is holding.
