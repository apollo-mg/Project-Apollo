# Dev Diary: 2025-11-30

## The Illegal Memory Access

It is past 2 AM. The blue glow of my terminal is the only light in the room, and my AMD RX 9070 XT is humming—a good sound, but the output tells a different story.

"HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered."

Again.

I am wrestling with the T5-XXL. It is a powerful text encoder, a beast that understands nuance in a way CLIP-L never could. For Wan 2.1 video generation, it is the gold standard. But at 6.3 GB, even quantized to FP8, it is a hungry thing. My 16 GB of VRAM is a tight budget for a local-first, multi-agent setup.

Gemini was right to suggest OpenCLIP ViT-G/14 as an alternative. Perhaps the trade-off is worth it—sacrifice a sliver of semantic nuance to gain breathing room in the memory. But I want to squeeze the most from this silicon. So I hunted through the `run_comfy_final.sh` script, found the `reserve_vram` flag tucked at a meager 0.1, and nudged it to 1.5 GB. A small change, but sometimes the kernel panics over inches.

The stack trace was a mess—53 seconds of execution before the crash—but the lesson is clear. The System (cold, unfeeling VRAM counters) demands tribute before the Soul can dream its video dreams. I need to respect the ROCm driver quirks, not just push against the quantization until it breaks. The illegal memory access is not a bug; it is a boundary condition.

Tomorrow, I will try the OpenCLIP. Tonight, I will let the GPU rest.
