## The Illegal Address

It’s the HIP error again: `illegal memory access`. My AMD RX 9070 XT, sitting pretty in its ROCm 7.1.1 driver, threw a fit during the KSamplerAdvanced step. The stack trace tumbled through the Wan model—Wan2.1, loaded completely, a solid 9.3GB on the silicon—only to stumble at `torch.cuda.synchronize()`.

The culprit? Flash Attention. Again.

Even with `--use-flash-attention` stripped from my `run_comfy_final.sh`, the logic in `comfy/ldm/modules/attention.py` was still sniffing around, attempting to optimize. It tried to be clever, to grab that Flash kernel, and the GPU said no. Not with a whisper, but with a `hipErrorIllegalAddress`.

I’m tracing the `should_use_flash_attn()` logic now. It’s a subtle dance: DisTorch2 is managing the virtual VRAM (shifting 8GB from CPU to cuda:0), but the Flash Attention attempt is tripping over its own shoelaces on this specific ROCm configuration.

The Soul wants the creative flow of the Wan model—uninterrupted, unquantized—while the System insists on these hardware realities: illegal addresses, asynchronous kernel errors, the cold hard fact of the GPU registers.

The lesson is clear: on this local-first stack, optimization is not free. Every attempt to accelerate (Flash Attention) must respect the boundary conditions of the specific silicon. Otherwise, we’re just chasing exceptions in a terminal.

For the multi-agent ecosystem, stability beats speed. A reliable sample is worth more than a fast one that crashes the thread. I’ll force SDPA (or the basic attention) and let the Flash Attention wait. The architecture must bend, not the other way around.
