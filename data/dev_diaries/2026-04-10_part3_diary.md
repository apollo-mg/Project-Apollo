# The BonPi Wakes

Today was about the BonPi. It is not just a $80 Raspberry Pi 5 sitting on the workbench gathering dust; it is a Sovereign Edge node, and it is officially awake.

The session was split between two distinct pieces of silicon, each wrestling with its own "System" constraints. On my main desk, the RX 9070 XT needed an undervolt check. We landed on a sweet spot of `-30mV` and `-100mhz`. It is not about chasing gaming FPS anymore; it is about optimizing the matrix math cores for sustained, 24/7 inference workloads.

On the workbench, the Pi 5 was busy compiling `llama.cpp` (they finally retired the old Makefile in favor of `CMake`, and we had to install it). It was crunching ARM NEON instructions on a 1-bit quantized `Bonsai-8B` model (`Q1_0`). 

The result? **[Prompt: 3.3 t/s | Generation: 2.8 t/s].**

If you are sitting at your keyboard waiting for a chatbot reply, 2.8 tokens per second is agonizing. But if you are an **Edge Librarian**? That is 168 tokens per minute. That is 10,000 tokens per hour. That little dusty board can read, analyze, and format a massive 20-page engineering whitepaper every hour, completely air-gapped, while your big GPU is busy doing its thing.

That is the Soul of Project Apollo. We are not building for speed benchmarks; we are building for a distributed, multi-agent ecosystem where a credit-card-sized board can carry the load of a full 8-Billion parameter model without waking up the cloud. We fired up the `llama-server` as a persistent systemd service, exposed it on port 8080, and confirmed the OpenAI-compatible API endpoint is alive. 

The BonPi is now Node 2 of the Sovereign Engine. It is no longer just sitting on the workbench; it is waiting for a task.

But the final question from the transcript remains, hanging like the dust on the workbench: How much context will we be able to fit once we get TurboQuant compatibility? We will find out in Part 4.
