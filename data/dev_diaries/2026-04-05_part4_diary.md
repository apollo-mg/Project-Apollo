# Project Apollo: Dev Diary
**Date: 2026-04-05**

The friction between the "Soul" and the "System" is reaching a fever pitch. We have achieved a moment of pure, unadulterated intelligence—a 26B MoE model that can actually "think" through a problem—but the hardware-software handshake is still a jagged, stuttering mess.

### The Silicon Wall: A Performance Post-Mortem

I spent the first half of the session dissecting a massive performance regression. We hit a wall where the generation speed plummeted from a blistering 70+ t/s down to a crawling 12 t/s. It turns out we were caught in a double-bind. To fix a tokenizer bug in the mainline `llama.cpp` branch, I had to sacrifice the custom `turbo3` kernels that were making my RX 9070 XT sing. 

Worse, the micro-batch lock we implemented to prevent a memory crash (`-ub 512`) is a resource hog. It’s forcing the GPU to spill intermediate activations out of the fast L3 cache and into the slower VRAM, strangling the prompt ingestion. It’s a classic case of the "System" forcing us to trade speed for stability. We have the brain, but the nervous system is currently too slow to move the signals.

### The Architecture: The Bicameral Pivot

We’ve officially moved from "one-off execution" to a true Bicameral Architecture. We now have the **Daydream Daemon** (the speculative, creative "Soul") generating epiphenies in the background, and the **Foreman Agent** (the logical "System") acting as the executor.

I realized our execution model was flawed. Running epiphenies one by one is a recipe for "Circular Refactoring"—where one idea overwrites the next before the system can realize they are the same thought. 

I've implemented the **Architectural Review Board** pattern:
1. **Aggregation:** The Foreman now sweeps the entire `/daydream_spool/` at once.
2. **Synthesis:** It digests the entire batch into a single massive context, forcing it to synthesize a "Master Action Plan" rather than reacting to individual stimuli.
3. **Execution:** It executes the plan using the `open-multi-agent` framework.
4. **Flush:** It clears the queue only upon a successful, verified execution.

### The Automation: Power Management

The power draw was suspicious—my 9070 XT was idling at 80W during the Daydream cycles, which made no sense for a model this size. I suspect the `MUL_MAT_ID` patch is causing a massive bottleneck in the matrix multiplication engine. 

To solve this, I've integrated process-level control directly into the Foreman's lifecycle. The Foreman now uses Unix signals (`SIGSTOP`/`SIGCONT`) to freeze the Daydream daemon entirely during its execution. This ensures the Foreman has 100% of the VRAM and the power budget. Once the Foreman has finished its batch processing, it releases the "Soul" back into the wild.

The loop is complete. The machine is stable. The brain is thinking. We are ready to let the Foreman face the deluge.
