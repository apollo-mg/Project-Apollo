## Dev Diary: 2026-04-09

The fan curve on my RX 9070 XT has been humming all afternoon, a low, steady thrum against the CachyOS desk. Today was about the margin—squeezing a stable 65,536-token context window out of the 16GB VRAM wall without the driver throwing a tantrum.

### The Tangle
For weeks, the "Illegal Memory Access" (that classic HIP error) haunted the Wan 2.2 generations. The standard fix was a hard disable of `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA`, a sacrifice of full PCIe speed for the sake of stability. I treated the SDMA flag as a red flag, a potential corruption in the transfer that forced me to babysit the `start_rotorquant.sh` script.

But today, the data spoke. I let it run. I paired `-ub 64` (micro-batching) with TurboQuant (`-ctk q8_0`) and left the SDMA switch in the "on" position (`=1`). The result was a clean, crash-free run of 20.5 hours on a 26B MoE model at full context.

### The Epiphany
It wasn't the hardware. It wasn't even the VRAM limit. It was the stride. The combination of the micro-batch size and the specific quantization strategy smoothed the driver's stride, allowing the PCIe transfers to complete without the "Illegal Memory Access" corruption.

### The Soul & The System
This matters because it frees the creative loop from the cold technical limit. The Wan 2.2 video generation—the "Daydream" for the local architecture—can finally run without me hovering over the terminal to catch a crash. It means the 35B MoE model can breathe its full depth without forcing a context truncation.

Phase 13 is settled. The path is clear for the next Wan generation.
