**2026-04-10: Chasing Shadows in the Silicon**

Today was a day of chasing shadows in the silicon. I'm staring at my RX 9070 XT—16GB of VRAM, precious on this RDNA 4 card—and trying to coax Wan 2.2 through its paces without triggering another HIP illegal memory access. It's a familiar dance with ROCm 7.1, the kind where the soul of the architecture (I want sovereign, local-first inference) meets the cold, hard reality of the system (strided tensors, non-contiguous memory layouts, and driver-level kernel quirks).

I spent hours patching ComfyUI. Not the flashy, high-level kind of patching that changes an API, but the surgical, AST-like kind: enforcing `.contiguous()` on tensors before moving them to the GPU, hard-clamping `tile_size` to 256 to save the VAE decode from OOM, and wrestling with the Triton Flash Attention backend until I finally decided to fall back to the safe, math-backed SDPA kernel. It was small work, but necessary work. Without it, the multi-agent pipeline stutters, and my "Sovereign Entity" can't dream.

The lesson? In the local-first multi-agent ecosystem, we don't have infinite context or infinite VRAM. We have to be ruthless about memory fragmentation and strategic about quantization. A 1-bit Bonsai runs at 130 tokens per second for a reason, and that reason is the precious bandwidth of the PCIe bus and the temperamental nature of SDMA when the cache is hot. I'm learning to respect the silicon as much as the architecture. The soul wants to be creative; the system insists on stability. Today, we found a truce.
