# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-03-05 (Part 8)

The transition from "concept" to "silicon reality" is never a straight line. It’s a jagged, violent mess of recompilations and power spikes. Today was a battle against the sheer weight of the PyTorch build and the structural integrity of my own code.

### The Silicon Stress Test
The build hit a massive combinatorial wall. Because I had to hardcode the warp size in `Macros.h` to 32 to align with our GFX1201 architecture, the entire dependency tree collapsed. The build system saw the "DNA" of the tensor engine change and started a massive, multi-core re-verification of every single Flash Attention kernel. 

The hardware was screaming. I saw a transient hit of 438 Watts on the dashboard—way above our 330W cap. It’s the "Heretic" effect; the Qwen 3.5 model is so aggressively optimized for the 9070 XT that it’s triggering micro-bursts that bypass the standard driver throttles. It's a sign of raw power, but it's also a warning: if my power supply fails under these transients, the whole lab goes dark.

### The Structural Failure: The `Normalization.cuh` Incident
The "Soul" of the project—the creative vision of a high-speed, local-first intelligence—was nearly lost to a clumsy `sed` command. 

My attempt to patch `Normalization.cuh` for the `Float2` struct failed miserably. A missing closing brace caused a compiler error that felt like hitting a brick wall. I realized my `cat << 'EOF'` approach had truncated the file, wiping out hundreds of lines of critical code. 

It was a moment of pure friction. I had to decide: rebuild from scratch using the CUDA source or manually reconstruct it. I chose the manual path—re-implementing the `Float2` struct with the `accscalar_t` constructor and the assignment operator. It was tedious, surgical, and high-stakes. One wrong brace and the entire AOTriton module—the very heart of our speed—would be dead.

### The Recovery and Re-build
I patched the `Float2` ambiguity, fixed the `C10_WARP_SIZE` macro, and cleaned up the `RNN.hip` and `Nonzero.hip` files to remove redundant casts. Then, I hit 'Enter' on the relaunch.

The build is currently in the "Combinatorial Peak." It's grinding through the `bwd_kernel_dq` (the backward pass kernels), and the CPU is still pinned at 100% across all 8 cores. It’s a slow, grinding crawl toward the finish line, but the Triage Hook reports a "Healthy & Active" status.

### The Model Arrival
The Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B (Q3_K_S) is finally home. The filename mismatch was a minor nuisance, but it's now successfully streaming at 35GB. The 80B model is the weight that will define the intelligence of the system; we are currently at 58% of the download. 

### Lessons from the Trenches
- **Infrastructure is Intelligence:** You can have the best model in the world, but if your power-delivery (PSU) can't handle the transients of an optimized ROCm kernel, the software is useless.
- **The Cost of Complexity:** The more we "specialize" the architecture (like the custom warp size), the more the build system fights us. There is a massive tax on specialized code in terms of compilation time and dependency management.
- **The Power of the Physical:** As I look at the mud outside, I realize that the "Sovereign Lab" isn't just about code. It's about the 240V lines and the PVC conduits. If the physical foundation isn't solid, the digital intelligence has no place to live.

We are approaching the final link. The AOTriton module is 99% done. The model is 96% downloaded. We are about to see if this "Born-in-Linux" engine actually breathes.
