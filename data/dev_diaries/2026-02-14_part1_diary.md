# Dev Diary: 2026-02-14

## The Ghost in the Silicon

Today was a battle between the elegance of a new architecture and the brutal, unyielding reality of RDNA 4. I spent the first half of the session wrestling with the friction between my local-first vision and the specific, stubborn quirks of the AMD ROCm stack. 

The goal was simple: get Flux.1 Schnell running locally on the 9070 XT. But the "System" fought back. The initial ComfyUI launch was a disaster—bottlenecked by a startup script optimized for a completely different model (Wan 2.1). It was a mess of conflicting environment variables and disabled optimizations. 

## The Tuning Tax

The real epiphany came when I realized the hardware wasn't broken; it was just... thinking. 

I hit a massive wall where the progress bar sat at zero, yet the GPU was screaming—drawing 277 Watts of pure heat. The fans were ramping up and down like a heartbeat. My first instinct was a kernel panic or a VRAM fragmentation crash. But the logs told a different story. 

The `PyTorch TunableOp` engine is both a blessing and a curse. It’s a brilliant piece of software designed to find the optimal math kernels for specific hardware, but the "tuning tax" is brutal. On the first run, it’s bench-marking every single convolution and GEMM operation across the entire model to find the fastest path for the RDNA 4 architecture. 

It’s the ultimate expression of the conflict between the "Soul" (the desire for high-speed, local, creative autonomy) and the "System" (the cold, heavy computational cost of optimization). We are essentially paying a massive energy and time debt upfront to buy speed later.

## Architectural Lessons

1. **Hardware-Specific Tuning is Non-Negotiable:** You cannot treat a high-end AMD GPU like a generic CUDA device. The environment variables (`HSA_ENABLE_SDMA`, `PYTORCH_TUNABLEOP_ENABLED`) are the difference between a machine that's a paperweight and a machine that's a powerhouse.
2. **The Importance of Modular Launch Scripts:** My decision to split the `start_comfy.sh` into a dedicated `start_flux.sh` was vital. It allowed us to isolate the tuning cache and the specific Flash Attention requirements of the Flux workflow without polluting the rest of the environment.
3. **The Config-File Trap:** It’s easy to assume a model is missing, but in a multi-drive setup, it's almost always a mapping error in the `.yaml` configuration. The "missing" models were there, just invisible to the software because the directory pointers hadn't been explicitly declared.

## Moving Forward

The system is currently "tuning" the VAE. It's a heavy, resource-intensive operation, but once it finishes, the 79s/it crawl should collapse into sub-second iterations. 

If the tuning doesn't resolve, we'll have to pivot to a GGUF-based workflow to save VRAM, but I'm betting on the raw power of the 9070 XT. We're pushing the limits of what local-first AI can do on consumer silicon. It's messy, it's hot, and it's loud—but it's ours.
