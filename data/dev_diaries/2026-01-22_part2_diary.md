# Dev Diary: 2026-01-22

## The Ghost in the Machine: KAMP vs. Silicon

Today was a battle against software abstraction. I was trying to unlock the true potential of the BTT Eddy sensor—to get that beautiful, continuous, sweeping scan that makes high-speed 3D printing actually viable. But the "Soul" of the project—the desire for a smooth, fluid hardware experience—kept hitting the "System" wall of legacy software.

The issue was KAMP (Klipper Adaptive Meshing & Purging). It was sitting in the middle of my execution pipeline, intercepting the `BED_MESH_CALIBRATE` command. Even when I tried to bypass it with a direct call to the native Klipper command (`_BED_MESH_CALIBRATE`), the system was stuttering. It was doing this rhythmic, jerky dance—pausing at every single point, moving the Z-axis up and down like it was still doing a traditional contact probe.

It felt like the sensor was screaming, "I want to fly!" and the software was replying, "No, we're doing this one point at a time."

I dig into the BTT documentation and find the smoking gun: `METHOD=rapid_scan`. My code was calling `METHOD=scan`. It seems the software implementation of the Eddy's high-speed mode was even more specific than the general Klipper command.

But even with that, the stuttering persisted. It turns out KAMP's adaptive logic was fighting the hardware. The overhead of the macro-intercept was too heavy, or its logic was forcing a standard mesh-point density that broke the continuous motion requirements. 

The decision became clear: Kill KAMP. 

Klipper has native adaptive meshing now. It's built into the core. It's cleaner. It's closer to the silicon. By purging the KAMP include files and stripping out the old,-bloat-heavy macros, I'm letting the hardware talk directly to the firmware.

The transition was surgical. I used SSH to dive into the Raspberry Pi, bypassed the Windows-based file transfer friction, and used `sed` to surgically remove the KAMP-induced friction in `printer.cfg` and `macros.cfg`.

I've updated the `PRINT_START` macro to use the native `ADAPTIVE=1` and `METHOD=rapid_scan` commands.

This is a massive win for the local-first philosophy. We aren't just running a printer; we're running a sophisticated, multi-agent-capable edge device. If the software-layer (KAMP) is too heavy for the real-time requirements of the hardware (Eddy), we don't just tweak it—we strip it back to the core.

Next step: Verifying the actual scan speed. If we can get the Eddy to sweep at 250mm/s without the "stuttering" ghost, we've officially unlocked the next generation of local-first manufacturing.

**Current Status:**
- Hardware: BTT Eddy (Operational)
- Firmware: Klipper (Native Adaptive mode)
- Software: KAMP (Purged)
- Architecture: Moving toward a streamlined, hardware-direct model.
