# Dev Diary: 2026-03-06 (Part 11) - The Memory Miser & The RDNA 4 Beast

The fight for the "Soul" of Project Apollo is often a fight against the cold, hard limits of my silicon. Today was a chaotic, high-stakes battle to reconcile the creative intelligence we want with the 16GB VRAM reality of my RX 9070 XT.

### The 0.6B "Language Trap"
We spent the first half of the session chasing a ghost. I had been struggling with a routing failure in the Gatekeeper. It turns out, the 0.6B Qwen model wasn't "broken"—it was just being too literal. I had been using the term `ESCALATE` in the prompts, which is an abstract concept the 0.6B model couldn't grasp. When I swapped the trigger to `ENGINEER`—a high-signal, concrete term the model had seen during training—the latency dropped to 0.4s. 

The "Soul" of the system was there, but it needed a simpler vocabulary to talk to the "System."

### The VRAM Tetris & The Ghost in the Machine
Then came the power crisis. The 9B model was hogging 15.3GB of VRAM, even though the math said it shouldn't. I dug into the `llm_interface.py` and found the culprit: a "Ghost" 4B model was pinned in memory, refusing to unload. It was a massive leak. 

By performing a "Nuclear Unload," we purged the 4B model and reclaimed the headroom. The system stabilized at 11.4GB, but the 9B model was still being "lazy." The power draw on the GPU plummeted from 300W to 200W, suggesting the ROCm scheduler was putting the RDNA 4 cores to sleep between tokens. I re-applied the 334W power cap and forced the high-performance clock states, but the throttle remained.

### The vLLM Collision
We tried to push the limits with the 9B-FP8 model in vLLM to hit that 300W peak. The math was brutal: 12GB for weights + 1GB for OS + 0.8GB for the 0.6B Gatekeeper = 13.8GB. With only 2.2GB left, we had zero room for a KV Cache. 

The vLLM engine threw a fit, rejecting the `--dtype fp8` flag. I had to pivot to a "Memory Miser" configuration: `--quantization fp8` and `--kv-cache-dtype fp8` with a forced 1024 context window. We were trying to perform surgery on the VRAM.

### The Architecture Paradox
The final boss of the day was a version mismatch. The `vllm-dev` Docker image was using an old `transformers` library that didn't recognize the new `Qwen3_5` architecture. It was a total roadblock. 

I found the workaround: a "Sovereign Patch." By manually editing the `config.json` and lying to the system—telling it the model was actually a `Qwen2` architecture—we bypassed the library's ignorance. The weights are compatible, the kernels are compatible, and we finally tricked the system into running the high-performance model.

### Current State: The Apollo Cascade is Live
The system is now a tiered hierarchy:
* **Stage 1 (0.6B):** The "Sovereign Shield" (Ultra-fast triage, ~0.8s).
* **Stage 2 (9B-FP8):** The "Workhorse" (Running in vLLM with surgical VRAM limits).
* **Hardware:** RDNA 4 is finally being fed.

We are currently waiting for the final performance verification to see if the "Memory Miser" patch allows the 9B model to finally hit that 300W saturation point. If it does, we have successfully built the most efficient local-first multi-agent architecture possible on consumer-grade silicon.
