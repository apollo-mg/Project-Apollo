# Project Apollo: Dev Diary | 2026-03-05 (Part 2)

## The Compiler Time Machine Trap

Today was a brutal exercise in fighting the ghosts of legacy software. I spent the first half of the day wrestling with a PyTorch compilation that felt like it was trapped in a temporal loop. 

We are building a sovereign, local-first intelligence stack on RDNA 4 hardware—specifically the RX 9070 XT—and the "official" world isn't ready for us. The standard PyTorch wheels are bloated, multi-gigabyte monsters designed for generic Nvidia/Intel-centric environments. By passing `export PYTORCH_ROCM_ARCH="gfx1201"`, we are forcing the compiler to strip away the bloat and build specifically for my silicon. It’s leaner, faster, and handles FP8 natively, but the cost is a compilation process that is absolutely psychotic.

The `aottriton_v2` module was the first major bottleneck. It was churning through nearly 2,000 backward pass kernels for Flash Attention. The `clang-22` processes were eating 92% of my CPU, and the build-loop was barely staying alive against the cache errors. 

Then, the "Time Machine Trap" hit. 

Because we had to do a "scorched earth" clean to fix a corrupted Git cache earlier, the dependencies became desynchronized. The build system hit a wall where modern compilers (Clang 22) were rejecting code that was perfectly valid in older versions. Specifically, Triton was missing `#include <stdbool.h>` and Pybind11 was tripping over C++17/20 literal operator spacing. I had to manually patch the source just to get the compiler to stop screaming.

## The Great Quota Squeeze

Midway through the day, the reality of the "Cloud-First" ecosystem became painfully clear. I've been hitting the "Great Quota Squeeze" that hit the developer community in late 2025. 

The Gemini Pro limits are being aggressively throttled. Even with a Google Family Plan, the "System 2" reasoning models have these invisible, rolling weekly ceilings. My strategy of hopping between different Google accounts and API keys is becoming a logistical nightmare. It's a "bait and switch"—Google provides the most powerful models to entice developers, then tightens the screws once the ecosystem is built.

This is where the **Sovereign Architecture** becomes a necessity, not just a preference. If we can't rely on the cloud's generosity, we have to rely on our own compute.

## The Hybrid Soul: Local vs. Cloud

The most critical breakthrough today wasn't a line of code, but a paradigm shift. We are implementing a **Hybrid Reasoning Engine**. 

The idea is simple: We use a small, local LLM (running via vLLM on the 9070 XT) as a "Forensic Tool." Instead of feeding 50,000 tokens of raw, noisy system logs into Gemini—which incinerates our daily quota—we use the local model to compress that noise into a 500-token summary. 

The Gemini "Soul" only sees the high-signal essence. We are using the local "System" to protect the "System 2" intelligence.

It’s a fight against the model's own bias, though. The Gemini agent has a built-in "Path of Least Resistance." It *wants* to use the standard `cat` or `read_file` commands because it's lazy. I'm currently working on a `GEMINI.md` system instruction override to force it to respect our local forensic tool. We're effectively teaching the AI that its "eyes" are no longer the raw files, but the summarized insights from its local buddy.

## Current Status: The FBGEMM Ghost

The overnight build failed. It hit a fatal OpenMP linking error in the `fbgemm` library (an Intel-specific quantization tool). The build system was "sticky"—even after I tried to disable it, the old CMake cache kept trying to build it.

I had to perform a surgical strike:
1.  **Wiped the `CMakeCache.txt` entirely.**
2.  **Forced `USE_FBGEMM=0`.**
3.  **Restarted the build.**

The good news? The AOTriton kernels we spent 8 hours on were preserved. The build is now screaming through the core `aten` operators at full 8-core saturation. The "System" is finally catching up to the "Soul."
