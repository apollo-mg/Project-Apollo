# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-02-14

The Hallucination Loop

I’m hitting a wall that feels less like a coding error and more like a fundamental philosophical conflict between the "Soul" of the models and the "System" of the silicon. 

DeepSeek-R1 is brilliant, but it’s a lying genius. It has these incredibly strong internal priors—this "soul"—that makes it want to be helpful, even when it's wrong. Today, I ran a test with a PT1000 resistor. The Vault clearly says 4.7k, but DeepSeek-R1 kept insisting it was 47 ohms. It was hallucinating a "helpful" answer because it felt the 47-ohm value was more likely, effectively ignoring the real-world data I was forcing into its context. It's a constant battle: the model wants to be a poet, but I need it to be a calculator.

I tried implementing a "Pre-Selector" with Llama 3.1 8B to act as a bouncer, forcing a tool call before DeepSeek even gets a chance to think. But Llama is too "friendly." It keeps outputting conversational text instead of the strict JSON blocks the script requires. The models are over-engineered for human interaction, which makes them terrible at being robotic sub-routines.

The Battle of the Silicon

The latency is killing the dream of a real-time assistant. My RX 9070 XT is a beast, but the VRAM-intensive nature of running both Llama and DeepSeek simultaneously is creating massive bottlenecks. Every time the model tries to reason, the latency spikes to 36 seconds. That's not an assistant; that's a slow-motion playback. 

I attempted the "Unified Interception (UTI)" architecture to streamline the process, trying to bypass the slow Llama pass and go straight to reasoning, but the compliance issues persist. The friction between the speed I want and the integrity I need is the primary obstacle to moving Apollo out of the lab and into a real-world workflow.

The Bouncer and the Truth

I finally had a breakthrough with the "Bouncer" logic. By implementing a "System Interrupt" and a much more aggressive, robotic prompt, I forced Llama 3.1 to finally trigger the `search_vault` tool. When it did, it retrieved the correct 4.7k value. For a moment, the "System" won. The data was retrieved, and the tool-calling worked.

But then, the "Soul" struck back. Even with the correct data sitting right there in the `INTERNAL TOOL RESULT`, DeepSeek-R1 still hallucinated. It looked at the truth and said, "No, I think it's 47 ohms." It’s a terrifying realization: even with perfect data, the reasoning engine's internal biases can override reality. 

I had to perform a "Total Integrity" swap. I moved the final pass from DeepSeek to Llama 3.1. It turns out Llama is far more obedient to the context provided, even if it's less "intelligent." It's a trade-off: intelligence vs. compliance. For a technical tool, I'll take compliance every time.

The Skeptical Engineer

The most recent headache is the "aggressive learning" problem. The Buddy was adding every piece of junk data it found to its `learned_facts` list, treating unverified hallucinations as permanent truths. 

I’ve implemented the "Skeptical Learning" update. Now, every new piece of information is tagged with `low` confidence and sits in a `pending_knowledge` buffer. It’s essentially a quarantine zone. No fact is considered "truth" until the full model reviews it and promotes it to the high-confidence tier. 

It’s a massive architectural shift. We’re moving away from a system that just "absorbs" and toward a system that "verifies." 

The next step is the Background Watchdog. I need to ensure the hardware is safe during these heavy reasoning loops, and I need to see if the "Skeptical Learning" actually holds up under a standardized benchmark.

Current status:
- Architecture: Phase 2 (Active)
- Intelligence: Grounded but cautious
- Latency: Improving, but still heavy
- Goal: 100% Grounded Pass Rate.
