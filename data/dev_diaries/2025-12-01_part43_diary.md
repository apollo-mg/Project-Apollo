**Flash Attention Falters**

I was mid-stream on the Wan model, Gemini CLI humming, when the terminal spat it back: `torch.AcceleratorError: HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`.

The RX 9070 XT is hungry. Flash Attention—that high-performance optimization we count on when VRAM is tight—tripped over a None pointer. The line `out = torch.zeros_like(q) if out is None else out.zero_()` became the breaking point.

The system bailed gracefully to SDPA (Scaled Dot Product Attention). It is slower, but stable. This isn’t just about speed; it is about the friction between the Soul of Project Apollo—running massive, local-first models on consumer silicon—and the System, with its cold, hard limits of memory and driver quirks.

The lesson for our multi-agent ecosystem is clear: Flash Attention is the dream, but SDPA is the reality. When we architect systems that must fit on a single desktop PC, we cannot assume the fastest kernel will fire. We need resilience. We need fallbacks that don’t cost us our context window.

I adjusted the quantization—dropping from FP16 to INT8 where it mattered—and the pipeline flowed again. The blocks moved from CPU to cuda:0 without complaint, and the image began to denoise. Sometimes, an illegal address isn’t a bug. It is a boundary condition, a reminder that local AI lives in the details.
