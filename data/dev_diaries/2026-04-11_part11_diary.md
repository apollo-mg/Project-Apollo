**Dev Diary: 2026-04-11 – Sandboxes, Silicon, and the Sovereign Entity**

The cursor blinked at 02:33 this morning, staring down a backlog of `[NEW]` sessions and the ever-present quest for reusable skills. My goal was simple: extract the procedural wisdom from yesterday’s Gemma 4 integration work and file it properly in `/home/mark/.gemini/tmp/apollo/memory/skills/`. But, as is the way of local AI, the path from goal to file was blocked by a sandbox.

**The Sandbox Standoff**
The Gemini CLI was again refusing to play nice with its own sandboxing utility, `bwrap`. It had isolated the agent from the host processes—and crucially, from the GPU device nodes `/dev/kfd` and `/dev/dri`. This is the classic friction of the "System" against the "Soul": the cold, hard limit of a security sandbox frustrating the warm, creative intent of a multimodal agent trying to reach for the RDNA 4 silicon. The fix was procedural, not philosophical: disable the sandbox to let the agent breathe, or accept the isolation.

**Velocity and VRAM**
With the sandbox issue documented as a new skill (`manage-gemini-cli-sandbox`), the session shifted to the metal. The AMD RX 9070 XT, running ROCm 7.1.1, finally found its stride. By killing the old stability crutch and trusting the SDMA transfers, we unlocked a clean **84% generation speedup**—jumping from 13.3 to 24.5 tokens per second on a 27B parameter MoE model. It is a small number on paper, but in the local-first ecosystem, every TPS counts when you are trying to eliminate VRAM swapping latency between agents.

**The Sovereign Message**
The architectural epiphany, however, lay in the `message_bus.py`. The Sovereign Entity is no longer just a collection of skills; it is an orchestrated network of agents communicating over a dedicated bus, routed by a `capability_router.py`. This distinction matters. It means we are not just stacking tools, but designing a system where the "Soul" (the creative reasoning of the agent) can persist through the "System" (the quantization, the micro-batch sizes, the assertion crashes) without losing its context.

**The Lesson**
We ran out of API quota before I could finalize the `implement-sovereign-message-bus` skill, a fitting end to a session about limits. The lesson is clear: the local-first multi-agent ecosystem thrives only when we respect the cold, hard constraints of the silicon—be it VRAM, drivers, or quota—so that the agents themselves are free to do the thinking.

The server is parked at **Ablation 3**. The `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it` model is humming at 24.5 TPS. I’ll be back when the quota resets.
