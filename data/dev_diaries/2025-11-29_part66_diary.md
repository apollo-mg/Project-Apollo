**Dev Diary: Part 66 – The OOM on RX 9070 XT**

The terminal blinked its red eye again. A `torch.OutOfMemoryError` stared back at me, cold and unyielding. My AMD RX 9070 XT GPU—capable, but finite—has 15.92 GiB of VRAM, yet only 4.64 GiB was free. PyTorch tried to claim 15.45 GiB for the `Wan2.1` model’s forward pass and failed.

This is the friction I live for: the "Soul" of Project Apollo against the "System" of silicon. The Soul wants to run large, local models without the cloud, to keep the inference loop tight and the privacy intact. The System, however, demands obedience to the hardware. It speaks in GiBs and in fragments.

The error was specific. It wasn’t just capacity; it was topology. The logs mentioned 619.65 MiB of memory that was “reserved by PyTorch but unallocated.” That is the ghost in the machine: memory fragmentation. The `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True` flag was the clue I needed to chase.

DisTorch2 was doing its heavy lifting, analyzing the virtual VRAM, mapping the `cpu` as the donor and the `cuda:0` as the recipient. It had placed the 6.27 GB model on the CPU, but when the sampling step arrived, the GPU stumbled. The handoff between the CPU and the GPU had hit a wall.

Here is the lesson for my local-first, multi-agent ecosystem: we cannot treat VRAM as an infinite ocean. We must treat it as a precious, finite well. Every quantization strategy—`Q4_K`, `Q6_K`, `F16`—is a negotiation between speed and capacity. If we want our agents to dance locally, we must respect the fragmentation before we scale the batch.

I am adjusting the config. I am setting the `expandable_segments` flag to true. I am ready to try again, with a clearer head about the limits that make creativity possible.
