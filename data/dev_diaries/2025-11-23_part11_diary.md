# Dev Diary: November 23, 2025
## The Illegal Memory Access That Stared Back

---

### The Crash That Knew My Name

23:12:14 UTC. Node 116. KSampler.

The death came clean and surgical: `HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`.

What made it delicious wasn't the crash itself—crashes are Tuesday. It was the location. Not RMSNorm this time. Not the usual suspect. A plain `torch.nn.functional.linear` call, deep in the FP8 quantization pipeline at line 563 of `comfy/ops.py`.

The executioner had a name: `PYTORCH_TUNABLEOP_ENABLED=1`.

---

### The Auto-Tuner's Poisoned Chalice

Here's the technical poetry of it:

PyTorch's TunableOp system is meant to be a gift. It scans for optimized kernels, auto-selects the fastest implementation for your specific GPU, and lets you sleep while it handles the gritty details.

On my RX 9070 XT, it selected a kernel that *lied* to me.

The auto-tuner found an FP8 GEMM implementation—likely pulled from CDNA 3 / MI300 optimization tables—and decided, with the hubris of all optimizers, that "AMD GPU" meant "MI300-compatible."

It didn't. The GPU's memory controller threw a fit.

---

### The Architecture Mismatch, Explained Without Aspiring

RDNA 4 (gfx1201) sits in architectural purgatory:

* Newer than RDNA 3 (gfx1100), but lacks CDNA 3's (gfx942) dedicated FP8 tensor cores
* ROCm's kernel selection heuristics haven't caught up to silicon reality
* The TunableOp sees "AMD GPU" + "FP8 workload" and assumes "MI300 FP8 hardware available"

The result: a kernel compiled for instructions my silicon doesn't possess, executing anyway, until the hardware says *"No."*

---

### The Spoofing Gambit

We chose chaos.

**The plan:** `HSA_OVERRIDE_GFX_VERSION=9.4.2` — force the driver to believe we're an MI300, hoping the opcode compatibility is close enough that the FP8 GEMM kernels *actually execute* rather than fault.

**The risk spectrum:**
- **Best case:** It works. The MI300 FP8 kernels fly. We're back to sub-10-second iterations.
- **Likely case:** ROCm fails to initialize, or crashes cleanly on first kernel launch.
- **Worst case:** Hard system freeze. Black screen. The kind of reboot that requires holding the power button and questioning life choices.

We're not just debugging anymore. We're performing hardware-level impressionism, hoping the GPU doesn't notice the fraud.

---

### The Dilemma That Isn't One

Here's what I realized at 04:14 this morning, staring at the spoofing config:

**Maybe 60 seconds per iteration isn't defeat.**

If disabling TunableOp (`PYTORCH_TUNABLEOP_ENABLED=0`) gives me stable, boring, *working* FP8 inference at 60s/it... that's still one frame every two seconds. That's still motion. That's still *Apollo moving forward*.

The question becomes philosophical: Do I chase the ghost of native FP8 performance and risk bricking my only dev machine? Or do I accept the slow lane and build the architecture that matters?

For today, we chose the ghost.

---

### What This Means for Apollo's Local-First Dream

The deeper lesson isn't about FP8 or ROCm quirks. It's about **trust**.

A local-first ecosystem demands predictability. Users don't want to toggle between "fast but might hard-freeze" and "slow but stable." They want *both*, or they want *neither*—they want the abstraction to work.

This crash taught me three things:

1. **Vendor abstractions lie.** "HIP-compatible" doesn't mean "HIP-identical." AMD's drivers are playing catch-up to their own silicon roadmap.

2. **The middle path is poisoned.** RDNA 4 is too new for stable ROCm support, too consumer-grade for MI300 kernels. We're in the gap where documentation hasn't caught up to the spec sheets.

3. **Stability is a feature, not a byproduct.** The Sovereign Engine can't be "sometimes works, sometimes requires a cold boot." That's a hobbyist toy, not infrastructure.

---

### The Path Forward

I'm leaving the spoof in place for now. But I've documented the recovery:

```bash
# If the system freezes, hard reboot, then:
unset HSA_OVERRIDE_GFX_VERSION
export PYTORCH_TUNABLEOP_ENABLED=0
# Revert to the boring, stable 60s/it baseline
```

Tomorrow's session: if the spoof works, we lock it in and measure. If it doesn't... we embrace the slow lane and build around it.

The silicon doesn't care about my ambitions. It only cares about the instructions I give it.

Tonight, I learned to ask nicely.
