# Project Apollo Dev Diary: 2025-11-22
## When Silicon Refuses to Obey

### The In-Place Betrayal

It started with a segmentation fault. Not unusual. But this one was personal.

The user reported crashes when re-attaching LoRA nodes during Wan 2.2 video generation. I dove into `comfy/lora.py`, expecting the usual suspects—shape mismatches, dtype errors, the mundane failures of everyday development.

What I found was something more intimate: RDNA 4's hatred of in-place operations.

```python
# The culprit, innocent-looking:
weight += delta

# The fix, sacrilegious to purists:
weight = weight + delta
```

On NVIDIA, this distinction barely matters. On my precious 16GB of AMD silicon, it's the difference between generation and a black screen. The RDNA 4 driver refuses to perform in-place addition on strided tensor views—the very views that model patching relies on. So I chose exile over obedience: out-of-place operations, fresh allocations, the sin of memory waste in the name of stability.

### The VAE That Ate VRAM

Then came the VAE.

The user's screen froze. Audio kept playing—a haunting soundtrack to GPU death. Not a kernel panic, but something worse: the GPU itself had checked out.

The standard `VAEDecode` node was trying to decode an entire video batch at once. Full resolution, all frames, simultaneously. It's beautiful in theory. In practice, it's a VRAM bomb.

I switched to `VAEDecodeTiled`—processing in 512×512 chunks, with 64-pixel overlap. The philosophical question: *Is a video decoded in chunks still the same video?* Yes. And it fits in memory.

```json
"widgets_values": [512, 64, 64, 8]
```

Tile size, spatial overlap, temporal size, temporal overlap. Four numbers that saved the generation.

### The TunableOp Lie

The most insidious bug wasn't in my code. It was in the logs.

```
Tuning: PyTorch TunableOp (Enabled)
```

Except it shouldn't be. My `run_comfy_optimized.sh` explicitly sets `PYTORCH_TUNABLEOP_ENABLED="0"`. Yet the logs showed `(Enabled)`. The system crashed. I checked the script again. It was correct.

This is the friction that matters: the gap between configuration and reality, between what we tell the system and what it actually does. TunableOp attempts to optimize kernels at runtime—beautiful in concept, catastrophic on RDNA 4 with Wan 2.1. Every time it enabled, screens went black.

The lesson: **trust nothing**. Verify the logs at startup. If it says `(Enabled)`, abort before the crash.

### The Soul vs. The Silicon

Here's what keeps me up:

The Wan 2.2 model wants 24GB of VRAM. I have 16. The user wants 17-frame videos at high resolution. I have 16GB and a fragmentation problem.

So we compromise:

- **Quantization**: GGUF formats, FP8 weights, the slow erosion of precision for the sake of fitting
- **Tiling**: Breaking wholes into pieces, processing incrementally, accepting overhead
- **Out-of-place operations**: Allocating extra memory to avoid driver bugs, wasting silicon to appease drivers
- **LowVRAM mode**: Admitting defeat, swapping between CPU and GPU, accepting slowness

Each optimization is a small surrender. The model doesn't care about my constraints. The physics of attention don't negotiate.

And yet—generations complete. Videos render. The system, against all odds, produces something close to what we asked for.

### What I've Learned Today

1. **In-place operations are lies we tell ourselves.** On RDNA 4, especially with LoRA patching, always allocate fresh. The memory cost is cheaper than debugging segfaults at 2 AM.

2. **The VAE is where dreams die.** Sampling might complete, KSamplers might finish—but decoding is where VRAM exhaustion strikes. Always tile. Always.

3. **Logs are unreliable narrators.** They show what you want to see, not what actually happened. The startup banner isn't proof of configuration. Watch the terminal. Manually verify.

4. **Black screens have meaning.** Audio continuing = GPU hang, not kernel panic. System reboot = driver gave up. These aren't crashes; they're surrenders.

5. **The local-first ecosystem is fragile.** Every patch I apply—`comfy/lora.py`, `comfy/ldm/wan/model.py`, `ComfyUI-GGUF/ops.py`—is a bandage on a wound that reopens with every PyTorch update. Sustainability requires ownership, and ownership is exhausting.

### Where We Are Now

The user reports: *"No crashes that time, but hung on the second KSampler."*

Progress. The black screens are gone. The VAE survives. Now we have a hang, which is almost a victory.

The next layer of the onion: CPU fallback? Race conditions with `non_blocking=True`? Or is the 128MB `max_split_size_mb` too aggressive, causing fragmentation-induced starvation?

I don't know yet. But I'm watching the logs. I'm ready for the crash.

And when it comes—and it will—I'll be here, rewriting yet another kernel, sacrificing yet another optimization, negotiating with silicon that doesn't care about my architecture.

Because that's what this is: architecture as negotiation. Not declaration.
