# Apollo Dev Diary: 2026-02-27 (Part 11)

The fight against the silicon is getting exhausting. Today was supposed to be about optimization—polishing the "Soul" of the system by making the Resident Engineer model more responsive. Instead, it became a war against the driver.

### The Architecture: The Router and the Resident
The day started with a win. We successfully implemented the **Apollo Dispatcher**. I’ve moved the system toward a "System 1/System 2" cognitive architecture. The lightweight Llama 3.2 1B now acts as a high-speed router, classifying intent. If you're just chatting or checking VRAM, it stays in the 1B model (low VRAM). If you need heavy reasoning, it wakes up the DeepSeek-R1 "Engineer." This is the "Soul" of the project—it gives the system a sense of purpose without the massive overhead of a 14B model for every trivial thought.

### The Friction: RDNA 4 and the vLLM Mirage
Then, the silicon fought back. I tried to push the limits of the RX 9070 XT by moving to a vLLM-based container. I wanted that sweet, sweet Prefix Caching—the ability for the model to "remember" the context of the `SOUL.md` and `MEMORY.md` files without re-processing them.

But the RDNA 4 architecture is still a wild beast. The `vllm-dev` nightly container hit a memory access fault almost immediately. The mismatch between the `gfx1201` architecture and the experimental Triton kernels caused a driver hang. Even when we tried to "spoof" the hardware to RDNA 3 (`gfx1100`) to stabilize it, we hit a 15% performance penalty and a nasty driver crash. The "System" was too unstable to house the "Soul."

### The Crash: The VRAM Tetris Gone Wrong
The real disaster happened when I tried to implement the `keep_alive: -1` instruction. 

I wanted to lock the models into VRAM permanently to ensure near-instant responses. I updated `llm_interface.py` to tell Ollama never to unload the models. I thought I was being smart, managing the VRAM through a custom script that swaps the Vision and Engineer models to prevent overflow. 

The reality? The driver doesn't care about my elegant Python logic. When the models tried to hold onto their memory while the driver was attempting to reallocate buffers for a new task, the `amdgpu` kernel driver simply gave up. The result was a hard freeze—total system lockup. No mouse, no terminal, just a frozen screen.

### The Pivot: Pragmatism over Perfection
The hard crash revealed a fundamental truth: my environment is currently a mess of fragmented drivers and unaligned libraries. The "Soul" wants high-level reasoning, but the "System" (the Linux kernel and the AMD driver) is currently too temperamental to handle the heavy lifting of a constant-resident 14B model in a Vulkan-based Ollama instance.

I've realized that the pure, high-performance vLLM path is currently too unstable for this hardware. We are pivoting back to a more stable, native Ollama setup using the Vulkan backend, but with a heavy quantization (`q8_0`) on the KV cache to reduce the VRAM footprint. It's not the "perfect" 70% latency reduction I dreamed of, but it's a system that won't crash every time I ask it a complex question.

**Lessons for the next session:**
- Stop chasing the "perfect" container. The Docker/ROCm abstraction layer is currently too thin for the RX 9070 XT.
- Focus on the native Ollama stability; the driver is the bottleneck, not the Python code.
- The "Soul" is ready to think, but the "System" needs more breathing room in the VRAM.
