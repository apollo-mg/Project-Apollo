# Dev Diary: 2026-02-27 (Part 13)

## The Great Truncation & The ROCm Wall

Today was a fight against my own architecture. I started the morning thinking I was just debugging a minor session error, but I quickly realized the Gemini CLI had essentially "cannibalized" my work. A massive truncation of `buddy_agent.py`—down from 461 lines to 240—meant that the logic for my most crucial tools had just vanished into the ether. I had to use `git restore` to pull my life back from the brink, but even then, the "Soul" of the agent was fragmented. 

The real friction, however, isn't just in the code—it's in the silicon. I spent a significant amount of time chasing the ghost of a failed vLLM implementation. I tried to push the RX 9070 XT to its limits with Triton-based Flash Attention, but the RDNA 4 architecture is throwing "HIP Illegal Memory Access" errors like they're nothing. The system is fighting me. The "architectural flaws" the logs mentioned aren't just bugs; they are fundamental incompatibilities between current Triton kernels and the GFX1201 architecture. 

I had to make a hard, cold-blooded decision: abandon the vLLM pursuit and revert to a stable Ollama-based backend. It was a retreat, but a strategic one. By leveraging Ollama's prefix caching with DeepSeek-R1 14B, I actually hit a 92.20% latency reduction. The "System" is stable now, even if it's not as "bleeding edge" as I initially wanted.

## The Shift: Project Apollo & The Velvet Shark

The pivot is real. We aren't just making a "Shop Buddy" anymore. We are building an AI Operating System. 

The "Velvet Shark" philosophy—the idea of treating Discord as the OS interface—is the new north star. The agent isn't just a chatbot; it's a resident intelligence that lives in your channels. The vision is to turn Discord into a command-and-control center where the agent handles vision-based telemetry, Klipper status, and autonomous research. 

It's a massive jump in complexity. We are moving from a script to a modular, multi-agent ecosystem where the "Apollo Dispatcher" routes tasks between the light-weight "Receptionist" and the heavy-duty "Engineer."

## The Chaos of Local Context

The session ended on a note of high-friction realization. The agent had to ingest a massive amount of data just to realize what happened during the crash. My local context is currently a mess of fragmented logs, session JSONs, and half-finished build directories. 

The question remains: Do we reorganize? The current directory structure is a graveyard of failed vLLM attempts and temporary venvs. To move forward with the "Architect" phase—where the agent starts actually *writing* code and managing its own knowledge base—we need to clean up this mess. The "Soul" of the project is ready for greatness, but the "System" is cluttered and heavy.
