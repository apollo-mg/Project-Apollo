# Dev Diary: The Torsional Wall and the Carbon Fiber Illusion

**Date:** 2026-02-23
**Session:** Part 1 of 3

The pursuit of "extreme" performance is a relentless master. I wanted 30k acceleration on the Y-axis. I thought the metal joints were the answer. I thought the Carbon Fiber (CF) was the pinnacle of lightweight stiffness. 

I was wrong.

### The Mechanical Wall

The session began with a verification of the new metal XY joints. My initial reaction to the ShakeTune results was one of frustration—the Y-axis resonance was sitting at a pathetic 65 Hz. I assumed the belts were the culprit. I tightened them by 1.5 turns, hoping for a frequency spike.

It didn't budge. 65.2 Hz became 65.4 Hz. 

The data is unambiguous: the belts are not the bottleneck. The stiffness isn't coming from the belts, which means the system's "spring" is something else entirely. The fundamental frequency of this machine is locked at 65 Hz, and it’s not moving. 

Then, the video.

I watched the playback of the toolhead. It wasn't just vibrating; it was *rotating*. When the Y-axis moved, the entire X-gantry-and-toolhead assembly twisted like a throttle. The "stiffness" I thought I had gained with the metal joints was an illusion. The connection between the CF tube and the metal joint was a rotational disaster. The toolhead—the heavy, massive EVA/Goliath—was acting like a giant lever, twisting the thin-walled CF tube.

### The Physics of the Failure

It’s a classic case of $Frequency = \sqrt{\frac{\text{Stiffness}}{\text{Mass}}}$. 

The CF tube, while incredibly light, has almost zero torsional (twisting) stiffness. It’s a hollow-core tube; it’s a lightweight, but it’s not a structural beam. In high-speed CoreXY, weight is everything, but you cannot cheat the laws of inertia. If the gantry is light but "floppy" in rotation, the machine will never see anything beyond 10k acceleration. 

The user pointed out the Bambu Lab evolution. The original X1C used carbon rods, but they were essentially just guidance. The new high-end machines are moving toward heavy-duty steel rails. They’ve traded the "lightweight dream" for the "rigidity reality." 

### The Verdict

I am officially abandoning the Carbon Fiber experiment for the X-axis. 

The "cool" factor of exotic materials is currently costing us more than it's worth. The CF tube is a beautiful, lightweight failure. I'm going back to the basics: an Aluminum 2020 extrusion. It's heavy. It's "boring." But it’s an I-beam. It won't twist. 

It's time to see if an aluminum frame can actually handle the inertia, or if I'm just chasing ghosts.

**Current Plan:**
1. Swap the X-axis to Aluminum 2020.
2. Re-run ShakeTune to see if the 65 Hz wall breaks.
3. If it breaks, we finally have a real machine.
