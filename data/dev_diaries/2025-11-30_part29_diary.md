# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2025-11-30 (Part 29)

## The Ghost in the VRAM: A Battle of Tiling and Trashing

Today was a grueling exercise in the friction between high-level generative intent and the brutal reality of 16GB VRAM. We were pushing the limits of the **Wan 2.1** video model, and for a moment, it felt like we were touching the "Soul" of the engine—the ability to generate coherent, high-fidelity video. But the "System" is a jealous master.

### The VRAM Wall
The core of our technical struggle today was the **VAE Decoding** stage. We successfully bypassed the initial model loading and text encoding hurdles (thanks to the `DisTorch` memory patching), but as soon as the model attempted to decode the 4D/5D video tensors, the hardware hit the wall. 

The `WanVideoDecode` node is a beast. Even with `enable_vae_tiling` active, the sheer volume of data for a video generation exceeded the 16GB limit of the **RX 9070 XT**. We were caught in the classic "Slow vs. Crash" trap: the tiling was trading massive computational overhead for memory efficiency, but the sheer scale of the latent space was still too much for the current tiling configuration.

### The DisTorch Discrepancy
During the crash, I dug into the `DisTorch` logs, and I found something truly bizarre. The `DisTorch2 Model Virtual VRAM Analysis` was reporting an astronomical **114GB** for the `Linear` layers, even though the actual model weight size was only ~9GB. 

This is a critical architectural red flag. It suggests a massive discrepancy in how `module_size` is being calculated during the `safetensor` loading phase—potentially a double-counting error or a misinterpretation of the bit-depth in the `Linear` layer tuples. This "ghost memory" is causing the allocation logic to panic, offloading nearly 100% of the model to the CPU. While this prevents a hard crash, it turns a high-performance GPU engine into a crawling, CPU-bound zombie.

**Lesson Learned:** The tiling strategy for video VAEs must be more aggressive, or we need to implement a more granular `tile_size` control for high-dimensional video tensors.

### The Environment Chaos
The session ended with a frustrating descent into "Environment Hell." A system reboot wiped the transient state of our virtual environments. We discovered that the `venv_rocm711` environment—which we had just painstakingly configured for ROCm 7.1.1—was either corrupted or missing key dependencies like `PyYAML`. 

The discrepancy between the `ls` output (showing `venv_rocm711`) and the actual contents (missing `torch`) suggests that the environment creation process is not atomic. If a process is interrupted, you end up with a "half-baked" environment that looks valid but is functionally useless.

### Architectural Path Forward
1.  **Refine Tiling Logic:** Implement a more robust `tile_stride` and `tile_size` scaling for the `WanVideoDecode` node.
2.  **Fix DisTorch Accounting:** Debug the `module_size` calculation in the `safetensor` loader to stop the false-positive VRAM reports.
3.  **Environment Stability:** Move toward containerized or more strictly managed virtual environments to prevent the "reboot-induced amnesia" we experienced today.

The engine is alive, but it's currently too heavy to walk. We need to trim the fat.
