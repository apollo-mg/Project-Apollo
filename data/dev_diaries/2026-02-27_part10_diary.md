# Dev Diary: 2026-02-27 | The Linux Migration & The Vision Breakthrough

The transition from the old environment to Ubuntu has been less of a "migration" and more of a violent restructuring. I thought I was just moving files, but the reality of local-first AI is that you aren't just moving data; you're fighting the very nature of how the OS perceives your hardware.

## The Silicon Friction: ROCm vs. Vulkan
The biggest headache today was the VRAM bottleneck. I was trying to run the Qwen2.5-VL vision model, but the Ollama service was hitting a wall. I had configured it to use the Vulkan backend to get a slight speed boost for the DeepSeek-R1 model, but the vision model was a total disaster. The Vulkan driver was throwing constant `ErrorOutOfDeviceMemory` crashes, failing to allocate buffers. 

It was a classic case of "Soul" vs. "System." I wanted the high-level intelligence of Qwen, but the "System" (the Vulkan implementation) couldn't have the stability to support it. I had to make a hard architectural choice: I reverted the service to native ROCm. I lost about 4 tokens-per-second on the DeepSeek model, but it gave me a stable, functional vision pipeline. The trade-off was non-negotiable.

## The Ghost in the Machine: Linux Permissions
Moving the Ollama models to the external TG 2TB drive introduced a nightmare of permission errors. The `ollama` system user couldn't traverse the `/media/mark/` directory. I had to patch the systemd service to run as my user (`mark`) just to break the sandbox. It’s a messy fix, but in the world of local-first dev, sometimes the quickest way to get the "Soul" working is to break the "System's" rules.

## The Vision Breakthrough
Once the backend was stabilized on ROCm, the vision pipeline finally breathed. I ran a test script—simulating a Discord `[FORCE VISION]` command—and the results were staggering. The model successfully identified a screenshot of the terminal, proving the end-to-end vision-capture-to-analysis chain is alive and well. We have eyes.

## The Modular Reality
I spent a significant portion of the session realizing that the documentation was lying to me. My roadmap said we had completed the "Module Extraction," but the code told a different story. The *structure* is modular—`core.py` and `toolbox.py` are doing the heavy lifting for the "Soul" (persona/memory), but the actual specialized "Buddies" are still living in separate bridge scripts. 

We have a modularized backbone, but we don't have a unified brain. We have the parts, but they aren't talking to each other yet.

## Current State & The Path Forward
The Discord bridge is running, the vision system is stable, and the model migration is complete. The "Desktop Eyes" (using `spectacle` to bypass the Wayland/scrot black-screen issue) is officially working. 

The next architectural leap is clear: **The Apollo Dispatcher**. We need a lightweight routing agent (Llama 3.2) that lives in `apollo.py` to act as the gatekeeper, deciding when to wake up the heavy DeepSeek-R1 or the Qwen vision models. We're moving away from a collection of scripts and toward a true autonomous agent.

**Next Objective:** Implement the Dispatcher to unify the Shop and Discord bridges.
