# Dev Diary: 2026-01-09

The machine is screaming at me. Not in a metaphorical sense, but in a literal, mechanical-resonance kind of way. I've spent the better part of the day fighting the physics of my own hardware, and the results are both beautiful and infuriating.

## The Physics of the "Shake"

I spent the morning trying to find the sweet spot for Square Corner Velocity (SCV) on my customized Ender 6/Goliath setup. I was attempting to calibrate the cornering speed at 6,000 mm/s² acceleration, expecting it to handle the high-speed profile I've designed.

I was wrong.

The print was a disaster. The Y-axis is acting like a giant tuning fork, throwing massive ringing ripples through the entire part. It’s a classic cantilever problem—my heavy aftermarket bed plate is basically a pendulum, and when the gantry slams into a corner, the whole frame oscillates. 

The "Soul" of the machine—the precision I want—is being strangled by the "System"—the physical limitations of the stock Ender 6 frame.

I had to make a tactical retreat. I dropped the outer wall acceleration down to 3,000 mm/s² and the speed to 120 mm/s. After running a VFA (Vertical Fine Artifact) test, the results were enlightening. At low speeds (40-60 mm/s), the stepper motors are "cogging"—the resonance is disgusting. But there is a "magic window" around 120 mm/s where the surface is incredibly clean. It's a narrow band of stability, but it's a band I can work with.

## The Collision of Software and Silicon

Later in the day, I tried to run an automated acceleration tuning tower. I was getting frustrated because the acceleration was capping out at 2,000 mm/s² despite my command. 

It was a brilliant lesson in the friction between my intent and the slicer's logic. I was treating the firmware as the master, but the OrcaSlicer G-code was the dictator. Every time the printer reached a new feature, the slicer was injecting `M204` commands, overriding my `SET_VELOCITY_LIMIT` macro. The machine wasn't broken; it was just being told to be slow by a piece of code I hadn't accounted for.

Lesson learned: In the world of local AI and high-speed robotics, the "System" (the G-code/Slicer) often has its own agenda that can override your "Soul" (the high-level architecture).

## The Roadmap to "Version 3.0"

The jaggedness in the vertical edge of my Z-axis is a persistent ghost. It's a Z-wobble—either the cantilever bed is bouncing or the lead screw is vibrating. 

I've looked into the WidowMaker project. It’s a beast. It uses the Voron Trident kinematics with a triple-Z setup. It’s exactly what I need to kill the Z-axis noise, but it's a massive undertaking. It would mean a total teardown and a complete re-engineering of the bed-frame interface to accommodate the linear rails.

My current plan:
1.  **Immediate:** Fabricate a 5mm aluminum top plate to stiffen the Y-axis and kill that ringing.
2.  **Intermediate:** Settle into the 120mm/s / 3k Accel "sweet spot" for my current profile.
3.  **Long-term:** The Tri-Z conversion. 

I'm building a hybrid—a "Vz-Trident-6." It’s messy, it’s experimental, and it’s probably going to require a drill press and a lot of caffeine, but it's the only way to turn this Frankenstein of a machine into something that actually rivals a Voron.

Time to go grab the drill.
