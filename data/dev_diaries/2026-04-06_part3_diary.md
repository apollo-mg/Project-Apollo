# Dev Diary: 2026-04-06 (Part 3)

## The Heat is the Limit

Today was a brutal reminder that even with the RX 9070 XT, I am still fighting a losing battle against thermodynamics. 

The core architecture of Project Apollo is becoming increasingly complex, and the sheer volume of context being pumped through the system is starting to hit a wall. I've been running into a recurring issue where the GPU core stays relatively cool, but the VRAM temperatures spike to 105°C during heavy matrix multiplication. It's the classic consumer-grade bottleneck: the BIOS is tuned for gaming, not for the sustained, high-bandwidth memory thrashing that happens when a local LLM starts evaluating massive prompt contexts.

I had to sit down with Gemini and hardcode the thermal management habit into the global memory bank (`~/.gemini/GEMINI.md`). I used the `save_memory` agent to ensure the system understands that I’m using LACT to manage the fan curves specifically for the VRAM. It’s a vital piece of the "System." If the silicon throttles, the "Soul"—the creative intelligence we're trying to cultivate—suffocates.

I also have `Coolercontrol` running a custom sensor for the case fans to compensate, but it's a constant game of whack-a-mole between software optimization and physical heat dissipation.

The friction is real. We are trying to build a massive, multi-agent ecosystem, but we are tethered to the thermal limits of a single workstation.

On the bright side, the `synthesizer_batch.log` shows the Daydream daemon was successfully caught and paused (`PID 10236`). This was a critical move. By killing that background noise, I've cleared the VRAM entirely, allowing the primary process to have 100% of the hardware. The system is now plowing through the current prompt evaluations. 

It's a weird, lonely feeling—watching a machine-driven synthesis process while I just sit there, waiting for the `master_action_plan.md` to drop. The architecture is hungry, and the hardware is struggling to keep up. But the way the synthesizer caught the daemon shows the "System" is finally becoming intelligent enough to manage its own resource contention. 

We'll see if the master plan holds, or if the thermal ceiling breaks it first.
