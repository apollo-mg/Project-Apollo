**Project Apollo: Dev Diary – 2025-11-30 (Part 6) – The Flash Attention Fumble**

It is the small things that bog down the pipeline. Today, my RX 9070 XT was humming, but slowly. Despite a seemingly healthy VRAM headroom on the new ROCm 7.1.1 driver, my video generations were dragging. The Soul—the creative flow of the Wan 2.2 model—was being throttled by the System.

I dug into the history, cross-referencing the raw logs. The culprit was a setting buried in a script. The older, working configuration lived in `run_wan_safe.sh`, where `FLASH_ATTENTION_TRITON_AMD_ENABLE` was `TRUE`. The new script was falling back to `split-cross-attention`, which, while stable, was burning more VRAM and cycles.

I moved to restore the optimized path, but the environment, `venv_711`, was stale. The friction began. First, `pyyaml` was missing from the virtual environment. Then `triton`. Then, a `ModuleNotFoundError` for `torch` when trying to build `flash-attn` from source. The dependency graph was fractal—installing one package revealed the absence of another.

I made a tactical call to unblock the flow. I disabled Triton Flash Attention temporarily, explicitly enabling `split-cross-attention` to ensure stability. But the cleanup continues. `torchsde` is now installed. `torchaudio` is next.

**The Epiphany:** A clean `venv` is a happy `venv`. I am rebuilding `venv_rocm711` from the wheels—`torch-2.8.0+rocm7.1.0`, `triton-3.4.0`, `pyyaml`—ensuring the foundation is rock-solid before I chase the speed of Flash Attention again. The System must be ordered for the Soul to run wild.

**Lesson Learned:** Never underestimate the cost of a missing `yaml` or a mis-matched `torch` version when the GPU is working hard. We need to get the dependencies right before we can push the creative envelope.
