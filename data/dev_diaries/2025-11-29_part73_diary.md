Dev Diary: 2025-11-29 — The HIP of It All

I was wrestling Wan 2.1 (14B) onto my 16GB RX 9070 XT when the OOM hit. SDPA wanted the full attention matrix, and the math didn't fit. I flipped the `--use-split-cross-attention` flag to slice the query tensor into manageable chunks. Peak memory dropped, but the generation died again—not with a gasp of "out of memory," but with a `hipErrorIllegalAddress`.

The stack trace led me to `apply_rope1`, right at `x_out = term1 + term2`. The VRAM wasn't full (the logs showed ~16GB free), but the pointer was wrong. It looks like a fragmentation quirk in the ROCm stack, or perhaps a stride alignment issue when the model layers were juggling between the GPU and the CPU cache (those 4GB moved to cpu in the DisTorch logs).

This is the good friction. The Soul (14B params of narrative potential) meets the System (the cold, hard limit of the silicon). Split attention buys me the memory headroom, but it doesn't fix a broken kernel. The trade-off is clear: I can trade speed for stability (30-50% slower sampling) to keep the creative flow alive.

Lesson for the local-first ecosystem: Total VRAM capacity isn't the whole story. Fragmentation is the silent killer. If you are running big, local models, check the kernel and the fragmentation before you check the quantization. The 9070 XT is hungry; it just needs a map that matches the terrain.
