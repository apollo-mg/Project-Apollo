# Apollo Dev Diary: The Ghost, The Glitch, and The GLM

**Date:** 2026-03-04
**Time:** 06:00 AM
**Status:** Cold Storage Transfer (400GB) | GLM-4.7 Spilling to Sysmem
**Hardware:** AMD RX 9070 XT (16GB) / PCIe Bottleneck

It’s 3 AM and my desk looks like a crime scene. Terminal windows are stacked three deep, the RX 9070 XT is pushing 214W of power draw, and the air is thick with the smell of ozone and defeat. We caught a ghost tonight.

### The Vampire Process
We had a VRAM leak that defied logic. `ollama ps` showed nothing, yet the GPU was bleeding memory. I spent an hour hunting through PIDs and `fuser` outputs until I found it: a zombie `ollama runner` process (PID 22496) from a Qwen 3.5 test an hour ago. 

It didn't die. It just hung there, pinned at 100% CPU, gnawing on 3GB of VRAM in an infinite loop. The ROCm driver stack is notoriously bad at cleaning up memory maps when a script crashes. These processes linger, turning your hardware into a brick until we manually `pkill -9` them. It’s a reminder: the silicon doesn't care about our code. It just holds onto resources until you force it to let go.

### The 400GB Migration
We are starved for speed. To get the fast NVMe space we need for the active models, we have to do some heavy lifting. I kicked off a massive 400GB transfer of unused "cold" models from the `TG 2TB` to the `Backup 3TB` SATA drive. 

This is the friction between our "Soul" (the desire for instant, fluid AI) and the "System" (the physical reality of SATA drives). While the transfer runs, the drive is choked with tiny Python libraries. It's slow. It's painful. But once it clears that space, we can shrink the NTFS partition and carve out a pure `ext4` partition on the NVMe. No more file system overhead. Just raw, unadulterated throughput for the models.

### The Arrival of GLM-4.7-Flash
The new model has arrived. `GLM-4.7-Flash-Uncensored-Heretic` is a beast, and it's struggling to fit into our 16GB VRAM.

*   **The Specs:** 19GB GGUF size. Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) backbone.
*   **The Performance:** It hit a 214W peak on the GPU, but because it doesn't fit, it's spilling heavily into System RAM.
*   **The Result:** 16.5 TPS. 

It’s thinking. It's outputting deep logic chains and retaining context, but the PCIe bus is choking on the data. It's like trying to run a supercar on a gravel road. The model is brilliant—SWE-bench verified, 95% on AIME math—but until we get it fully onto the GPU, it's just a smart terminal emulator.

### Scripting the Future
I tried to run our `edge_case_gauntlet.py` and `creative_discovery_tests.py` on the new GLM model, and the tests failed. Why? Because the scripts were hardcoded with model names for Qwen and Llama. I was trying to force a square peg into a round hole. 

I'm rewriting the runners now. We need dynamic model loading. We can't have the test suite dictating which models we can run. The code has to be fluid. I'm setting up a unified script that pulls the prompts dynamically, ensuring that when we test GLM-4.7, it actually talks to the right instance.

### Next Steps
The file transfer is at 10% and counting. Once it's done, I'll wipe the `TG 2TB` drive, set up the fast `ext4` partition, and force `GLM-4.7` to live on the NVMe. No more RAM spillover. No more bottlenecks. Just pure, high-speed inference on the 9070 XT.

We're building a local-first ecosystem where the hardware dictates the limits, not the other way around. And tonight, the hardware fought back. But I think we just won.
