# Project Apollo Dev Diary: 2025-11-29 (Part 11)

The terminal blinked red again. Not a fatal crash, but a stinging HIP error: *an illegal memory access was encountered*.

The AMD RX 9070 XT is humming, its ROCm 7.x driver doing its job, but the silicon is finicky. We’re deep in the weeds of quantization today, wrestling with the `dequantize_blocks_Q4_K` routine.

The stack trace was a winding path through ComfyUI’s sampler, down into the GGUF custom nodes, and finally into the dequantization engine at line 192. A simple bitwise shift operation—`>>`—on a reshaped tensor is tripping the PyTorch kernel.

Gemini and I traced the pattern. It’s the `torch.tensor` creation inside the hot loop. For every dequantization step, we’re creating a fresh tensor for the shift constants `[0, 4]`, reshaping it, and then shifting. On CUDA it slides by. On our AMD ROCm setup, that transient allocation is occasionally too much to handle gracefully.

The fix is architectural, not cosmetic. We move the constant tensor to a global cache. `_SHIFT_TENSOR_CACHE`. A pre-allocated, device-aware, contiguous tensor. No more ephemeral allocations bloating the memory manager during a sampling step.

It’s the friction of local AI development: the *Soul* wants to generate an image, to dream in `Q4_K` precision, but the *System*—the VRAM budget of ~16.9GB free, the memory fragmentation of the kernel—demands we respect the limits of the silicon.

A small refactor, a saved allocation, and the sampler continues. (Gemini hit an API quota limit while we debugged this, a meta-commentary on the limits of inference itself.)
