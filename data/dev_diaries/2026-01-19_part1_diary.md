# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-01-19 (Part 1)

## The Z-Axis Chaos: Fighting the Ghost of Mechanical Binding

Today was a brutal lesson in the friction between theoretical geometry and the reality of physical assembly. I thought I had the math for the Tri-Z configuration sorted—approximating the Voron Trident geometry onto the Ender 6 frame. I spent a good chunk of the session refining the `z_tilt` coordinates, trying to get the probe triangle just right so the bed could level itself without crashing.

The architecture is there. The code is there. But the hardware is being a stubborn, noisy beast.

### The Micro-Correction Loop
The first hurdle was the motor assignments. I realized I was mapping the drivers incorrectly to the physical motors. Swapping them around to align the TMC5160 with the Front-Left (Z) was a necessary correction, but as soon as I applied the new `printer.cfg`, the system threw a tantrum. 

The error `Unable to write tmc spi 'stepper_z'` is the bane of the TMC5160. It's a communication failure—either the jumpers on the Octopus board aren't set for SPI, or the driver isn't getting enough juice. I spent time debugging the driver-to-MCU communication, eventually realizing that the driver's orientation or the SPI bus settings were the bottleneck. 

### The "Loud" Reality
After troubleshooting the communication, I moved to the electrical/mechanical testing. I used the `STEPPER_BUZZ` command to verify each motor's direction. It’s a small, 1mm test, but it’s the only way to see if the motors are fighting themselves.

Everything looked correct. The motors moved "UP" (decreasing Z distance) as expected. But the second I tried to run a real `G28` (Home Z) command, the machine revolted. The motors emitted a violent, grinding noise, and the bed—heavy with the weight of the frame—slowly began to sink. 

It’s a classic diagnostic nightmare. Is it a torque issue? Is it a speed-to-current ratio mismatch? Or is it mechanical binding?

If the motors can move 1mm individually but jam when all three try to lift the bed together, it's almost certainly a **mechanical binding issue**. The frame is likely not perfectly parallel. When moving a single motor, the frame has enough "play" to absorb the misalignment. But when all three Z-actuators move at once, they are essentially wedging the bed frame into its own rails. The rigid geometry is fighting the reality of a slightly twisted frame.

### Core Lessons & Systemic Friction
The tension here is between the **Soul** (the elegant, automated leveling logic we've built) and the **System** (the actual, physical, slightly-crooked metal frame). 

*   **Lesson 1: Digital perfection is useless without mechanical tolerance.** A perfect `z_tilt` map won't matter if the physical assembly is a geometric lie.
*   **Lesson 2: The "Small-Scale Illusion."** The `STEPPER_BUZZ` test gave me a false sense of security. Because it only moved 1mm, I didn't see the binding. The real struggle only happens when the load is sustained.
*   **Lesson 3: The Debugging Loop.** Moving the drivers to different slots (5, 6, 7) was a smart move to isolate the board vs. the driver, but it didn't solve the fundamental issue of the motors fighting the physical geometry.

Next steps: I need to check the lead screw alignment and probably loosen the nut-to-rail tension just enough to let the frame "breathe" while we test. We're not just building a printer; we're fighting the laws of physics.

**Current Status:** Hardware-locked. Need mechanical intervention before the software can breathe.
