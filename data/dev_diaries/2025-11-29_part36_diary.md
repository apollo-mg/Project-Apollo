The RX 9070 XT Hisssed

The 9070 XT hissed today. A classic `torch.OutOfMemoryError` stopped the Wan 2.1 in its tracks. Not a shortage of capacity—the 16GB of VRAM is generous—but a shortage of order. The stochastic rounding, that subtle FP8 conversion, reached for a mere 34MiB and found the memory manager in a bind.

I adjusted the dial. One gigabyte of reserved VRAM was too thin a margin; three gigabytes felt right. The DisTorch loader was shuffling the 14B-parameter weights between GPU and CPU, but the fragmentation was real. `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF` with `expandable_segments:True` now guards the boundaries, smoothing the rough edges of the ROCm driver.

The soul of Project Apollo is local-first, multi-agent, and unbound. But the system—cold, hard HIP memory limits—demands respect. I learned that quantization is not free; the conversion itself costs memory. For our local-first ecosystem to thrive on desktop silicon, we must treat memory as a finite garden, not an infinite field.

Lessons for the road: Reserve early. Respect the fragmentation. And always, always check the swap.
