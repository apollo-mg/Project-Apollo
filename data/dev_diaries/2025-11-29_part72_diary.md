## 2025-11-29: The 15.45 GiB Beast

I'm staring at a `torch.OutOfMemoryError` that refused to be tamed. My RX 9070 XT—15.92 GiB of precious VRAM—was asked to swallow a 15.45 GiB allocation for a single attention matrix. Even with the model quantized to fp8, the math backend was materializing the full attention map for a long Wan 2.1 video sequence, and the GPU simply ran out of room.

The friction here is classic: the Soul of the creative workflow (fluid video generation) bumping into the System of hard silicon limits. I was using `--lowvram` and `--use-split-cross-attention`, but the fix was broken by my own hand.

Earlier, I had patched `attention.py` for AMD stability, but in doing so, I'd forced PyTorch SDPA. That override had silently killed the `split-cross-attention` logic right when I needed it most. The split wasn't splitting because my override was telling the code to go straight to the "Math" backend—efficient, yes, but memory-hungry for large sequence lengths.

The epiphany came at line 550. I revised the AMD-specific override to check `use_split_cross_attention` first. If the flag is set, I now leverage `attention_split` with its einsum operations. It's lighter on VRAM, trading a bit of compute for the memory headroom I desperately need.

The lesson is architectural: in a local-first ecosystem, we cannot simply dump the SDPA optimization onto AMD and call it a day. The "Math" backend is not free for lunch when VRAM is scarce. I need to respect the flag, favor the split, and let the GPU breathe.
