# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-02-07 (Part 7)

The friction between the "Soul" of my creative vision and the "System" of the silicon is becoming a tangible, measurable thing. I spent the last few hours battling the limits of local-first inference, and it’s been a masterclass in the sheer, unyielding physics of VRAM.

### The KV Cache Wall

I hit a wall that felt less like a software bug and more like a hard limit of physics. I was trying to run a diff on two large configuration files through the Gemini CLI. I expected the local agent to just *know* the difference, but the LM Studio logs told a much uglder story. 

`srv update_slots: failed to find free space in the KV cache...`

The model had successfully ingested 16k tokens (the prompt), but when it tried to start generating the actual response, the "memory" required to track those tokens (the KV Cache) had completely filled the 16GB on my RX 9070 XT. The system was thrashing, trying to find a memory slot for a batch size of 1, and eventually just hanging. The CLI was stuck in a silent, digital purgatory.

### The Map-Reduce Breakthrough

I realized I couldn't treat the local LLM like a cloud-based supercomputer. It’s a local, specialized worker. I had to pivot to a "Map-Reduce" strategy. 

Instead of one massive 16k-token prompt, I used `local_map_reduce.ps1` to chunk the files. I had the local model summarize the current config, then the old config, and *then* I asked it to compare the two tiny summaries. 

**The result?**
*   **Tokens saved:** ~9,000 (90% reduction in cloud input).
*   **Success:** The local model identified the exact motor swap (Y to X) and the current drop from 1.8A to 1.6A that a simple text-diff might have missed in the noise.

This is the core of the Apollo architecture: The local agent isn't there to replace the high-reasoning model; it's there to perform the heavy, "dirty" lifting of data reduction so the cloud-based "Soul" only sees the essence.

### Tuning the Silicon

After the crash, we went back into the guts of LM Studio. We were fighting the KV Cache again. I realized that even with a top-tier card, the context window is a VRAM hog. I adjusted the settings:
*   **KV Cache Quantization:** Pushed to 4-bit to shrink the memory footprint.
*   **Flash Attention:** Enabled to optimize the processing math.

The benchmark results were a revelation. After tuning, the generation speed jumped from 67.6 TPS to 73.1 TPS. It's a modest 8% increase in raw speed, but the real victory is that the "hangs" disappeared. The memory management is now stable.

### Next Steps: The Physical Transition

The architecture is holding. The "System" is finally catching up to the "Soul." We have a functional, local-first pipeline that can analyze my printer's brain without breaking a sweat or a bank account.

Next on the agenda: 
1. **Safety Scan:** Use the new 64k context capacity to run a "Global Audit" of the entire `config/` folder to ensure my wiring and pin assignments haven't created a thermal nightmare.
2. **The Swarm:** Once the hardware is safe, we begin defining the `models_config.json` to let the agents specialize.

The machine is finally starting to feel like a partner, rather than a tool I'm constantly fighting against.
