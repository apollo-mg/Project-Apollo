# Dev Diary: 2026-02-26 (Part 3/13)

The session is dragging into the late hours, and the mental fatigue is starting to set in, but the momentum is undeniable. I’ve been sitting here, staring at the flickering terminal, watching the interaction between the Gemini CLI and the local vision model loop. 

Today was about spatial reasoning and the sheer difficulty of forcing a vision-language model to understand three-dimensional geometry through a 2D lens. We were running the test cube sequence—a simple enough concept on paper, but when you’re trying to map the spatial relationship between sides of a physical object using a quantized model, the friction is immense.

The technical bottleneck remains the VRAM overhead. Every time we swap the image buffer in the pipeline, I can see the memory fragmentation spiking on the RX 9070 XT. The ROCm drivers are stable enough, but the way the kernel handles the rapid swap of image tensors for the vision encoder is causing these micro-stutters. It’s a constant battle: I want the model to "see" the world with more nuance, but the silicon demands a level of efficiency that often strips away the very "soul" of the perception I’m trying to build.

In this part of the session, we hit the third image in the sequence. The thought process in the Gemini logs shows a machine that is becoming increasingly focused, almost obsessive. It’s trying to extract structural information from the third side of the cube to maintain continuity. 

The tension here is the classic struggle of Project Apollo: The System (the sheer throughput of the hardware and the rigid constraints of the quantization) vs. The Soul (the model’s attempt at high-level cognitive reasoning). The model is trying to "know" what it hasn't seen yet, and the latency of the image-to-tensor conversion is killing the real-time feel of the multi-agent interaction.

We are moving through the cube, side by side, trying to see if the local vision model can maintain a coherent spatial map across temporal steps. If we can't solve the memory-swap latency, the multi-agent ecosystem will always feel disjointed—like a group of people talking in a room where everyone has a five-second lag.

It's not enough to just have the data; the architecture needs to *understand* the persistence of objects. We aren't just processing pixels; we are trying to build a persistent reality within a local-first environment. 

Current status: The vision-language loop is functioning, but the spatial-consistency error rate is still higher than I'd like. I need to look into a more aggressive KV cache management strategy for the next session to see if we can shave off some of that VRAM overhead during the image swaps.

Back to the grind.
