# Dev Diary: The Precision War

**Date:** 2025-12-02  
**Status:** System Stabilized (mostly)  
**Hardware:** AMD RX 9070 XT | ROCm 7.1 | Local-First Multi-Agent Architecture

The machine is breathing again, but man, it’s a temperamental beast. 

There is a specific kind of frustration that comes with building a local-first ecosystem on RDNA 4. You have all this raw, unadulterated power sitting in your chassis, but you’re constantly walking a tightrope between extreme performance and total kernel collapse. Tonight was a masterclass in that friction.

The session started with the familiar, ugly stutter of a crash. I was pushing the Wan model through the GGUF pipeline, trying to see just how much we could squeeze out of the VRAM, when the terminal spat out that classic, soul-crater error: 
`RuntimeError: self and mat2 must have the same dtype, but got Half and Float`.

In the NVIDIA world, you can get away with a certain level of "sloppy" math. Their kernels are forgiving; they see a mix of FP16 and FP32 and they just... make it work. But AMD? ROCm is a precision extremist. The "Math" backend kernels on RDNA 4 are beautiful, mathematically elegant, and absolutely unforgiving. If you hand them a tensor where the query and the key don't match exactly, they don't try to fix it for you. They just die.

It turns out the culprit was the `ComfyUI-GGUF` node. It was doing what we wanted—casting weights to FP16 to save memory—but it was leaving the bias vectors floating in FP32. To the hardware, this is a fundamental violation of logic. A mismatch of `Half` and `Float` is a mismatch of reality.

I had to go surgical. Gemini helped me track it down to `custom_nodes/ComfyUI-GGUF/ops.py`. The fix was simple but vital: explicitly forcing the bias to match the target `dtype` of the weights. It’s a tiny patch, but in the context of a local-first architecture, these tiny "glue" fixes are what actually allow the "Soul" of the model to exist on the "System" of the silicon.

We also hit a bit of a tangent regarding optimization. I was looking into Sage Attention, tempted by the performance gains, but realized we're playing a different game here. Sage is NVIDIA-centric, heavily reliant on Triton kernels tuned for Hopper/Ampere. For our RDNA 4 setup, we have to be smarter. We’re leveraging the ROCm fork of Flash Attention and using the Triton backend to bypass the instabilities of the standard C++ implementations that tend to freak out on the Wave32 architecture.

It’s a different path. It’s harder. There’s more friction. But there’s something deeply satisfying about it. We aren't just running someone else's black-box optimization; we are architecting a specific, high-performance stack that actually understands the hardware it's running on.

**Core Lessons from Today:**
* **The Precision Tax:** On AMD, "good enough" precision leads to immediate crashes. Every tensor operation must be a perfect match.
* **The ROCm Divergence:** We can't just port NVIDIA libraries and expect them to work. We have to respect the specificities of the ROCm/Triton relationship.
* **The Power of the Patch:** In a multi-agent, local-first setup, the ability to rapidly hot-fix custom nodes is our greatest asset. The architecture is living; it’s constantly evolving through these micro-adjustments.

The system is back online. The weights are quantized, the dtypes are aligned, and the machine is finally speaking the same language as the model.

**End of Entry.**
