# Dev Diary: 2026-04-06 (Part 6)

The friction between silicon and soul is getting harder to manage. I’m staring at a 16GB VRAM ceiling that feels more like a brick wall than a boundary. 

I spent the bulk of today wrestling with the RDNA 4 architecture. The RX 9070 XT is a beast, but ROCm is still acting like a temperamental child. I’ve been chasing "Illegal Memory Access" errors through the bowels of the Wan 2.1/2.2 video generation pipeline. It’s not just a software bug; it’s a fundamental mismatch in how the kernels handle memory strides on this hardware.

Every time I try to implement a performance-boosting feature—like Triton-based Flash Attention—the system throws a tantrum. I tried to force it, but the driver just crashes during the `torch.cuda.synchronize()` calls or when it hits a non-contiguous tensor during a LoRA application. 

I had to get surgical. I’ve been writing these "Nuclear Option" laundering patterns: forcing tensors through a CPU-to-FP32-to-Contiguous-to-Numpy pipeline just to bypass buggy stream-copy kernels. It’s ugly. It’s inefficient. But it works. It’s the only way to get the 14B model to actually run without the entire session collapsing into a memory death spiral.

The architectural breakthrough came from realizing that I can't trust the "optimized" paths. I had to hard-code fallbacks to standard PyTorch SDPA (Math mode) because the specialized kernels for RDNA 4 are just too unstable for multi-modal workloads right now. It costs me VRAM—forcing the `tile_size` down to 256—but it gives me stability. In the hierarchy of my project, stability is the "Soul," and the "System" is just a series of increasingly complex workarounds to keep that soul alive.

I also successfully unlocked a 65,536-token context window on the 9070 XT. This was a massive win. Using custom `llama.cpp` builds with TurboQuant Asymmetric KV Caching (`-ctk q8_0 -ctv turbo3`) and MoE Micro-Batching (`-ub 64`), I finally broke past the `MUL_MAT_ID` crash that has been plagining the local 35B MoE. It’s a massive leap for local-first autonomy.

The technical lessons from today are clear:
- **The Contiguity Mandate:** On RDNA 4, non-contiguous tensors are poison. If you aren't calling `.contiguous()` before a cast or a move, you're asking for an Illegal Memory Access.
- **The SDMA Problem:** I've had to disable SDMA (`HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=0`) for several tasks. The driver-level instability during async copies is too high to ignore.
- **The VRAM Tetris:** My architecture is becoming a game of managing fragmentation. I'm now using `max_split_size_mb` and aggressive garbage collection thresholds just to keep the model from choking the system.

As I look toward the next phase, the goal is to move from these reactive, "firefighting" patches to a more proactive, multi-agent orchestration. I want the "Receptionist" (System 1) to filter the noise, while the "Engineer" (System 2) handles the heavy lifting. But until the ROCm drivers stop acting like they're haunted, I'll keep patching, keep cloning, and keep fighting the silicon.

The transition from hobbyist to enterprise-grade AI Systems Engineer isn't built on elegant, high-level code alone; it's built on surviving these gritty, low-level hardware battles.
