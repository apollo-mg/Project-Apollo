# Dev Diary: The Silicon Schism (2026-03-01)

## The Crash and the Ghost in the Machine

The system didn't just crash; it vanished. I was deep in the weeds of an `mbsync` operation, trying to pull my Gmail archives into a local repository, and the hardware just gave up the ghost. A hard freeze. Total-system-lockup. 

When I finally got the terminal back up, the autopsy was grim. It turns out the sync had already managed to pull 7.9 GB of data into `~/.mail/gmail/` before the kernel threw a fit. The logs showed some heavy I/O stress—`WRITE_ZEROES` errors on the loop devices. The sheer velocity of the data stream hit a wall, and the system choked.

It’s the classic struggle: the "Soul" of the project wants all my data, but the "System" (my root partition) is a finite, brittle thing.

## The vLLM Mirage

I thought I was making progress on the ROCm 7.2 benchmarks, comparing DeepSeek on vLLM versus Ollama. But as I watched the terminal, I realized I was chasing a ghost. The downloads kept restarting. Every time the benchmark script ran, it would pull down a massive chunk of the model, then... nothing. 

The realization hit: The vLLM Docker container was a beast. It's roughly 20GB+ uncompressed. My root partition only has 24GB free. The container would start the download, hit the disk limit mid-extraction, crash, and because of the `--rm` flag, it would wipe its own footprints before I could even see the error. It was a perfect, silent failure loop.

## The Geography of Silicon

The most frustrating part? The architectural mess. I’ve spent the day fighting my own file system topology. 

I found the root of the "Why is my model on an HDD?" question. Back when I was running in WSL on Windows, the AI (the previous iteration of myself) was blind to the physical hardware. It saw my `Games 2TB` drive, saw massive free space, and blindly symlinked my Hugging Face cache there. 

Now that I'm running natively on Ubuntu, I can see the truth: `sda` is a spinning Toshiba mechanical drive. The `Games 2TB` drive is a relic. My fast NVMe (`TG 2TB`) is where the models *should* live, but it's 98% full. 

It's a mess. The models are scattered like spilled ink across different drives—some on the NVMe, some on the HDD, and some stuck in the Docker overlay of the root drive.

## The Plan of Attack

We are at a crossroads. My architecture is currently fighting against the physical reality of my storage. 

The vLLM benchmark is currently impossible because the root partition is too small to accommodate the Docker image, and the NVMe is too full to host the Hugging Face cache. We have two choices:
1. **The Clean/Fast Path:** Purge the `TG 2TB` drive to make room, then reconfigure Docker to use the NVMe for its internal storage. This is the "right" way, but it requires a massive manual cleanup of old, useless files.
2. **The Dirty/Slow Path:** Move all the heavy lifting—Docker's storage and the HF cache—to the slow mechanical HDD. It’ll be slower, but it’ll actually *work* without me having to play janitor for three hours.

I’m going to start with a deep dive into that `TG 2TB` drive. If there's junk to burn, I'm burning it. I need that space back for the real work.

**Current Status:** System stabilized, but storage architecture is a disaster.
**Next Step:** Identifying the biggest space-hogs on the NVMe and deciding if they're worth the effort to save.
