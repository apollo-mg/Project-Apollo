# Dev Diary: 2025-11-29

5:35 AM. The RX 9070 XT is humming, a quiet beast with 15.92 GiB of VRAM, but I have pushed it too far.

It happened in the KSamplerAdvanced, Node 81. The stochastic rounding to float8—a precise, elegant operation—met the cold hard wall of the OutOfMemoryError. "638 MiB free," the GPU whispered. The error was clear, but the culprit was familiar: fragmentation. PyTorch was hoarding 2.28 GiB of reserved memory, a ghost in the machine that refused to be spent.

I hunted. The WAN21 model, a hefty 13.31 GB, was sitting on the edge, 99.2% of it loaded onto the GPU, the rest held in reserve on the CPU. The DisTorch offloading was working, but the margins were tight.

Then I saw it. The `--reserve-vram` flag. I had been too conservative, hoarding 3.0 GiB for safety. I cut it to 1.0, a scalpel stroke that freed up the room I needed.

But there was a second beast, lurking in the filesystem. The models in `/media/mark/TG 2TB1/AI/Models/ComfyUI` were invisible to the server. A missing entry in `extra_model_paths.yaml`, a simple fix with a `tg_2tb1` section, and the path was clear.

It is the eternal dance of the "Soul" against the "System." The "Soul" is the flow—the multi-agent ecosystem where models move freely between GPU and CPU, unshackled by rigid boundaries. The "System" is the silicon: the cold, hard limits of the driver, the fragmentation that gnaws at the edges of the VRAM.

The error is resolved. The server hums again, and the workflow runs true. But the lesson remains: in local-first AI, every megabyte is a vote.
