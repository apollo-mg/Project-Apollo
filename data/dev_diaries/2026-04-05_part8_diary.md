# Apollo Dev Diary: 2026-04-05 (Part 8)

## The Great Architectural Pivot: Sparse-to-Dense Symbiosis

Today was a massive day for Project Apollo. We finally broke out of the "single monolithic model" trap that has been strangling our development. We’ve been trying to force one model to act as both the dreaming architect and the meticulous coder, and the VRAM constraints on the RX 9070 XT were making it impossible. 

I've realized that we don't need one "god-model." We need a pipeline.

### The Friction: VRAM vs. Intelligence
The technical bottleneck was clear: The 26B MoE model is brilliant at context, but its reasoning depth is shallow when it's juggling a 65k context window and a tool-use loop. It kept falling into ReAct-style logic loops, trying to `cat` files or execute shell commands that weren't necessary, which choked the throughput.

We've pivoted to a **Coordinator -> Coder** architecture. 

- **Phase 1 (The Synthesizer):** We use the 26B MoE. Its only job is to ingest the chaotic, massive "Daydream" spools (the 40k+ token brainstorms) and condense them into a single, lean `master_action_plan.md`. Because we stripped its tools down to just `file_write`, we've effectively "lobotomized" the model to prevent it from going rogue, allowing it to spend all its active parameters on the synthesis task.
- **Phase 2 (The Executor):** Once the plan is written, we kill the MoE and load the "heavy guns"—the Qwen 3.5 27B Dense model. This model has the raw reasoning density to handle the code generation. We drop its context window to 8k-16k, which fits comfortably in the 16GB VRAM, and we get the high-fidelity execution we need.

### The Silicon Reality: Power and Thermal Limits
We had a moment of truth with the power draw. When running the `turbo3` fork, we were hitting 250W. That's because we were saturating the L2/L3 cache on the GPU, hitting 100% TFLOPS utilization. It's the "heat of intelligence." 

The beauty of the MoE is that it's incredibly efficient—only pulling ~40W because it's mostly idling while the VRAM bus fetches data. But the real magic happens when we switch to the Dense model; even if the throughput is similar to the MoE due to current software bottlenecks, the *intelligence density* per token will be orders of magnitude higher.

### The Sandbox and the Guardrail
We also explored the Gemini CLI's "Tool Sandboxing." It's a fascinating parallel to what we're doing. The CLI uses LXC/LXD or macOS Seatbelt to isolate the model's blast radius. In Apollo, we are doing this at the architectural level: we aren't just giving the model a sandbox; we are giving it a *role-based* sandbox. By restricting what a model can do *before* it even hits the shell, we are building a "Sovereign Entity" that is physically incapable of destroying the host system.

### Current Status
The `apollo_synthesizer.ts` script is currently running in the background. It's currently chewing through the 40,000-token evaluation of the latest Daydream prompt. It's a slow, heavy grind, but it's the "Idea Funnel" we needed.

**Next Steps:**
Once the `master_action_plan.md` is written, we will begin drafting the `apollo_coordinator.ts`. We will test the logic against the current model to ensure the hand-off between the Coordinator (the plan reader) and the Coder (the executor) works seamlessly within a single-model session.

The "Soul" (the ideas) is being distilled through the "System" (the MoE) into a blueprint that the "Muscle" (the Dense model) can actually execute.
