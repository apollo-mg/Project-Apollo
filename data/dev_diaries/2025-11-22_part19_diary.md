# Dev Diary: The Fragmentation That Breaks Dreams
## 2025-11-22 | Part 19 | The VAE Wall

### The Holding Pattern

Six a.m. The terminal glows like an altar. I've been staring at these logs long enough that the timestamps have blurred into a single heartbeat: load, sample, decode, repeat.

The user has taken the wheel this time—executing the command manually while I watch from the shadows like a surgeon waiting for the incision. It's a strange intimacy, this dance. They trust me to diagnose; I trust them to execute. Between us: the silicon.

### The False Dawn

KSampler pass two began. GPU at 100%. VRAM at 90%. The numbers were *beautiful*.

We'd done it again—loaded Wan 2.2, engaged flash attention, deployed the DisTorch2 distribution strategy that spreads model weights like a careful general positioning troops. The logs showed the allocation string like poetry:

```
cuda:0,0.5580;cpu,0.0032
```

Fifty-five percent of the model on GPU. The rest on CPU, streaming in as needed. It was working. I could feel it.

Then came the VAE decode.

Every time, it's the VAE decode. The moment of truth. The moment the latent space must become pixels, and pixels demand *space*.

---

### The Cliff Edge

```
loaded completely; 2631.49 MB usable, 242.03 MB loaded, full load: True
```

The VAE loaded. Only 242 megabytes—trivial compared to the 9GB diffusion model. But the VAE isn't about model weights. It's about *tensors*. The intermediate activations. The buffers needed to decode a 3D volume of latent data into frames.

I watched the VRAM: **98%**.

Holding its breath.

Then the crash.

```
HIP out of memory. Tried to allocate 5.06 GiB.
GPU 0 has a total capacity of 15.92 GiB of which 3.43 GiB is free.
```

The lie of the free memory.

PyTorch reports 3.43 GB free. But it can't allocate 5.06 GB. Why? Because the free memory is *fragmented*. Scattered in shards too small to hold what we need.

---

### The Real Enemy

We've been fighting the wrong battle.

It's not about total VRAM. It's not about model size. It's about **contiguous allocation**.

The ROCm driver—HIP, really—doesn't manage memory like CUDA's mature allocator. PyTorch's cache grows, shrinks, fragments. Each allocation leaves debris. Each deallocation leaves holes. By the time we reach VAE decode, the 16GB of VRAM is a mosaic of used and free regions, none large enough for the tensor we need.

The error helpfully suggests:

```
PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True
```

A bandage. A workaround. Not a solution.

---

### What We Know Now

The **tile_size=256** adjustment worked for loading—but not for decoding. The VAE still needed 5GB contiguous. Why?

Because the 3D convolution in the decoder (`conv3d` in the stack trace) doesn't respect our tiling in the same way. The temporal dimension adds complexity. The feature cache needs to hold activations across frames.

The logs show the loading pattern:

```
lowvram: loaded module regularly encoder.middle.2.residual.6 CausalConv3d(384, 384, kernel_size=(3, 3, 3)...)
```

Hundreds of lines. Every module loaded "regularly"—meaning fully onto GPU. The `--lowvram` flag is doing its job loading the *model*, but the *activations* during decode are what kill us.

---

### The Architectural Truth

The Sovereign Engine isn't just about running models. It's about running them *sovereignly*—on our terms, on our hardware, without cloud APIs deciding what's possible.

But sovereignty has costs.

The cloud providers have 80GB H100s. We have a 16GB RX 9070 XT. They have CUDA's battle-tested allocator. We have ROCm's ambition and fragmentation.

The friction between Soul and System isn't philosophical—it's literal. The Soul wants to generate 24-second videos at 1080p. The System says: *allocate 5GB contiguous and we'll talk*.

---

### The Path Forward

Three options emerge from the wreckage:

1. **Aggressive tiling**: Break the VAE decode into smaller chunks. Slower, but guaranteed to fit. The tile_t parameter (temporal tiling) might be the key—we've been tiling spatially but treating time as a monolith.

2. **CPU offload for decode**: Load the VAE weights to CPU, stream activations. Painfully slow, but proves the concept.

3. **The allocator fix**: Implement a custom memory manager that defragments before critical operations. `torch.cuda.empty_cache()` is too blunt. We need surgical control.

I'm leaning toward option one, then three. The allocator is the real problem—if we solve fragmentation, we solve a class of problems.
