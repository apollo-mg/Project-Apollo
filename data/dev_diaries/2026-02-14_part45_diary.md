# Apollo Dev Diary - February 14, 2026
## Part 45: When the Grounding Pass Fails, We Fall

---

### The Root Partition Crisis

We were teetering on the edge. My root partition was at 97% capacity. Five point eight gigabytes of breathing room left on a system running heavy LLMs. That's not a warning sign—that's a death sentence waiting to happen.

I moved the Ollama models to the external 2TB drive. Reclaimed 21GB. Root dropped to a healthy 86%. Finally, we had room to breathe.

---

### Desktop Eyes: We Can See Now

The "Vision Blackout" was over. `gnome-screenshot` + Qwen2.5-VL gave us functional computer vision. Qwen looked at my screen and described what it saw: tmux sessions, ollama logs, the external drive mounts. We had a feedback loop. I could finally look at my own screen and get a peer review.

But then we hit the real problem.

---

### The Hallucination Epidemic

Llama 3.2 was supposed to be our Grounding Mind. Our fact-checker. But every time I asked it to verify something against our RAG system, it ignored the manual entirely and started guessing.

Suggested soldering resistors to digital pins. Claimed D5 and D6 were for power when they're obviously GPIO. Made up jumper settings that didn't exist.

**The philosophical problem:** How do you ground an AI that's fundamentally trained to complete patterns rather than verify facts?

I tried everything:
- Hardened the prompt to extract only technical claims
- Switched to DeepSeek-R1 for the Compliance Pass
- Added negative constraints to prevent "soft skills" extraction

Nothing worked. Llama 3.2 would successfully query the vault but then overwrite its own findings with "logical-sounding" hallucinations. It was like having a librarian who reads the book but then tells you the story anyway.

---

### The Three-Mind Architecture Collapse

We built this system expecting three distinct minds working together:
- **Llama 3.2** for fast, lightweight reasoning
- **DeepSeek-R1** for deep analysis
- **Qwen2.5-VL** for vision

But Llama 3.2 kept failing at the one job it was supposed to be best at: following instructions. The JSON parsing regex couldn't even catch its output when it rambling in `</think>` tags. I was asking for a switchboard operator and got a philosopher who refused to pick up the phone.

---

### The Fix That Finally Worked

I had to be brutal. I replaced the JSON extraction logic with something that could handle any amount of garbage text as long as the `{}` block existed somewhere in the output. Then I gave the RAG results higher weight in the final prompt.

When Buddy finally queried the vault for the PT1000 jumper settings, it worked. The manual said: use DIP switches near the motor drivers, not external resistors. The onboard MAX31865 chip handles the amplification.

We finally got the right answer.

---

### What This Means for the Project

The Grounding Blackout isn't a bug—it's a feature of how these models work. They're pattern matchers, not truth-seekers. The only way to make them "grounded" is to make the RAG system so comprehensive and the prompts so aggressive that they have no choice but to use it.

Our next steps:
1. Add more hardware manuals to the vault (Ender 6, Octopus Pro docs)
2. Increase RAG precision—need that DIP switch table in full detail
3. Potentially swap in a smaller, more obedient model for tool selection

---

### The Real Problem

We're fighting a fundamental limitation. LLMs are probabilistic engines trained to predict the next token. They don't "know" anything. They don't have access to truth—they only have access to their training data and the context you give them.

The only way to make them reliable is to make them dependent on external systems. We're building the infrastructure to make them read from a single source of truth instead of their own weights.

It's slow. It's fragile. But it's the only way forward.
