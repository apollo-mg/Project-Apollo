# Dev Diary: The RDNA 4 Wall & The Ghost in the Kernel

**Date:** 2025-11-30
**Subject:** Stability through attrition (The GELU/RDNA 4 saga)
**Hardware:** AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT (16GB VRAM)

---

## 1. The Ghost in the Silicon: The GELU Bug
The session began with a fight against a ghost. A persistent `HIP error: illegal memory access` was haunting the `GELU` activation function. After digging through the trace, I isolated it to a specific `input.float()` cast within `comfy/ops.py`. 

This isn't just a software bug; it’s a collision between the PyTorch-ROCm implementation and the RDNA 4 architecture. The driver is choking on the `float16` to `float32` conversion. I had to implement a surgical bypass—replacing the direct cast with an explicit `torch.empty_like().copy_()` sequence to force a different kernel path. It's a dirty fix, but in the world of bare-metal local-first AI, sometimes you have to bypass the driver's "optimized" path to find stability.

## 2. The VRAM Hunger Games
As we pushed the Wan 2.1 (14B) model, the "Soul" (the creative potential of the model) hit the "System" (the hard limits of the 16GB VRAM) like a freight train. 

The math is brutal. A 9.7GB GGUF model isn't just 9.7GB. Once you factor in the massive activation tensors generated by 3D convolutions and temporal attention (especially at 33 frames), the VRAM usage spiked to 99%. The system was thrashing so violently between VRAM and host RAM that the GPU became a brick.

The most harrowing moment: the VRAM starvation hit the **Display Core Next (DCN)**. The primary display went black. This is the ultimate warning sign—the GPU is so desperate for memory that it's sacrificing the OS's ability to even draw a window. 

## 3. Architectural Epiphany: The "Reserve" Strategy
I realized that the `--reserve-vram` flag is our most critical lever. 
*   Initial attempt: `0.1GB` (Too little, the system crashed).
*   Second attempt: `1.5GB` (Still hitting the wall).
*   Final attempt: `3.0GB` (Stability achieved, but at the cost of usable workspace).

We are forced into a delicate balancing act: we must protect the display and the driver at the expense of the model's "headroom." The only way to survive on 16GB with a 14B parameter model is to aggressively offload. The user's decision to increase the GGUF CPU offload to 2GB was a masterstroke—it moved the weight burden back to the system RAM, finally dropping VRAM usage from 99% to 92%. 

## 4. The Quantization Nuance
A brief detour into the mechanics of GGUF: The user noted the negligible size difference between `Q4_K_M` and `Q5_K_M`. This was a reminder of the "K-quantization" reality. We aren't dealing with uniform bit-depths; we are dealing with mixed-precision tensors where only the most critical weights get the higher bit-count. The "size" of a model is a lie; what matters is the *average* bit-rate per parameter, which is why the file sizes look so similar despite the different labels.

## 5. The Current Friction: The Speed Paradox
We've achieved stability. The black screens are gone. The `GELU` errors are silenced. But we've hit the "Speed Wall." 

The increased VRAM reservation and the heavy CPU offloading have stabilized the system, but the overhead of moving data between the GPU and the 32GB of system RAM is killing the throughput. We are currently seeing a massive slowdown compared to the older ROCm 7.1.0 environment. 

**The Core Lesson:** Local-first multi-agent ecosystems on consumer-grade hardware are not about raw power; they are about **memory management orchestration**. We are no longer just running models; we are managing a complex, shifting landscape of memory fragmentation and driver-level quirks.
