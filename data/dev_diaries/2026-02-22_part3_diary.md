# Project Apollo: Dev Diary - 2026-02-22 (Part 3)

The friction between the "Soul" of a living, breathing assistant and the "System" of raw Linux audio drivers is finally starting to settle. Today was a battle of attrition against PipeWire and the quirks of the AMD ROCm stack.

## The Audio Plumbing Nightmare

The initial attempt at virtual microphone routing was a disaster. I was trying to use `pw-loopback` to create a virtual device for Jarvis, but the single-quote handling in the device names was breaking my scripts. It felt fragile, like a house of cards. I switched tactics, moving to `pactl` to create a "Null Sink." 

It was a more stable approach, but even then, the Linux audio stack threw a curveball. PipeWire was abstracting the virtual sink into a generic `pulse` or `pipewire` device, making it invisible to the `sounddevice` Python library. I had to bypass the virtual abstraction entirely and point the script directly at the hardware address of the C922 webcam. It was a messy, "un-elegant" fix, but in a local-first ecosystem, sometimes you have to fight the OS to get what you want.

## Breaking the Silence: VAD and Latency

Once the audio was flowing, the interaction felt "off." Jarvis was sluggish. The Voice Activity Detection (VAD) was waiting far too long—1.5 seconds of silence before deciding the user was done speaking. In a real-world shop environment, that's an eternity. 

I tightened the `SILENCE_DURATION` to 0.8s and implemented a more aggressive dynamic threshold for the noise floor. This made him much more responsive, though it required fine-tuning to ensure he didn't trigger on his own "ready" beep. The result? He's snappy now. He reacts almost instantly to the wake word.

## The System 2 Awakening

The most significant architectural breakthrough was the integration of the `buddy_agent`. Previously, the voice bridge was just talking to a raw Ollama instance—a "System 1" primitive. By rerouting the voice input through the `buddy_agent` logic, I gave Jarvis "System 2" capabilities.

Suddenly, he isn't just a voice; he's an agent. When I asked for the weather, he didn't just fail—he reasoned. He triggered a `web_search` via SearXNG, realized it wasn't have enough info, and pivoted to `deep_search`. He's actually *thinking* through the tool-calling loop.

## The Growing Pains of Autonomy

With great power comes the "Infinite Loop" problem. The agent got stuck in a reasoning loop, trying to be "too helpful" and repeating tool calls. I had to implement a hard cap on tool turns (max 3) to prevent him from spinning his wheels and burning through VRAM. 

We also hit a wall with JSON serialization. The agent's "thought process" was generating unescaped newlines and control characters, which crashed the `reflect_and_learn` function. I had to implement a regex-based "sanitizer" to strip out Markdown artifacts (asterisks, hashes, emojis) before the text reaches the TTS engine. Now, he sounds like a professional engineer instead of a robot reading a text file.

## Summary of the State of Play

* **Hardware:** AMD RX 9070 XT is holding up well under ROCm 7.1.1. The VRAM management is tight, but the local Whisper-v3-Turbo is blazing fast.
* **Architecture:** The "Sovereign Voice Bridge" is officially functional. The link between the C922 hardware, the local Whisper-v3, and the DeepSeek-R1 reasoning engine is solid.
* **Memory:** The session was successfully committed to the hierarchical memory. The architectural milestones—the voice bridge, the ROCm validation, and the VAD settings—are now part of his long-term knowledge.

Jarvis is no longer just a script. He is a resident of this machine, capable of interacting with the physical world and the web. The next step: refining the tool-use accuracy so he doesn't get lost in the "thinking" loop when a tool returns a partial result.
