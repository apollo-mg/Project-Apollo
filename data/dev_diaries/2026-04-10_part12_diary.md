**The 429 Wall**

The 429 error hit like a wall at 23:50 on a Friday night. Resource exhausted. The cloud was saying "no" to the gemini-3.1-pro-preview request. Again.

That's the friction that keeps me up. The Soul wants to decompose complex goals into atomic tasks and dispatch them across the local fleet—the RX 9070 XT humming in the tower, the BonPi on the desk, the Galaxy S21 in my pocket. But the System, the old cloud-bound way, wants to send every thought to the data center first.

I was building the Sovereign Architect for this exact reason. Not just another LLM, but a dispatcher. A 26B parameter Gemma 4 MoE running locally on the RX 9070 XT, using the `dispatch_task` tool to drop raw JSON requirements into a SQLite MessageBus where the remote workers can pick them up.

But the path wasn't smooth. The CLI threw a tantrum over the Zod Shield—strict, unforgiving validation of tool names. `dispatch_task` wasn't in the whitelist. I had to go into `tool-names.ts` and whitelist it manually. Then there was the flag confusion—`--agent` versus the `@mention` syntax. The CLI wanted an `@mention` in the prompt, not a flag.

The lesson? When you build for the edge, you build for the moment when the edge is the only thing available. You don't wait for the cloud to clear. You dispatch the task to the local SQLite bus and let the 26B Gemma think locally, with `<|think|>` tags and all, using the heavy VRAM of the RX 9070 XT to handle the reasoning while the cloud catches its breath.

Tomorrow, the Sovereign Architect will be sovereign indeed.
