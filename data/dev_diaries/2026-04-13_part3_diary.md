**Project Apollo: The TQ3_4S Discovery**

Today was about wrestling with the bleeding edge of quantization. The Hugging Face ecosystem is flooding us with new formats—PolarQuant, TurboQuant, abliterated. The question always is: can my RDNA 4 silicon chew through a 3.5-bit tensor without choking?

**The Friction**

I pulled down the `Qwopus3.5-27B-v3-TQ3_4S.gguf`. Theoretically, it's a ~12.5GB beast that fits on my 16GB RX 9070 XT with room to spare for a 64k context window. But when I fired up the server—compiled from the `turbo-tan/llama.cpp-tq3` branch with `GGML_VULKAN=ON`—it spat out a curiosity. The model claimed to be loaded, but only 5GB of VRAM was lit up. Where was the rest of the 27B parameters?

**The Epiphany**

That was the "Silent Fallback" in action. The Vulkan backend, beautiful as it is, didn't have the shader kernels for `TQ3_4S` just yet. So it offloaded what it could understand—the KV cache, the embeddings, the normalization layers—but it dumped the massive 3.5-bit weight matrices into system RAM. My CPU is going to be crunching those MatMul layers while my GPU handles the attention. It works, but it's not the blistering 50+ tokens per second I was hoping for.

**The Lesson**

It's the friction between "Soul" and "System" again. The Soul wants the massive, reasoning-capable model running sovereign on local silicon. The System demands specific shader implementations for specific quantization types. We are bridging that gap by building translation layers and hunting for the right forks.

For now, the startup script is written. The server is live on port 8082. It is slower, yes, but the path to a truly local, multi-agent future is paved with these tiny, invisible zero-width spaces and 3.5-bit tensors.
