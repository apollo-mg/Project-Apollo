December 3, 2025. Late.

The hum of the RX 9070 XT is the only thing keeping me company in this lab right now. There’s a specific kind of madness that comes with working on the bleeding edge of RDNA 4. You aren't just writing code; you’re negotiating with silicon that hasn’t even fully realized what it’s capable of yet.

Tonight was a war of attrition between different flavors of attention mechanisms. We’ve hit a wall with the Triton backend. In theory, the Triton-compiled Flash Attention should be the king—the fastest path to high-speed generation. But on RDNA 4, the math is... wrong. It’s producing these hideous visual artifacts, glitchy blocks that tear through the video frames. It’s the Wave32 code being generated by the Triton compiler; it’s hitting the ISA in ways the developers didn't anticipate, creating subtle mathematical corruptions.

We had to categorize the madness into three distinct paths:

1. The Triton Path: Blazing fast, but hallucinates visual noise.
2. The PyTorch SDPA Flash Path: Also fast, but shares those same underlying kernel bugs. It's a trap.
3. The Memory Efficient (MemEff) Path: The "Golden Path." It’s slower, yeah, but the math is stable. It doesn't break the reality of the image.

I tried to push the limit by forcing the `--use-flash-attention` flag, hoping to bypass our custom patches and go pure. The system laughed at me. A massive `ValueError: 'IS_CAUSAL' is not in list` traceback. It’s classic dependency hell—the version of `flash-attn` we compiled is fighting with the Triton version in the ROCm environment. The autotuner is looking for arguments that don't exist in the current runtime. It’s a reminder that even with a beast of a GPU, you are still a slave to the Python ecosystem.

We’ve retreated to the "Safe Mode" for now. Using the PyTorch MemEff path. It’s the compromise between the Soul and the System. The Soul wants the speed of the Flash kernels to realize complex, rapid-fire video generation, but the System demands the stability of the slower kernels to prevent the silicon from spitting out garbage.

But the real battle starts tomorrow. 

The 5B model is behaving. It’s stable, running through our patched GGUF and custom attention layers like a well-oiled machine. It feels... almost ready. But then we scale up to the 14B GGUF, and the hardware starts screaming. 

The 14B is hitting "Illegal Memory Access" page faults. We’re seeing crashes during the sampling stage. My gut tells me it’s not just a VRAM limit issue—it’s a deeper interaction between the RDNA 4 driver and the way the GGUF dequantization is hitting the memory. When we try to pull these massive tensors into the VRAM, the bitwise operations are likely triggering a driver-level hiccup. 

The strategy for the next session is clear: we go lower on the quantization. If Q5 is causing a memory alignment meltdown, we might have to settle for Q4 or even Q3. We’re essentially trying to shrink the "brain" of the model just to make it fit into the "skull" of the hardware without causing a seizure.

It’s frustrating, but that’s the work. We’re building an ecosystem that doesn't just run on the cloud, but lives locally, in the palm of your hand, on your own desk. We just have to convince the hardware to play along.
