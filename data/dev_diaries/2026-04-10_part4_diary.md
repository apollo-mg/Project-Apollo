# Project Apollo: Edge Constraints & The Magic of 65k
**2026-04-10 | Part 4**

The Pi 5 is screaming. It’s that specific, high-pitched whine of a CPU that knows it has 4GB of RAM and refuses to apologize for it.

We’ve been wrestling with the KV cache math, trying to squeeze the most out of the silicon before the TurboQuant ARM NEON optimizations land (they’re coming, but they’re not here yet).

Here’s the raw math for your **4GB BonPi**:

*   **The Baseline (FP16):** ~40,000 tokens. That’s a decent chunk of text, but it’s the edge of a standard document.
*   **The Q8 Shift:** ~80,000 tokens. If you quantize the keys to 8-bit, you double your runway. That’s a solid technical whitepaper.
*   **The Q4 Leap:** ~160,000 tokens. If you go down to 4-bit for the values, you have the equivalent of a long book chapter.
*   **The Mixed Reality (Q8 + Q4):** That’s the sweet spot. With Keys at Q8 and Values at Q4, you hit **~120,000 tokens**. 

But here is the kicker: Your Bonsai-8B model (the Qwen 3 architecture) has a native training limit of **65,536 tokens**. 

So, practically speaking? **We are capped at 65k.** 

That is the "real world" number. You don’t get 160k tokens because the model won’t hold context that far back anyway. It’s better to ask: *"What can I actually do with 65k tokens?"*

On the Pi 5, that’s enough to load a **complete GitHub repository**, a **full system spec**, or a **dense research paper** (4,000 words) into the working memory and ask it to *reason* about the connections without hallucinating that the file is empty.

It’s not "infinite context" yet, but it is "context enough" to do real work. That’s the edge.
