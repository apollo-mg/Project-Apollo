# The Silicon Boundary: Wrestling a 14B Model onto 16GB of Memory

**Date:** November 21, 2025  
**Location:** The lab, somewhere between frustration and revelation  
**System State:** Compromised, but not defeated

---

## The Promise and the Betrayal

There's something almost romantic about the ambition of Project Apollo—to build sovereign intelligence that lives in your basement, not someone else's data center. But romance doesn't care about page tables. And the AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT, for all its 16GB of VRAM and gfx1201 architecture, cares very much.

Today's enemy: a 14-billion parameter model (Wan2.1-T2V-14B) that refuses to behave on consumer silicon.

The error message was brutal in its simplicity:

> `HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`

Not out of memory. Not a timeout. An *illegal memory access*. The GPU was trying to read from addresses it wasn't allowed to touch, and the memory controller shut it down like a security guard ejecting a drunk patron.

---

## The Autopsy

The stack trace led me through layers of abstraction like an onion peeled by a surgeon with a grudge:

```
KSampler → sample_dpmpp_2m → model() → forward_orig() → block() → ffn() → F.gelu() → 💀
```

The murder weapon: a GELO activation function in the feed-forward network.

The suspect: something in how the quantized weights (Q4_K_M) were being unpacked or addressed during the forward pass.

What struck me most wasn't the crash itself, but the timing. The first generation attempt with Flux worked perfectly. The second, loading Wan2.1, died on the first step. Same workflow structure. Same batch size of 17. Same resolution (832×480). The difference? Architecture. Scale. Hubris.

---

## The Memory Math That Lies to You

Let's do the accounting that makes me want to throw my laptop through a window:

- **Model weights (Q4_K_M):** ~8-9GB (compressed, but needs expansion during compute)
- **Activation cache (batch=17, res=832×480):** ?GB (unpredictable, explosive)
- **Intermediate tensors:** ?GB (the silent killers)
- **VAE buffer:** ~3.5GB loaded
- **CLIP encoder:** ~6.4GB loaded

Total *theoretical* requirement: 20+GB  
Total *available*: 16GB  
Total *survival chance*: Approximately zero

The `--lowvram` flag felt like a promise. It was a suggestion. A polite request to the kernel manager to *try* not to segfault.

---

## The ROCm Factor

Here's where it gets spicy. I'm not on CUDA. I'm on ROCm 7.1, running HIP (Heterogeneous-Compute Interface for Parallel), which is AMD's attempt to speak NVIDIA's language while being fundamentally different at the hardware level.

The error message helpfully suggested:

> `For debugging consider passing AMD_SERIALIZE_KERNEL=3`  
> `Compile with TORCH_USE_HIP_DSA to enable device-side assertions.`

Translation: "Your problem is at a level we don't fully support yet. Good luck."

The async nature of HIP kernel errors means the stack trace might be lying to me. The crash could have happened three steps ago, and I'm just seeing the corpse now.

---

## The Epiphhany (Earned, Not Given)

Here's what I realized staring at that traceback at 2 AM:

**The problem isn't the model. It's the workflow.**

Batch size 17 for video generation? That's not a parameter choice. That's a declaration of war on my memory controller.

The Wan2.1 model wasn't designed with the understanding that it would be running on a $600 graphics card in someone's home lab. It assumed data centers. Infinity Fabric. ECC memory. Cooling solutions that require their own HVAC system.

But *I* am not a data center. I am a basement. And there's dignity in that limitation.

---

## The Path Forward (Scarred, But Clear)

1. **Batch size reduction:** 17 → 1, maybe 4 if I'm feeling suicidal. Video generation is already exponential hell; I don't need to compound it.

2. **Resolution sanity:** 832×480 is non-standard for a reason. Try 512×512. Survive first. Optimize later.

3. **Quantization investigation:** Q4_K_M might be too aggressive, causing unpacking overhead that explodes memory. Try Q6_K or even FP16 for the activation-heavy layers.

4. **The nuclear option:** Torch compile with `TORCH_USE_HIP_DSA` enabled. See what the device-side assertions actually complain about. Maybe the illegal access isn't illegal—it's a bug in the ROCm stack.

5. **Acceptance:** Some models won't run on this hardware, not cleanly, not efficiently. The alternative is building the infrastructure to match the ambition—and that's a different project entirely.

---

## The Philosophical Cost

There's a tension here that keeps me up at night:

**Sovereignty requires limitation.**

If I want true local-first AI, I can't assume infinite compute. I can't assume the latest H100 cluster. I have to build systems that respect the constraints of the edge, the home lab, the compromised hardware of real users.

The illegal memory access wasn't just a bug. It was a boundary condition. A reminder that intelligence doesn't scale linearly with parameters—it scales with the wisdom to know when *not* to load all 14 billion of them at once.
