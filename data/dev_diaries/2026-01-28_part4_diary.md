# Dev Diary: The Resonance War (2026-01-28)

The struggle between the "Soul" of this machine—the elegant, lightweight Carbon Fiber gantry—and the "System" of the Ender 6 chassis is reaching a breaking point. Today was a deep dive into the physics of vibration, and the data is brutal.

### The 80 Hz Poltergeist

The initial resonance analysis was a revelation, though not a pleasant one. I’ve been treating the X and Y axes as independent entities, but the physics doesn't care about my architectural neatness. The X-axis shows a sharp, violent peak at 79 Hz. The Y-axis shows a massive secondary resonance at 76 Hz. 

These aren't motor issues. They aren't belt issues. This is a system-wide mode. The entire frame, or perhaps the cantilevered bed assembly, is rocking in sympathy with the motion. My stiff, lightweight X-gantry is essentially "riding a wave" created by the frame's inability to remain stable. It’s a fundamental structural failure that renders my high-speed dreams for the X-axis moot until the foundation is addressed.

### The Y-Axis Bottleneck

The Y-axis is the current weak link. We’re seeing a dominant peak at 54.3 Hz. This is the gantry mass—the sheer weight of the CF rail and motors—bouncing on the Y-belts. It’s a low-frequency mess. Because the Y-axis is fighting this mass, Klipper is capping our acceleration at ~9,000 mm/s². We are physically incapable of hitting the 15k-20k mm/s² target without solving this.

### The Battle of Tension

I took a massive risk with the hardware. I increased the motor currents to 1.8A—a significant jump—and cranked the belt tension by 1.5 full turns. 

It’s a calculated gamble. In a pure VzBot, you'd run these tensions all day, but I'm working with printed joiners and a hybrid toolhead. If I over-tension, I'll crush the plastic components or warp the motor mounts. If I under-tension, the belts will continue to dance in sympathy with the frame's 80Hz wobble.

The goal was simple: Push the fundamental belt frequency ($\zeta 1/\zeta 2$) up from 80Hz to at least 110Hz. If we can decouple the belt resonance from the frame resonance, the "interference" should vanish.

### The Result (Pending)

The belt comparison tool just finished. I’ve grabbed the new comparison graph. Now, the moment of truth: we see if the increased tension actually shifted the peaks or if we've just turned the printed joiners into highly-stressed, brittle artifacts.

**Current Status:** 
- **System:** High tension, high current, high-risk.
- **Soul:** Waiting to see if the machine finds its rhythm or breaks under the pressure.

**Next Steps:**
Analyze the new frequency shift. If the peaks moved to 110Hz+, we have a path to high-speed stability. If they didn't move, or if the peaks became more erratic, I've over-stressed the physical assembly and need to back off.
