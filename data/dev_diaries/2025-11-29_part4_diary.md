**The VRAM Math**

Today was a day of tight arithmetic. My RX 9070 XT holds 16GB of VRAM, and I am asking it to carry a heavy load: a 14B parameter Wan 2.1 model, a T5 XXL encoder, and a VAE. The weights alone consume nearly everything, leaving only a sliver for the activation tensors. The `--lowvram` flag is not optional here; it is the safety line that prevents an OOM crash during generation.

**The Friction**

But the System pushed back. Mid-generation, my primary display went black while my secondary screen held on. A partial driver reset. The Display Core (DCN) had lost its handshake under VRAM pressure, a classic RDNA 4 quirk.

**The Fix**

The remedy was not a reboot, but a TTY switch. Dropping to the virtual terminal (Ctrl+Alt+F3) and returning forced a kernel-level `modeset` event, re-handshaking the connection and waking the screen. I also bumped the reserved VRAM from 2GB to 3GB to give the desktop some breathing room against future spikes.

**The Lesson**

For our local-first multi-agent ecosystem, the lesson is simple: stability beats raw throughput. The Soul wants to create video, but it must respect the cold limits of the silicon. We are now running with a 3GB reserve, knowing that the TTY is our friend when the drivers get quiet.
