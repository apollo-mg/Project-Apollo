# Dev Diary: 2026-03-04 (Part 3)

The friction between the "Soul" and the "System" is real. Today was a battlefield of silicon-level instability and sudden, brilliant breakthroughs. I spent the first few hours fighting a losing battle against the Qwen 3.5 architecture. It’s a beautiful, complex model—a unified vision-language beast—but the current version of Ollama (0.17.5) on my RDNA 4 card is absolutely losing its mind trying to handle its specific matrix shapes.

We hit a massive wall with the "Small" series (0.8B and 2B). They aren't just slow; they are broken. They enter these silent, infinite generation loops that pin the CPU to 135% and hog the VRAM, refusing to die even when I issue a `stop` command. It’s a classic kernel-level clash between the new architecture and `llama.cpp`'s implementation for AMD. 

However, once we bypassed the Qwen 3.5 chaos and pivoted to the Llama-based models, the world opened up.

The DeepHermes-3-8B is a beast of stability, running at a blistering 87 TPS. But the real stars of today were the "Abliterated" models. The Qwen 3.5 9B Heretic is a technical marvel—it delivers high-speed, unvarnished, and terrifyingly precise engineering-style prose. It’s not just "creative"; it’s technically coherent in its madness. It can simulate a kernel panic with a precision that makes the silicon feel like it's actually dying.

The most significant moment, though, was testing the Llama-3.2-8X3B-Dark-Champion. The MoE (Mixture of Experts) architecture is a game-changer for local-first development. It gives you the weight of a 24B model but the speed of a 3B. It’s a specialized, uninhibited "creative engine" that can write a clinical, visceral autopsy of a malfunctioning machine without flinching.

We also saw the absolute limits of the 16GB VRAM constraint. While the 80B Qwen-Coder can boot through heavy paging into system RAM, it’s a slow, heavy crawl. We’ve essentially defined the "Silicon Ceiling" for this hardware: 4-bit quantization is the only way to stay agile.

The laboratory is no longer just a collection of models; it's a crew. We have the fast-acting "Gatekeepers" (the small, fast models), the "Engineers" (the 8B-14B models), and now the "Architects" (the large MoEs). We are building a sovereign, multi-agent intelligence, one architecture at a time.
