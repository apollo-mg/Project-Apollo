# Dev Diary: 2025-11-23 — The VRAM That Divides Us

## The Spoofing Gambit Fails

I woke up today determined to crack FP8 on RDNA 4 through architectural deception. The plan: spoof a MI300 identity (`HSA_OVERRIDE_GFX_VERSION=9.4.2`) and force ROCm to treat my consumer silicon like the datacenter beast it dreams of being.

It lasted 0.4 seconds before the segfault.

```
./run_wan_safe.sh: line 59: 70340 Segmentation fault (core dumped)
```

The ISA difference between CDNA 3 and RDNA 4 isn't cosmetic. The kernel tables don't lie. I had to swallow my pride and accept that AMD's consumer driver stack hasn't caught up to the hardware they shipped me.

## The GGUF Salvation

If FP8 is the dream, GGUF Q4_K_M is the compromise we build our empire upon.

**The numbers don't lie:**
- **Size:** 9GB (actually fits in VRAM)
- **Speed:** 30s/it → **~5.5 seconds per frame**
- **Stability:** Absolute, with `TunableOp=0`

I spent the morning in a ritual dance of environment variables:
- `PYTORCH_TUNABLEOP_ENABLED=0` (required)
- `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=1` (speed)
- `--normalvram` vs `--lowvram` (the eternal question)

## The 16GB Wall

Here's the truth no one tells you about video generation: **16GB of VRAM is the new 8GB of 2020.**

The math is brutal:
- FP8 Model: ~14GB
- CLIP (T5 XXL FP8): ~4.7GB
- VAE + Activations: ????GB
- **Total:** 19+ GB

My 16GB card became a Tetris board where I'm the player and the blocks are 4GB each.

The `lowvram: loaded module regularly` messages scrolled like a screensaver for an hour. Each line a reminder: *you are not the master of this silicon, you are its negotiator.*

## The FFmpeg Frenzy

A thousand lines of startup noise from `torio` probing for FFmpeg 6, failing, probing for FFmpeg 5, failing, finally accepting FFmpeg 4 with the grace of a defeated soldier.

Ubuntu 22.04 ships with 4.4.2. The ecosystem demands 6.0. I chose peace over purity.

## The Green Screen

At 04:41, I got a solid green image. Not art. Not abstraction. Green.

The VAE had overflowed. Or the GGUF split was wrong (HighNoise vs LowNoise). Or the scheduler was cursed. The exact culprit doesn't matter—only that I circled back to settings that worked before.

**Lesson:** The system is stable enough. The chaos is in the configuration space, not the silicon.

## The 4090 Comparison

This is the moment I wanted the most.

| Metric | RTX 4090 (24GB) | RX 9070 XT (16GB) |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Per-Frame Time | ~6.0s | **~5.5s** |

My consumer card, running compressed GGUF weights on bleeding-edge ROCm, is **matching** the 24GB flagship's FP8 performance.

The irony? I'm doing it through quantization and careful memory management, while the 4090 has 8GB more VRAM and CUDA to spare.

## The VAE Segfault at 65 Frames

I pushed to 65 frames and the decoder died.

```
lowvram: loaded module regularly decoder.head.0 RMS_norm()
Segmentation fault (core dumped)
```

The UNet had hogged all VRAM (`loaded completely`). The VAE tried to allocate its decode buffer for 65 frames and found nothing but air.

**The fix:** `--reserve-vram 2.0`

It forces ComfyUI to evict the UNet before the VAE arrives. A simple protocol for two giants sharing a small room.

**Result:** 354 seconds for 65 frames. **5.44s per frame.** Perfect linear scaling.

## The Architecture That Emerged

```
run_wan_safe.sh (Final Configuration):
├── PYTORCH_TUNABLEOP_ENABLED=0
├── HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=1
├── --normalvram
├── --reserve-vram 2.0
└── --use-flash-attention
```

This is the configuration that works. This is the compromise between the 14B-parameter dream and the 16GB reality.

## What I Learned

1. **Spoofing doesn't bypass physics.** ISA differences don't vanish with environment variables.

2. **GGUF isn't a fallback—it's a feature.** The quantization overhead is negligible compared to the VRAM freedom it buys.

3. **The reserve-vram parameter is the most important knob.** Too low (1.0) and you crash on VAE decode. Too high (3.0) and you're swapping constantly. **2.0 is the sweet spot.**

4. **16GB is enough, but you have to earn it.** Every megabyte is negotiated through flags, quantization, and careful node selection.

5. **The RX 9070 XT punches above its weight class.** But only if you respect its limits instead of fighting them.

## The Soul vs. System

I want to load the FP16 model and generate 120-frame videos in 30 seconds. The System says: *dream on.*

So I load the GGUF. I tune the reserves. I accept 5.5 seconds per frame. And when the cyberpunk robot vendor flips noodles in the rain, I tell myself: **this is what sovereignty looks like.**

It's not the speed I wanted. It's the speed I earned.
