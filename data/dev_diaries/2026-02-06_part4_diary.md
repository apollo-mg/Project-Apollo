# Dev Diary: 2026-02-06 (Part 4)

The battle against configuration entropy is never easy, especially when the silicon is actively fighting you. Today was a grueling cycle of wrestling with the Klipper parser, the physical geometry of a re-engineered gantry, and a hardware-level communication failure that felt more like a ghost in the machine.

### The Ghost in the Config
The session started with a frustratingly vague error: `Option 'sensor_type' in section 'probe_eddy_current btt_eddy' must be specified`. 

I suspected the BTT Eddy configuration was lingering in the shadows. I spent an hour hunting through `printer.cfg` and its many includes (`eddy_macros.cfg`, `autotune.cfg`), trying to find where the parser was still seeing the commented-out block. I even tried a "nuclear option"—deleting the entire block and its `SAVE_CONFIG` counterpart.

But the parser is a stubborn beast. Even after the deletion, Klipper still screamed. It turned out the `SAVE_CONFIG` block was still holding onto a ghost of the Eddy, and because I had nuked the main definition, the parser hit a logic dead-end. 

The purge worked, but it triggered a cascade of errors. By deleting the `SAVE_CONFIG` section, I accidentally nuked the active PID settings for the extruder. I had to manually restore the `[extruder]` control parameters from the backup to get the thermal loop back online. It was a reminder: in a local-first ecosystem, your "backup" isn't just a safety net; it's your source of truth when the live environment becomes corrupted.

### Geometry & Kinematics: The Great Swap
Once the thermal loop was stable, the printer finally booted. But the "Soul" of the machine—the physical motion—was broken. 

The X-axis was behaving, but the Y-axis was moving in reverse. In a CoreXY system, this is a classic symptom of a motor-swap or a logic inversion. After some deduction, I realized the motors were physically/logically swapped. I executed a surgical strike on the config, swapping the pin definitions for `[stepper_x]` and `[stepper_y]`.

The result? The axes swapped. X became Y, and Y became X. A standard CoreXY phenomenon. I corrected it by further inverting the `dir_pin` on one motor, and finally, the geometry aligned. The nozzle now hits the absolute `0,0` corner of the bed. The machine is finally "aware" of its own body.

### The Sensor Sabotage
The final boss of the day was the BD Sensor. After fixing the kinematics, the sensor—which was working perfectly before the config reshuffle—refused to communicate. 

The error `Unable to communicate with bdsensor,1024` is a nasty one. It suggests the I2C bus is dead. I suspected a pinout mismatch between the Octopus Pro 1.0 and the 1.1 version. The 1.1 version apparently remapped the EXP1 header, meaning my attempts to use the standard `PE14/15` pins were likely hitting a "Reset" pin on the new board, potentially causing the MCU to reboot every time the sensor tried to talk.

I tried every permutation: swapping SCL/SDA, shifting to the `PE9/PE10` pins (the new mapping), and adding `^` for pullups. Nothing. The communication remains broken.

The realization hit: if the sensor was physically damaged (as noted in the logs), the 5V-to-SCL short-circuit might have killed the bus. Or, more likely, the physical wiring is simply on a different header entirely. The next step is a physical migration of the sensor to the BLTouch header (PB6/PB7) to bypass the messy EXP1 mapping.

### Core Lessons
- **The System is Fragile:** Even a small change in a `SAVE_CONFIG` block can break the entire dependency chain of a complex multi-agent/multi-sensor setup.
- **Hardware-Software Friction:** You cannot debug a software-defined pin without verifying the physical hardware revision (v1.0 vs v1.1). The "Soul" (the sensor) is currently trapped in a "System" (the MCU) that speaks a different language.
- **The Local-First Advantage:** Working locally on my AMD-powered rig allowed me to rapidly iterate through these configuration-heavy cycles, but it also means I am 100% responsible for the integrity of the local state.

**Current Status:** Kinematics are solved. X/Y limits are locked. The sensor is the current bottleneck.
**Next Step:** Move sensor to BLTouch header and re-map pins.
