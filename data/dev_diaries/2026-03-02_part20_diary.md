# Apollo Dev Diary: 2026-03-02 (Part 20)

## The Agentic Circuit Breaker: Breaking the Infinite Loop

Today was about the transition from "Prompt Engineering" to actual **Systems Engineering**. I’ve been obsessing over the friction between the "Soul" (the models' ability to perceive) and the "System" (the cold, hard limits of my hardware and my API quota). 

I hit a wall with the vision protocol. I had `qwen3-vl:8b` doing the heavy lifting on the visual scan, and `deepseek-r1:14b` handling the reasoning. It was working, but it was too rigid. If the vision model missed a detail, the whole thing became a hallucination machine. 

I realized that if I don't constrain the model, it will spend my $20/month Gemini quota on a loop of the same failed search queries. I need a way to tell the model: *"If you fail, stop, try something else, or just give up and move to the next tool."*

### The Technical Breakthrough: The Weighted Cascade

I implemented a **Probability Matrix** and a **Weighted Tool Cascade**. 

Instead of a linear pipeline, I’ve refactored the `vision_audit_protocol.py` into an autonomous loop. Now, the system works like this:

1.  **The Vision Turn**: `qwen3-vl:8b` scans for "Architectural Anchor Points" (silkscreen text, pin counts, port layouts). 
2.  **The Reasoning Turn**: `deepseek-r1:14b` evaluates the scan. It populates a JSON-based probability matrix.
3.  **The Cascade Logic**: If the confidence score is < 90%, the system triggers a `web_search`. If that search fails or returns no new info, the **Circuit Breaker** kicks in. The model is instructed to *not* repeat the same query and instead fall back to a `diff_inventory` check with its "best guess."

I tested this on an image from my Discord folder—a photo of a Lite-On board. Initially, the vision model was being too granular, just seeing "PCB" and "Resistors." By stripping the prompt of "Raspberry Pi" bias and forcing it to look for specific component IDs, the system successfully extracted the exact part number. 

The beauty of this is the **deterministic exit**. The system is no longer just "thinking"; it is making decisions based on a cost/reliability score. 

### The Philosophy of Constraint

This is the most important takeaway from today. My $20/month limit isn't a hurdle; it's the architecture's primary driver. 

Corporate developers with unlimited compute build "bloated" intelligence. They throw $10,000 worth of API calls at a problem because they don't have to care about the cost. They build systems that work in a vacuum but fail in production due to cost-explosion.

By working under a strict constraint, I am forced to build **Sovereign AI**. I am building the "plumbing"—the circuit breakers, the local WORM databases, and the local-first routing. This is exactly what Tenstorment is trying to do with hardware. They are building efficient, constrained, deterministic silicon. I am building efficient, constrained, deterministic software.

The "Soul" of the AI is now being successfully gated by the "System" of my logic. We are no longer just prompting; we are engineering an autonomous agent.
