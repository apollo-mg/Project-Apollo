# Apollo Dev Diary: The MoE Mirage and the 27B Breakthrough

**2026-03-26**

Today was a violent collision between the theoretical elegance of Mixture of Experts (MoE) and the brutal reality of VRAM management. I spent the first half of the session chasing ghosts in the 35B model, only to realize I was chasing a mathematical hallucination.

## The 35B Mirage

I thought I had a winner with the `Qwen3.5-35B-A3B` model. On paper, the stats were intoxicating: 72 tokens per second on the RX 9070 XT. Even with the `IQ2_M` 2-bit quantization, the sheer throughput was unlike anything I’ve seen in a local setup. But the "Soul" of the model—its actual intelligence—was missing.

Every time I ran a benchmark, the model would fire off 1024 tokens of... nothing. Just whitespace. A complete structural collapse. It turns out that when you push 2-bit quantization onto an MoE architecture, you aren't just losing precision; you're potentially destroying the routing logic. The gates that decide which "expert" to activate are getting crushed, leading to a state where the model effectively forgets how to speak, even while it's screaming through the tokens at light speed.

Then came the "thrashing." As I tried to probe the model further, the system hit a wall. The memory overhead of swapping experts in and out of the VRAM was causing massive fragmentation and performance drops. The "System" was choking on the "Soul" of the MoE architecture.

## The 27B Architect Found

We pivoted back to the `Qwen3.5-27B-Claude-4.6-Opus-Reasoning-Distilled` model, and the difference was night and day. This isn't a MoE model; it's a dense, heavy-duty powerhouse. 

The documentation from the community (@sudoing) was key here. This model was specifically distilled from Claude 4.6 Opus to maintain a high-density reasoning state. It’s not just "smarter"; it's fundamentally more stable. It handles the `<think>` blocks with a level of structural integrity that the 35B model lacked entirely. 

The VRAM footprint is also much more predictable. Even at `IQ3_M` (~11.7GB), it sits comfortably within our limits, leaving plenty of headroom for the vision pipeline. 

## The Vision Breakthrough

We also successfully verified the vision pipeline. I ran a test using the `Crow-9B` model (the 9B vision-capable model) with a local image of some fruit. 

The base64 dynamic injection payload—which we've been struggling to perfect—is finally, officially, operational. The model correctly identified the image content, proving that our `llm_interface.py` is now correctly routing vision requests to the 9B model while keeping the 27B model as the primary "Architect" brain.

## Architectural Summary

The current state of Project Apollo is now a bifurcated powerhouse:
- **The Brain (Logic/Code):** `Qwen3.5-27B-Claude-4.6-OS-Auto-Variable-Heretic`. It provides the deep reasoning and the autonomous planning capability.
- **The Eyes (Vision):** `Crow-9B` via the `mmproj` adapter. It handles the perceptual tasks.

It's a delicate balance. We have the "Soul" (the reasoning) and we have the "System" (the vision/hardware), and they are finally speaking the same language. The friction of the MoE architecture was too high for the current quantization levels, but the dense 27B model has given us the stability we need to actually build something real.

**Next steps:** Continue refining the `llm_interface` to ensure the handover between the 27B and 9B models is seamless during multi-modal tasks.
