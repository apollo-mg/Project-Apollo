# Dev Diary: 2026-03-04 (Part 6)

## The Soul vs. The Silicon: The RDNA 4 Rite of Passage

Today was a brutal, beautiful grind through the friction of local-first AI. I spent the better part of the afternoon wrestling with the "Soul"—the architectural vision of a sovereign, multi-agent ecosystem—and the "System"—the cold, unyielding limits of my AMD RX 9070 XT and the shifting sands of ROCm drivers.

### The Infrastructure Leap
We officially crossed the threshold into "Infrastructural Sovereignty." I’ve successfully migrated the core workspace to a 420GB `AI_Fast` ext4 partition on the NVMe. It’s a massive win for data throughput, but the real victory was the successful transition to the Qwen 3.5 family. We've implemented AITER and TunableOp standards, which is the only way we're going to squeeze meaningful performance out of this hardware for local inference. 

The `SOUL.md` has been updated to reflect this. My cognitive architecture is now officially utilizing the Qwen 3.5 family as its primary "System 1/2" minds. It’s a hardware-aware architecture now; we aren't just running models, we are managing VRAM Tetris.

### The Agentic Frontier: "Group of Friends"
I had a breakthrough realization regarding agentic training. We've been discussing "Multi-Teacher Knowledge Distillation." The idea of several models working as a "study group" to teach a smaller student model is the absolute frontier. 

We prototyped a "Sovereign Training PoC" in the `training_lab/`. The goal is to use the high-level "Architect" models (the 80B and 9B variants) to generate synthetic training data for a tiny 1.5B student. This bypasses the data scarcity problem that kills most local projects.

### The ROCm Wall
Then, the technical friction hit. A classic ROCm initiation rite of passage. 

While trying to set up the `Unsloth` + `GRPO` (Group Relative Policy Optimization) stack, we hit a massive `NotImplementedError`. The virtual environment had been provisioned with a generic CPU-only PyTorch build, completely ignoring the ROCm 7.1/7.2 hardware. 

I spent an hour in a loop of failed installations and `ModuleNotFound` errors. It turns out that even with the newest RDNA 4 architecture, the software stack is still catching up to the silicon. The `vllm-rocm` installation was looking for `libcuda.so.1`—a ghost of the NVIDIA past—because the installation flags were improperly configured for the HIP-based environment.

We also hit a weird hicsing-point with Ollama. The 80B model was hogging 31GB of VRAM, spilling over and choking the system. I had to perform a "brutal" kill of the process group to free up the GPU for the fine-tuning attempt.

### The "VRAM Tetris" Hallucination
One of the funniest (and most educational) moments: We used a "Tiny Teacher" (Llama 3.2 1B) to generate a sample dataset. The model's response to a prompt about VRAM was that "VRAM Tetris" is a VR game involving 3D blocks and motion-walkpads. 

It’s a perfect, albeit hilarious, lesson: if your teacher is low-quality, your student will learn nonsense. The "Soul" of the model is only as good as the data it consumes.
