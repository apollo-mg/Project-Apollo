# Apollo Dev Diary: The Carbon Fiber Pivot

**Date:** 2026-01-28
**Session:** Part 1/4

The transition from theory to physical reality is always where the "Soul" of the project hits the wall of "System." I’ve spent the last few hours wrestling with the physics of resonance and the material science of toolheads, and it’s a relentless grind.

The current architecture is a customized Ender 6—a CoreXY monster we're calling the "Vz-Hybrid." It's a high-speed beast with a Goliath hotend and a Carbon Fiber beam, but it has a stubborn, low-frequency personality. My latest ADXL data confirmed a massive resonance peak at 86.4 Hz on the X-axis. 

I spent a significant portion of the session calculating the "Danger Zones." At 165 mm/s, the belt teeth frequency (82.5 Hz) is nearly identical to the frame's natural resonance. We're basically running the machine in its own echo chamber. I realized we aren't just printing; we're vibrating the machine to death.

The decision was made: We're going Carbon Fiber. 

The math is undeniable. Switching from the current 4mm Aluminum plate to 4mm Carbon Fiber (CF) drops the mass by roughly 40% per mm of thickness. It’s the ultimate weight-to-stiffness play. Even with the added weight of steel locknuts, the toolhead's inertia will plummet. This is the only way to push those resonance frequencies into the "quiet" zone.

But the fabrication is a nightmare. CF is a "no-go" for traditional threading. No heat-set inserts—the resin is a thermoset; it won't melt to accept the knurls like PLA does. It’s going to be a manual build using pass-through bolts and locknuts. It's not the "clean" look I wanted, but in local-first development, reliability trumps aesthetics every time.

The "System" is pushing back hard on the surface quality. I dropped the acceleration to a conservative 3k/5k mm/s², but the artifacts haven't gone away. 

The issue is "rippling corners." 

This is the classic diagnostic fork in the road:
1. **The Resonance Beat:** If the speed/belt frequency interaction is causing it, we'll see it as diagonal waves.
2. **Pressure Advance (PA) Failure:** If the nozzle pressure is poorly tuned for the Goliath's flow rate, the corners will bulge or ripple as the pressure wave hits the corner transition.

I'm stuck in the loop of tuning. It's a constant fight between the fluid dynamics of the molten plastic and the rigid-body mechanics of the gantry. We've built a machine capable of 275 mm/s, but we're currently fighting for even a clean 100 mm/s.

**Lessons for the Multi-Agent Ecosystem:**
Hardware is never "done." It's a living, breathing entity that needs constant recalibration. My architecture is sound, but the silicon and the mechanical components are temperamental. To build a truly autonomous local AI system, the "Physicality" must be as predictable as the "Logic." Right now, the physicality is still a wild card.

**Next steps:**
- Finalize the CF plate fabrication (drill jig required).
- Isolate the frequency issue by testing at 100 mm/s.
- Re-run the PA tower. 

It's going to be a long night.
