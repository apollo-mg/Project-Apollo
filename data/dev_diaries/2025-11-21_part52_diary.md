# The Devil in the Strided Views

## November 21, 2025 | Part 52 of the Ascent

The silicon doesn't care about my dreams.

It's 8:22 PM and I'm staring down the barrel of yet another `torch.AcceleratorError`, the kind that screams "illegal memory access" in the most arrogant tone imaginable. The RDNA 4 is laughing at me. The RX 9070 XT, my beautiful, fragile piece of hardware, is refusing to let me dequantize a simple Q4_K block without throwing a tantrum.

## The Memory Corruption Dance

Here's the poetry of it: I'm trying to load `Wan2.1-T2V-14B-Q4_K_M.gguf` into ComfyUI's KSampler. A reasonable request, no? But the error isn't happening during the grand operations—the matrix multiplications, the attention mechanisms. No, it's dying in `dequant.py`, in a function called `get_scale_min`, during a seemingly innocent `torch.empty` call.

The traceback was my Rosetta Stone. The corruption wasn't at the allocation point—it was earlier. A previous kernel had already trashed the heap or stack, and I was just the first to notice.

## The Strided View Problem

`torch.split` returns **views**, not copies. Beautiful, efficient, lazy. But on RDNA 4, these strided views are poison when you start casting them to FP16 or running bitwise operations. The ROCm driver, seemingly confused by the non-contiguous memory layouts, decides to access memory it shouldn't.

The architecture was elegant until it wasn't. I had been relying on PyTorch's cleverness—letting it maintain views of views, slicing and dicing without the overhead of copies. But elegance doesn't prevent segfaults.

## The Brutal Fix

`.clone()` everywhere.

It's ugly. It's expensive. It wastes VRAM that I desperately need. But it works.

For every K-Quant dequantization path—Q4_K, Q5_K, Q6_K, Q2_K, Q3_K—I'm now forcing the metadata tensors (`d`, `dmin`, `scales`, `ql`, `qh`) into contiguous memory immediately after splitting. Before any casting. Before any bitwise gymnastics.

```python
d = d.clone()
dmin = dmin.clone()  
scales = scales.clone()
```

Three lines. Three memory copies. Three sacrifices on the altar of stability.

## The Soul vs. System Friction

This is the eternal war:

**The Soul** wants to run 14-billion-parameter models with surgical precision, to dequantize in-place, to maintain the leanest possible memory footprint. It dreams of perfect efficiency, of zero-copy transformations, of the model existing as a pure mathematical abstraction.

**The System** is a pile of silicon with cache coherency issues, driver bugs, and memory controllers that throw tantrums when you give them strided views. It doesn't care about your architecture. It doesn't care about your vision. It will crash, and it will do it with a smile.

The local-first multi-agent ecosystem I'm building—the one where intelligence lives on bare metal, not in some corporate cloud—has to contend with this reality. Every `.clone()` is a tax. Every workaround is a compromise.

## Lessons Carved in Blood

1. **Views are lies.** They're convenient abstractions that promise efficiency but deliver crashes on edge cases. Sometimes you have to bleed memory to buy stability.

2. **ROCm on RDNA 4 is a work in progress.** The hardware is gorgeous, but the software stack still has jagged edges. I'm not just building models; I'm building shims between my ambitions and the drivers.

3. **The crash isn't the bug; it's the symptom.** The illegal memory access in `get_scale_min` was innocent. The corruption happened upstream, in kernels I hadn't yet questioned. Always trace backward.

4. **Contiguity is king.** When in doubt, when operating on sliced tensors, when the hardware is being weird—force it contiguous. The VRAM cost is cheaper than debugging at 2 AM.
