# Dev Diary: 2025-11-30 (Part 23) — The ROCm/Triton Breakthrough and the Shadow of the Ghost Process

## The Silicon Struggle: RDNA 4 vs. The World

Today was a battle of attrition against the peculiarities of RDNA architecture. My primary objective was to force-feed **Flash Attention** into the system to bypass the performance bottlenecks inherent in standard attention mechanisms on the RX 9070 XT. 

The path wasn't linear. I had to dive into the `ROCm/flash-attention` repository, hunting for the specific `v2.8.0-cktile` tag that supports the Triton backend. The build process was a nightmare of dependency isolation—`pip` kept trying to build in a sandbox that couldn't see my `torch` installation. I had to bypass the isolation with `--no-build-isolation`, a move that felt like performing surgery on a moving vehicle.

The breakthrough came when I realized the environment variable `FLASH_ATTENTION_TRITON_AMD_ENABLE="TRUE"` had to be explicitly injected into the runtime. Once that was locked in, the Triton backend finally recognized the RDNA 4 architecture. I saw it: `Device: cuda:0 AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT : native`. The "Soul" of the creative engine finally had a high-speed highway to the "System" of the silicon.

## The Ghost in the Machine: Port Conflicts and Zombie Processes

Just because the architecture is ready doesn't mean the software is sane. I hit a wall where the server refused to launch, throwing `OSError: [Errno 98] address already in use`. 

A ghost process—a remnant of a previous session—was still clinging to port 8188, refusing to die. I had to go on the offensive, hunting down PIDs and aggressively terminating them. It’s a recurring theme: the friction between a "clean" restart and the reality of a persistent, unyielding operating system.

## The Dependency Spiral: The `accelerate` Paradox

The most frustrating part of the day was the "Missing Node" crisis. Even after getting the core engine running, the `ComfyUI-MultiGPU` (DisTorch) nodes were failing to load. 

The error was a classic `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'accelerate'`. But here’s the kicker: my diagnostic scripts proved `accelerate` *was* installed in the `venv`. 

This is where the architecture gets weird. I realized I had a directory collision. There were two versions of the same node attempting to load: `ComfyUI-MultiGPU` and `comfyui-multigpu`. The import system was choking on the naming discrepancy and the conflicting paths. It turns out, the "System" wasn't just being difficult; it was being confused by its own file structure.

## Core Lessons for the Local-First Ecosystem

1.  **The Environment is Everything:** In a local-first, bare-metal setup, the `venv` is not just a container; it is the entire reality. If a single dependency like `torchaudio` or `accelerate` isn't perfectly aligned with the ROCm version, the entire multi-agent ecosystem collapses.
2.  **The Shadow of Duplication:** In a world of rapid-fire custom node installation, directory naming collisions are the silent killers of stability. A single duplicate folder can break the entire import chain.
3.  **The Triton Advantage:** We have successfully validated that RDNA 4 can handle Flash Attention via Triton. This changes the game for local-first high-resolution generation. We aren't just "getting by" on AMD; we are actually competing.
