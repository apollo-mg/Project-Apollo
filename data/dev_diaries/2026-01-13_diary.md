# Dev Diary: 2026-01-13

## The Aluminum Paradigm

Today was about reclaiming the physics of the machine. For weeks, the Y-axis has been the bottleneck—a low-frequency, wobbling mess that kept my effective acceleration capped at a pathetic 9,000 mm/s². The "Soul" of the project (the dream of high-speed, high-fidelity local-first manufacturing) was being strangled by the "System" (the physical limitations of the gantry rigidity).

I finally installed the custom-fabricated aluminum top plate. It’s heavy, it’s dense, and it’s exactly what the CoreXY architecture needs to fight the inertia of the toolhead.

The results from the resonance testing were nothing short of a revelation. 

When I ran the calibration, the frequency shift was staggering. The Y-axis raw resonance jumped from 72 Hz to 95 Hz. That's a massive increase in structural stiffness. The machine is no longer a floppy mess; it's a rigid, high-frequency beast.

But the data revealed a new, more complex ghost in the machine.

The Y-axis graph showed a violent Z-axis coupling. As the Y-axis moves, the toolhead is "nodding"—literally rocking on the rail. It's a mechanical torsion issue. The sheer mass of the EVA/Goliath combo is creating torque that the X-extrusion can't handle. It’s a beautiful, technical friction: I’ve built a frame so stiff that the vibration is now migrating into the Z-axis, turning a 2D movement into a 3D oscillation.

I had to dive into the math of the shaper selection. The `2HUMP_EI` algorithm is doing the heavy lifting here. While it’s not as surgically precise as `MZV`, it’s handling the complex, multi-frequency resonance caused by the gantry twist. It’s sacrificing a bit of theoretical peak performance to "smash" the resonance peaks into submission.

The machine is now hitting ~136 Hz on the X-axis. We are officially in "Speedboat" territory. 

**The Technical Reality:**
- **Rigidity:** Massive improvement. The aluminum plate killed the old low-frequency modes.
- **Acceleration:** We’ve effectively doubled the ceiling. The testing suggests 19,600 mm/s² is the limit before the toolhead-induced "nodding" becomes uncontrollable.
- **The Next Wall:** The linear rail carriage. The Z-axis vibration during Y-sweeps is the next bottleneck. I'll likely need to swap to a higher preload carriage to stabilize the X-extrusion against the Y-axis torque.

**The Lesson:**
In a local-first ecosystem, the hardware isn't just a vessel; it's a variable. You can't just "code" your way to speed if the silicon (or in this case, the aluminum) can't handle the frequency. The architecture of the machine must evolve at the same rate as the logic of the agents controlling it.

**Current Status:**
- `printer.cfg` updated with `2HUMP_EI` for both axes.
- Acceleration limit pushed to 15,000 mm/s² for initial test prints.
- Hardware feels like a tank. Now, let's see if the prints look like it.
