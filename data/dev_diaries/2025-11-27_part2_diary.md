# Dev Diary: 2025-11-27 (Part 2)
## The Ghost in the Silicon: F5-TTS and the ROCm Struggle

The "Soul" of Project Apollo—the ability to mimic human expression—is currently trapped in a recursive loop of directory-naming tantrums and memory-access hallucinations. Today was supposed to be the day I achieved dual-voice sovereignty. Instead, I spent most of the session fighting a folder structure that seems to have its own set of contradictory laws.

### The Architecture of Chaos
The transition from GPT-SoVITS to F5-TTS felt smoother than it actually was. While the training for the GPT component finished with blistering speed (10.18 it/s, a rare moment where the hardware felt like it was actually working *with* me), the F5-TTS workflow is a manual, finicky beast. 

The first major friction point: **The Directory Paradox.** The F5-TTS WebUI is a fickle master. It expects a `dataset/` folder, but the transcription process insists on a `wavs/` folder. I spent a significant amount of time performing "surgical strikes" on the file system—renaming, moving, and symlinking—just to satisfy a script that couldn't decide where its own metadata lived. 

### The VRAM Wall and the "Death of the Universe"
Then came the UI settings. The "Auto-Settings" feature in the F5-TTS WebUI is a menace. It suggested an epoch count of `137,551`. I am an architect, not a god; I cannot wait until the heat death of the universe for a voice model to converge. 

We hit a critical bottleneck regarding the root partition. With only 3.5GB free, a 5GB model download would have crashed the entire system. I had to implement a quick-fix migration, moving the Hugging Face cache and the checkpoint directories to the 2TB drive via symlinks. It’s a messy, "hacky" solution, but in a local-first, bare-metal environment, these are the necessary scars of progress.

### The HIP Error: A Lesson in Hardware Limits
The real battle began when the training actually started. 

The user, following the "Auto-Settings," pushed the Batch Size per GPU to `5592`. The result? A catastrophic `torch.AcceleratorError: HIP error: an illegal memory access was encountered`. 

This is the classic friction between the **System** (the AMD RDNA 4 architecture) and the **Software** (the PyTorch/ROCm implementation). The error occurred deep within the `layer_norm` kernel. It’s the silicon’s way of saying: *"You're asking me to hold too much in my hands, and I'm dropping everything."*

It wasn't just an Out-of-Memory (OOM) error; it was a kernel-level instability. The asynchronous nature of HIP means the crash happened at a different time than the actual fault, making debugging a nightmare. 

**The Lesson:** On RDNA 4, batch sizes are not negotiable. The "optimal" numbers suggested by academic papers often ignore the specific quirks of the ROCm driver stack. We are moving to a more conservative `2000` frames per batch. It’s slower, but it’s stable.

### Core Lessons for the Multi-Agent Ecosystem
1.  **Local-First is Fragile:** Running heavy-duty training on a single-user workstation requires constant, manual intervention in the file system. Automation is great, but it can't account for the "directory-naming madness" of experimental research code.
2.  **Hardware-Aware Quantization:** We cannot rely on `bitsandbytes` (8-bit Adam) blindly. While it's the standard for NVIDIA, the ROCm implementation is still a wild frontier. We have to stick to standard 32-bit Adam and manage our VRAM through manual batch-size throttling.
3.  **The Data-Structure Gap:** There is a massive gap between how researchers structure data and how production-ready engines expect it. The "Prepare" step in F5-TTS is a bottleneck that requires precise file-path alignment.

**Status:** F5-TTS is currently attempting a low-batch-size run. If the loss curve stabilizes, the era of dual-voice capability begins.

**Next Move:** Monitor the `layer_norm` stability and check the `tensorboard` logs. If the ROCm kernel holds, we have a winner.
