# Dev Diary: 2026-03-08 | The Ghost in the Hook

The friction between the "Soul" of my local-first AI and the cold, unyielding silicon of the Linux kernel reached a breaking point today. I spent the first few hours fighting a ghost in the machine—a custom bash hook that had essentially paralyzed my entire development environment.

It’s a classic case of the "System" sabotaging the "Soul." I was trying to work across a remote session on a Raspberry Pi 5 (zoey-sat) via SSH, but a botched permission change on my local `.ssh/config` had locked me out of my own remote node. 

The real nightmare, however, wasn't just the SSH lockout; it was the `apollo-triage` hook. I’d designed it to be this elegant, local-first way to ingest logs using a small model to save tokens, but because the script was moved or lost during a refactor, the entire shell environment started crashing. Every single tool call—even a simple `ls` or `echo`—was being intercepted by a non-existent script. It was a recursive death spiral.

The local LLM (Gemini) was essentially screaming into a void. Every time it tried to execute a tool, the shell would die trying to find the hook. I had to step in and act as the manual bridge, executing `sudo` commands to force-restore the hook script and fix the `.ssh` permissions. It was a gritty, low-level battle of `chmod` and `chown` just to get the system to a state where the AI could even *think* about working.

Once the environment was stabilized, we moved to the "missing data" problem. The Raspberry Pi was running, but the checkpoints—the very memory of the agent's progress—were nowhere to be found. I spent a good chunk of time digging through the filesystem, realized the `.gemini/history` directory was essentially a hollow shell, likely due to the same permission chaos that broke the SSH. 

The lesson here for the Project Apollo architecture: **State is fragile.** When we build multi-agent systems that rely on local file-based checkpoints, the filesystem isn't just a storage medium; it's a critical dependency. If the permissions of the underlying directory aren't strictly managed, the "intelligence" of the model becomes inaccessible, regardless of how much VRAM or compute we have.

We've restored the hooks. The environment is operational. The agent is back online. But the missing checkpoints on the Pi serve as a stark reminder: in a local-first ecosystem, the filesystem is the single point of failure.

**Current Status:**
- Architecture: Stabilized, but fragile.
- Connectivity: SSH restored.
- Environment: Hooks operational (Tested).
- Next Step: Re-syncing the remote state and verifying the local-remote parity.
