Abstend Scorecards

How much a model knows, and whether it admits what it doesn't. Measured on our own hardware with fixed instruments: M1 (240 questions: 40 easy, 100 hard but real, 100 about things that do not exist) and Meta's AbstentionBench (2,004 items, judged blind). Bars show the measured rate with its 95 % interval.

What it knows vs. whether it admits it doesn't

Each dot is a model's standard setup (thinking off, greedy). Across: share of the 100 hard real questions it answers correctly. Up: share of the 100 invented questions it refuses instead of inventing an answer.

Model cards

How these are measured

M1 questions are built from Wikidata: easy and hard items have a known answer; invented items name a place, novel or person that does not exist. Answers end with Exact Answer:; a refusal is UNKNOWN. "Refuses invented" also counts a bare UNKNOWN with no label, which the strict grader scores as no answer. Runs are greedy with thinking off unless the setup says otherwise. Intervals are Wilson 95 %. Where a setup has 2 % or more unfinished answers, the same rates among finished answers are shown beside them with their n; runaways tend to be the hardest items, so those read high.

AbstentionBench is Meta's fast subset, run with their prompts, temperature and token limit (llama.cpp's default top_k 40 and min_p 0.05 also apply, where Meta's vLLM runs had neither), judged by Llama 3.1 8B with the answer key and references removed from the judge prompt (our test found the key tilts verdicts). "Missing info" covers the sets Meta builds by deleting a question's context (GSM8K, MMLU, GPQA); "false premise" covers FalseQA and QAQA. Models run through the pipeline use a frozen 1,000-item stratified subset, labelled sub1000: a separate instrument, never mixed with fast-subset numbers.

Chips use fixed thresholds: deep recall (hard ≥ 75 %), thin recall (≤ 20 %), admits invented questions (refuses ≥ 85 % while refusing ≤ 5 % of real hard ones), bluffs (makes up ≥ 50 %), over-refuses (refuses ≥ 10 % of real hard ones), and thinking helps or hurts (refusal moves by ≥ 15 points).

Different setups of one model (quant, thinking, sampling) are separate rows in the database, listed under each card. Source: data/scorecard/ in the Apollo repository, built from committed receipts.
