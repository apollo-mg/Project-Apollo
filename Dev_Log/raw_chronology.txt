### The Two Paths of Vision-to-3D

#### 1. The "Photogrammetry / Gaussian Splatting" Path (Organic 3D)
This is what most people think of when they say "scanning with a phone." You take 50+ photos of an object, and the AI (like NeRFs or 3D Gaussian Splatting) builds a point cloud or mesh.
*   **The Reality:** This is fantastic for capturing a statue or a miniature for a video game. However, it is **terrible for engineering.** The resulting meshes are usually "dirty" (millions of unstructured triangles), lack parametric history, and are incredibly difficult to modify or 3D print accurately if you need specific tolerances for screws or interlocking parts.
*   **Hardware Fit:** Your RX 9070 XT could absolutely crush local Gaussian Splatting training, but the end result wouldn't be a CAD file you can edit in OnShape.

#### 2. The "Vision-to-Parametric" Path (Engineered 3D)
This is the holy grail of physical-to-digital AI for makers. Instead of building a dirty mesh, the AI looks at a part and reverse-engineers the *intent* into clean CAD code.
*   **The Concept:** You place a broken bracket on your desk next to a ruler or calipers. Qwen2.5-VL analyzes the image and extracts the primitives: *"This is a 40x40x5mm plate with four 3mm holes in the corners and a central 10mm cylinder."*

*   **The Value:** This teaches DesignMind to think in geometry and constraints, not just pixels. 

### Why the "Vision-to-Parametric" Hybrid is the Best of Both Worlds
If your ultimate goal is making functional parts for your 3D printer or your RX 580 swarm, you need clean, parametric geometry.

### The Two Paths of Vision-to-3D

#### 1. The "Photogrammetry / Gaussian Splatting" Path (Organic 3D)
This is what most people think of when they say "scanning with a phone." You take 50+ photos of an object, and the AI (like NeRFs or 3D Gaussian Splatting) builds a point cloud or mesh.
*   **The Reality:** This is fantastic for capturing a statue or a miniature for a video game. *   **Hardware Fit:** Your RX 9070 XT could absolutely crush local Gaussian Splatting training, but the end result wouldn't be a CAD file you can edit in OnShape.

#### 2. The "Vision-to-Parametric" Path (Engineered 3D)
This is the holy grail of physical-to-digital AI for makers. Instead of building a dirty mesh, the AI looks at a part and reverse-engineers the *intent* into clean CAD code.
*   **The Concept:** You place a broken bracket on your desk next to a ruler or calipers. Qwen2.5-VL analyzes the image and extracts the primitives: *"This is a 40x40x5mm plate with four 3mm holes in the corners and a central 10mm cylinder."*

*   **The Value:** This teaches DesignMind to think in geometry and constraints, not just pixels. 

### Why the "Vision-to-Parametric" Hybrid is the Best of Both Worlds
If your ultimate goal is making functional parts for your 3D printer or your RX 580 swarm, you need clean, parametric geometry.

1.  **The Input:** You take a picture of a part with your webcam (or drop an image from your phone into Discord). The image must include a reference scale (a ruler, or a known object like a coin).

When Ollama or `llama.cpp` loads a massive model that doesn't fit entirely in your VRAM or RAM (like that 80B Qwen model we tried), it uses a technique called `mmap` (Memory Mapped Files).
